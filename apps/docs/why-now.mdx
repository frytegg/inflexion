Inflexion could not have been built — as a *priced, fully collateralized,
non-custodial* market — three years ago. Two things had to become true at once: the
**theory** of in-range impermanent loss had to mature from actuarial guesswork into
something with a model-light fair value and a concrete hedge, and the **infrastructure**
had to be able to evaluate the resulting closed form on-chain, live, per quote, at a
precision that does not leak.

Both happened. This page is the timing argument.

<Note>
**The single distinction this whole page turns on:** the two academic results below are
**theory anchors** for *why* in-range IL is priceable and hedgeable. They are **not** the
pricer. The pricer is an exact closed-form [Φ-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum) computed
on-chain by the Stylus `FairValueOracle`. Do not conflate the validation with the
machinery.
</Note>

## The two theory anchors

The case for a *priced, collateralized* IL market only became rigorous recently. Two
results — one on the **cost** of the LP's exposure, one on the **hedge** for it — turn
"LPs lose a lot to IL" into "this specific risk has a model-light fair value and a
concrete replication." They are the reason the approach is sound; they are not how the
protocol computes a number.

### Anchor 1 — the hedge exists: static replication (Lipton–Lucic–Sepp, 2025)

The structural problem with IL has always been that it *felt* like an exotic, path-heavy
payoff that you could only reserve against actuarially — guess a distribution, charge a
fat uncertainty premium, hope. Lipton, Lucic & Sepp showed that an IL-protection claim is
**statically replicable by a strip of vanilla options**. Static replication means the hedge is a *fixed* portfolio
set at inception — you do not rebalance into the storm.

Two consequences fall straight out, and both are load-bearing for Inflexion:

- **It has a model-light fair value.** If the payoff is a combination of vanillas, its
  fair value is the same combination of vanilla prices. That is what makes a *closed-form*
  premium even conceivable.
- **It has a concrete hedge.** A market maker who underwrites the claim is not stuck
  holding naked short gamma; they can **export the risk** to the global options market.
  This is precisely what [Path B](/protocol/path-b-mm) market makers do — and why a hedged
  MM makes the *whole system's* risk smaller rather than just relocating it.

### Anchor 2 — the cost is closed-form: LVR (Milionis–Moallemi–Roughgarden, 2022)

The complementary result quantifies what the LP is *paying for* being in the pool.
Milionis, Moallemi & Roughgarden showed that the AMM's adverse-selection cost —
**loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR)** — has a **closed form proportional to instantaneous
variance**, equivalently the **theta (time-decay) of the replicating short-option
position**.

This is the precise framing of the problem Inflexion solves. A Uniswap v3 LP holds a
position whose value `V_lp(P)` is **strictly concave in price** in range — the LP is
structurally **short gamma**, and IL is the *realization* of that short-gamma / LVR cost.
The concavity is not hand-waving; it is the
second derivative of the in-range value function:

$$
V_{lp}(P) = L\left(2\sqrt{P} - \sqrt{P_a} - \tfrac{P}{\sqrt{P_b}}\right),
\qquad
\frac{d^2 V_{lp}}{dP^2} = -\tfrac{1}{4}\,L\,P^{-3/2} < 0 .
$$

A concave value function is a short-option payoff. The LP pays LVR (theta) for it. **What
Inflexion sells is the long-convexity leg that offsets it** — capped at the in-range
maximum, [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) — priced as the exact risk-neutral expectation of that
capped payoff. The two anchors meet here: LVR says *short gamma has a closed-form cost*;
static replication says *the offsetting claim has a model-light fair value and a hedge*.
Inflexion is the market that connects them.

<Tip>
Read these two anchors as one sentence: a v3 LP is short gamma with a **closed-form cost**
(LVR), and the **hedge for it is a strip of vanillas** (static replication). That is the
entire theoretical license to build a priced, collateralized IL market. The
[theory-anchors page](/math/theory-anchors) carries the full derivation chain.
</Tip>

## Why the anchors are NOT the pricer

This is a seam worth stating plainly.

The anchors say the claim *can* be priced and hedged. They do **not** give Inflexion the
number it puts on-chain. Inflexion does not reimplement a strip-of-vanillas hedge to back
into a price, and it does not run Monte Carlo or look up a calibrated surface. It computes
the **exact** risk-neutral expectation of the *actual* capped v3 payoff in closed form.

The capped payoff `min(IL, MaxIL)` is **piecewise in the terminal price** — a constant
arm, a linear-in-`P` arm, and a `√P` arm, split by the range edges and the two
cap-crossing prices. Each arm integrated against the lognormal density of `P_T` is a
standard interval moment in the normal CDF `Φ`. So the fair premium is a finite **Φ-sum**
(≈6–10 terms, Black–Scholes class):

$$
\text{FairPremium} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[\min(IL,\,\text{MaxIL})\right]
= \text{fairRate} \cdot \text{MaxIL},
\qquad
\text{fairRate} = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\min(IL,\text{MaxIL})]}{\text{MaxIL}} .
$$

No fitted coefficients, no lookup table, no Monte Carlo — **evaluated live per quote**,
with `σ_ref` as the only stochastic input. The full arm-by-arm construction is on
[the Φ-sum page](/math/fair-value-phi-sum).

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation: the only residual approximation.** The Φ-sum is exact *given*
the model. **Limit:** the one modeling assumption is risk-neutral GBM with `μ = 0, r = 0`.
**Why:** no on-chain formula can remove the GBM
assumption — that would require a forward-vol view the chain cannot see. **Mitigation:**
it is covered two ways — a deliberately conservative [`σ_ref`](/math/sigma-ref) (a
`max(σ_short, σ_long, floor)` with the floor set *above* calm ETH vol), and the residual
forward-vol premium is left as **MM alpha**, the very incentive that keeps
forward-looking-vol market makers in the two-sided market.
</Warning>

So the lineage is: **anchors validate → Φ-sum prices.** The anchors establish that the
approach is theoretically clean. The Φ-sum is what actually clears a trade.

## The infrastructure enablers

Theory is necessary but not sufficient. A model-light fair value is worthless if you
cannot evaluate it **on-chain, live, and precisely enough that the price does not leak.**
The second half of "why now" is that the chain finally can.

### Arbitrum One — the liquidity is here

Arbitrum One has the **deepest Uniswap v3 liquidity** of any rollup. The
product hedges a *specific* v3 position's in-range IL, so it has to live where the
positions are. Building on the chain with the largest population of in-range v3 LPs is the
difference between a demo and a market.

### Chainlink — the settlement and volatility input

[Settlement](/protocol/lifecycle) reads the Chainlink round pinned at expiry `T` and pays
`min(realized_IL, MaxIL)` from pre-locked collateral; the realized-volatility oracle
([`σ_ref`](/math/sigma-ref)) is built from EWMAs of Chainlink log-returns at two horizons.
Both depend on Chainlink being **fully deployed and live** on Arbitrum. The
[oracles page](/protocol/oracles) covers the staleness, sequencer-uptime, and lone-spike
defenses.

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation: the testnet has no Sequencer Uptime Feed.** **Limit:** Arbitrum
*Sepolia* — where Inflexion is live today — has **no Chainlink L2 Sequencer Uptime Feed**.
**Why:** it is a testnet with no SLA; the feed simply is not deployed there. **Mitigation:**
`OracleManager` skips the sequencer check on testnet *by design*, and this check is gated to
be re-enabled before any mainnet deploy. It
is a known, parameterized switch, not an oversight. See [oracles](/protocol/oracles).
</Warning>

### Stylus — the exact Φ-sum, on-chain

This is the enabler that turns the theory into a *trustless* product rather than an
off-chain quote you have to believe. The Φ-sum is compute-heavy — it leans on the error
function — and Solidity's Abramowitz–Stegun `erf` is only accurate to ~`1.5e-7`, which when
divided through by MaxIL amplifies to ~`1e-3` at the tight tier. That is enough error to be
*picked off* on a price you publish.

Arbitrum **Stylus** lets the protocol write the oracle in Rust/WASM with integer
fixed-point arithmetic — and because Stylus **bans WASM floating point at activation**,
the result is deterministic and machine-precise. The Stylus
`FairValueOracle` matches a 50-digit `mpmath` reference to **≤ `1e-12` on fairRate** (in
practice a few wei at 1e18 scale). That precision is *why the
on-chain published price can be trusted as the fair value* rather than a loose estimate.

<Info>
**A precise nuance worth owning:** Stylus is production **for the compute-heavy
`FairValueOracle`** — not everywhere. The tiny `ILMath` kernel was *benchmarked* in Stylus
and measured ~5.3× *more* expensive cached, because there is too little compute to amortize
Stylus's per-call overhead. So **Solidity `ILMath` is production; the Stylus `ILMath` port
is a rejected benchmark.** "Stylus is faster" is not a blanket claim — it is true exactly
where the compute is heavy.
</Info>

### A maturing options surface — the risk has somewhere to go

The final enabler is external to the chain. Static replication only matters if the
replicating vanillas *trade*. The off-chain options market (Deribit, which holds >90% of
ETH options) and on-chain options venues (Panoptic) now exist as real venues where a
[Path-B market maker](/protocol/path-b-mm) can **export** the short-gamma risk it
underwrites.

This makes [Pillar 3](/concepts/three-pillars)'s risk-export argument concrete rather than
aspirational: without a place to hedge, a closed pool of IL writers is just **ETH short
gamma circulating against itself**; with a maturing options surface, hedged MMs make the
system's *net* risk smaller. The theory anchor (static replication) and the infra enabler
(a venue to replicate in) are two ends of the same hedge.

Each beat was missing as recently as a few years ago. Together they make a *priced, fully
collateralized, non-custodial* in-range IL market not just possible but the obvious next
primitive — which is the whole [why-inflexion](/why-inflexion) thesis.

## What "now" does NOT mean

Honest scope, because the timing argument is easy to over-sell.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="It does not mean the math is novel.">
    The IL formula, the convexity proof, and the lognormal interval moments are textbook
    once you have the anchors. Inflexion's contribution is the **synthesis**: pricing the
    *exact capped v3 payoff* in closed form and publishing it on-chain at machine
    precision, with a [two-rail](/concepts/two-rails) market around it. The novelty is the
    product and the dataset, not a new theorem.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="It does not mean the no-bad-debt guarantee leans on the timing.">
    The [no-bad-debt](/concepts/no-bad-debt) guarantee is **structural and
    oracle-independent**: in FULL mode `collateral = MaxIL ≥ payout` by construction,
    regardless of what `σ` was or what the anchors say. The theory anchors and `σ_ref` are
    load-bearing for *pricing* and *depositor* solvency, never for the FULL no-bad-debt
    invariant. The two are separate claims — see the disclosures below.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="It does not mean the data moat is mature on day one.">
    The timing makes the *architecture* of the [data moat](/concepts/data-moat) possible
    now; the **dynamics** (the pool-vs-MM forward-vol spread) mature with multiple
    competing market makers and real flow. The structures ship from day one; the dataset
    begins at the redeploy that ships the moat events. See the maturity disclosure below.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
