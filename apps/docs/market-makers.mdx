## Overview

Inflexion lets a Uniswap v3 LP pay a fixed upfront premium to transfer the
**in-range impermanent-loss risk** of a specific position to an underwriter, who
posts collateral and is paid for taking the risk. At expiry the LP is paid their
realized IL — **capped at `MaxIL`** — trustlessly, from the underwriter's
collateral.

There are **two underwriters an LP can be matched against**, and every swap is
priced off the same on-chain published fair value, the **`FairPremium`**:

| Path               | Underwriter                     | Pricing                        | Infrastructure             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| **Path A** (cvAMM) | The pooled `ConvexityVault`     | `FairPremium · (1 + poolLoad)` | None — always on, on-chain |
| **Path B** (MM)    | A market maker's own collateral | `FairPremium · (1 + loadBps)`  | An off-chain quote relayer |

When an LP opens a swap, the protocol **prices both rails and executes the
cheaper one**. A market maker is therefore not a dependency of the system — it is
a **competitor to the pool**. This page is about Path B.

## Why market making is permissionless and optional

The cvAMM pool (Path A) is the **always-on floor of liquidity**: signature-free,
keeper-free, and fully on-chain. The protocol is complete, solvent, and usable
with Path A alone — no off-chain infrastructure is required for an LP to get
priced and protected.

A market maker is **optional** and additive:

- **Nothing about correctness, solvency, or liveness depends on it.** It is a
  price-improvement layer, not a load-bearing component.
- **An MM only ever wins a swap when it _strictly_ beats the capped pool price.**
  If the MM's quote is absent, stale, or simply not cheaper, the swap silently
  routes to the pool.
- **It is permissionless.** Any address that posts collateral can quote; there is
  no whitelist and no "last look" (see below).

In short: the market maker is how a sophisticated underwriter undercuts the pool
and captures flow it can price better — without ever being able to degrade the
guarantee an LP already has from the pool.

## How an MM quote works

An MM does **not** sign an absolute price. It signs a **load** — `loadBps`, a
spread in basis points over the protocol's own published `FairPremium`:

```
premium = FairPremium · (1 + loadBps / 10_000)
```

The `FairPremium` is recomputed on-chain at execution time from the LP's exact
position geometry and the live volatility oracle, so the MM is always quoting a
spread over a fair value it does not control. This is what makes a short-lived,
signature-only quote safe to publish.

Each quote is an **EIP-712 signed message** (`SignedQuote`) carrying, among other
fields:

- `loadBps` — the spread over `FairPremium` (capped at the protocol's `maxLoadBps`;
  a quote above the cap is simply ignored by the router).
- `quotePrice` + `priceBandBps` — the oracle price the MM signed against and the
  band it is willing to honor (see [Quote lifetime](#quote-lifetime)).
- `validUntil` — the absolute expiry timestamp of the quote.
- `quoteId` + `maxNotionalV0` — a capacity key: one quote can fill multiple swaps
  up to a notional cap.
- `nonce` — a Permit2-style bitmap nonce for cancellation.

Signatures are verified on-chain for both EOAs and contract signers (EIP-1271), so
a vault or a smart-account market maker is a first-class participant.

### Firm quotes, no last look

Inflexion quotes are **firm**: once an LP presents a valid signed quote, the MM
**cannot** renege after seeing the order. There is no "last look." Firmness is
what makes the quote trustworthy to the taker — but a firm quote that lives too
long is a free option for the taker, who could hold it and exercise only when the
market has moved in their favor (a pickoff). Inflexion closes that with a **short
lifetime plus an oracle band**, not with last look.

## Quote lifetime

Quotes are **Good-Til-Date (GTD)**, never Good-Til-Cancelled. Every quote must
carry a `validUntil`, and the protocol bounds it to a **5–15 second** window
(default ≈ 8s). A quote with no expiry, or one dated too far out, is rejected.

On top of the time fuse, a quote is **auto-voided the instant the live oracle
price drifts beyond `priceBandBps`** from the `quotePrice` the MM signed against.
So a quote is firm:

1. for at most a few seconds, **and**
2. only while the market has not materially moved.

This short-GTD-plus-band design is the protocol's defense against stale-quote
pickoff, and it is why a market maker streams fresh quotes continuously rather
than resting a long-lived order.

## Posting and cancelling quotes

**Posting is instant and free.** A market maker signs quotes off-chain — an
EIP-712 signature, no gas and no transaction — and streams them to the relayer. It
can re-quote every block or faster; updating its price is just emitting a new
signature.

**Cancelling has two layers:**

- **Passive (the normal path).** Quotes self-expire in 5–15 seconds and
  auto-void on any band-breaking price move. A fast MM "cancels" simply by not
  refreshing — and stale quotes cannot be picked off because the band check kills
  them. No gas, no action.
- **Active, on-chain.** `cancelNonces(uint256[])` burns Permit2-style bitmap nonce
  bits in a single transaction to hard-revoke a still-valid quote before its
  `validUntil`. The bitmap encoding makes mass-cancellation cheap (many quote ids
  per transaction). This is the only on-chain action a market maker needs to take
  to manage live quotes.

The result is a tight off-chain quote/re-quote loop (free), with the time fuse and
oracle band handling almost all "cancellation," and `cancelNonces` reserved for
yanking a live quote immediately.

## Routing: the MM wins only by beating the pool

When an LP opens a swap through the router, the protocol:

1. Computes the **pool** premium `FairPremium · (1 + poolLoad)` (Path A).
2. If a usable MM quote is supplied, computes the **MM** premium
   `FairPremium · (1 + loadBps)` (Path B).
3. Executes whichever is **strictly cheaper** — ties and missing/invalid quotes go
   to the pool.

An MM quote is treated as _unusable_ (and the swap falls back to the pool, without
reverting) if it is absent, expired, signed by an under-collateralized MM, outside
the oracle band, over the load cap, or otherwise malformed. Only the **executed**
path consumes the MM's nonce and capacity — pricing a quote that then loses leaves
it fully spendable.

## Collateral and settlement

A Path-B market maker underwrites with **its own collateral**, posted in the
`UnderwriterVault`. When its quote wins a swap, the protocol locks exactly `MaxIL`
of that collateral for the life of the swap. At expiry, the LP's realized IL —
capped at `MaxIL` — is paid out **from the MM's locked collateral**, and the
remainder is released back to the MM.

Because the payout is capped at the collateral that was locked, a Path-B swap
**cannot produce bad debt** under the protocol's stated assumptions (capped
payoff, solvent collateral asset, oracle/settlement liveness) — exactly the same
guarantee the pool provides on Path A.
