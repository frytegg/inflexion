The `/earn` page is where a **depositor becomes an underwriter**. You supply `dUSDC` to the pooled
**cvAMM vault** (the `ConvexityVault` — Path A, the always-on, signature-free underwriter), and in
exchange you earn the premium that protected LPs pay to transfer their **in-range** impermanent-loss
risk. You are taking the other side of an [in-range convexity hedge](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge):
LPs are long the convexity, the pool is short it, and you are paid the volatility-risk premium for
holding it.

This is **claim B** — and claim B is the opposite of a yield promise. Read the panel below first; it is
not a footnote.

<ClaimB />

<Note>
Claim A (LPs are paid in FULL mode — no bad debt, qualified) and claim B (depositor capital is not
guaranteed) are **separate claims and are never merged**. The no-bad-debt guarantee is to the *protected
LP*, not to you. See [no bad debt](/concepts/no-bad-debt) for claim A in full, and
[the tranches](/concepts/tranches) for the waterfall that governs your downside.
</Note>

**SDK:** `DepositorClient` (`depositor.*`) over the live `ConvexityVault`. Defaults:
`vault = core.convexityVault`, `usdc = demoUsdc`, `sigmaToken = demoWeth`.

## Why this panel sits above everything

The very first thing rendered on `/earn` — above the vault overview, above the deposit box — is the
**"Capital is NOT guaranteed"** panel. It is deliberately placed first, and it shows by default while
vault state is still loading. The page does not let you read APY-shaped numbers before you read the risk.

The panel spells out, verbatim, four things you must internalise before depositing:

<Steps>
<Step title="You are underwriting in-range IL">
Your capital backs the protocol's promise to pay LPs `min(realized_IL, MaxIL)` while their position is
in range. You are short the convexity you are being paid for.
</Step>
<Step title="Junior is first-loss">
The junior tranche absorbs underwriting losses **before** the senior tranche is ever touched. If you
deposit junior, you are the first-loss buffer.
</Step>
<Step title="Senior is protected from underwriting loss only">
Senior is shielded by the on-chain junior-first waterfall — `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` — from
*underwriting* loss. It is **not** protected from the systemic tail (USDC depeg, oracle/settlement
failure, contract bug).
</Step>
<Step title="No-bad-debt is for the LP, not for you">
The `/earn` disclosure is explicit: the no-bad-debt guarantee *"is for the protected LP, not for you"*.
Do not read claim A as cover for claim B.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation, in one place.**

- **Limit:** depositor capital can be lost. Neither tranche is risk-free.
- **Why:** you are the underwriter. The protocol pays LPs *from your collateral* at settlement; if
  realized IL across the book exceeds the premiums collected, the pool takes the loss — junior first,
  then senior once the buffer is gone, then the systemic tail hits everyone.
- **Mitigation:** the protocol is **FULL-collateralized at MaxIL per swap** (every open swap is backed
  exactly to its cap), the payoff is **capped** (`min(realized_IL, MaxIL)`), the load stack prices the
  risk above the on-chain fair value, and the junior-first waterfall structurally subordinates junior to
  senior. These bound the *shape* of the loss; they do not eliminate it. Pick your tranche accordingly,
  and read [the tranches](/concepts/tranches) before sizing.
</Warning>

## Vault overview (pool-wide)

The overview reads `depositor.getVaultState()`, refetched every 20 seconds. It returns a
`Degraded<VaultState>` envelope — if the RPC is down it degrades to `no-rpc`; if the vol oracle is
uninitialised it degrades to `vol-uninitialized`. It never throws.

What it shows:

| Metric | Meaning | Colour cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Total assets** | All `dUSDC` underwriting capital in the vault (senior + junior). | — |
| **Locked** | Collateral backing open swaps — `Σ MaxIL` of active protections. | amber |
| **Free** | Open capacity, pool-wide fungible — what can still back new fills. | teal |
| **Utilization** | `locked / total` — how much of the pool is committed. | — |
| **Regime** | `calm` / `normal` / `stressed`, from σ_ref vs the load bands. | badge |

Below the headline, the **load stack** is decomposed: **σ_ref** (the EWMA
reference vol), **Concentration** (the HHI, colour-coded), **Base load** (the regime floor), and
**Total load** — the *price-to-beat*, clamped to `maxLoadBps` (invariant I10) and shown in teal. This is
the same load stack a Path-B MM must undercut to win a fill, surfaced here so depositors can see the
premium their capital earns. See [the load stack](/math/load-stack) for the full derivation.

<Info>
**Free collateral is pool-wide fungible.** `VaultState.lockedByMarket` is a *concentration colour map*
keyed by caller-supplied marketIds — it is HHI / concentration colour, **not** a per-market budget.
Your deposited capital can back any market's fills, which is exactly why
[MaxIL is the unit of risk](/concepts/maxil) that makes positions fungible to the pool.
</Info>

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation: the load stack can read as "pending".**

- **Limit:** the load-stack row can render a pending note instead of numbers.
- **Why:** the total load depends on σ_ref from the `VolOracle`; if the oracle is uninitialised or the
  RPC is degraded, `getVaultState` returns `available: false` with reason `vol-uninitialized`.
- **Mitigation:** the page renders an honest pending note, not an error, and recovers automatically on
  the next 20s refetch once the oracle is live. Total assets / locked / free are read directly from the
  vault and remain visible; only the load decomposition needs the vol oracle.
</Warning>

## Senior vs junior — the explainer that governs your downside

Two cards render the tranche split. The copy is load-bearing because *which tranche you pick is the
single biggest risk decision on this page.*

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Senior — low-variance">
**"Low-variance · underwriting-loss-protected."**

The senior tranche is structurally protected by the junior-first waterfall (`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`)
from **underwriting loss only**. While junior capital is sufficient to cover all locked collateral,
senior is never touched by an ordinary IL payout that exceeds premiums.

It earns less of the premium (junior captures most of it) in exchange for that buffer. It is **not**
risk-free: once the junior buffer is exhausted, senior takes the **systemic tail** — USDC depeg, an
oracle/settlement failure, a contract bug.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Junior — first-loss">
**"First-loss · captures most of the premium."**

The junior tranche absorbs underwriting losses **before** senior is ever touched, in exchange for most
of the premium yield. You are the buffer.

There is also a liquidity caveat baked into the contract: a **junior withdrawal can revert
`JuniorBelowLocked`** while collateral is locked — the protocol will not let junior drain below the
locked amount, because that would break senior's protection (see [Withdraw](#withdraw-request--cooldown--withdraw)).
</Tab>
</Tabs>

Each card shows the tranche's assets and its **% of vault**.

The on-chain mechanism, in one line: junior is subordinated to senior by the
`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` invariant, enforced at withdrawal time so junior cannot drain below the
locked amount and break senior protection. For the full waterfall and worked loss-attribution numbers,
read [the tranches](/concepts/tranches).

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation: "protected" is not "risk-free".**

- **Limit:** the senior tranche is described as "underwriting-loss-protected" — that protection has a
  hard boundary.
- **Why:** the waterfall (`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`) only shields senior *while junior buffers*. It
  does nothing against the systemic tail: a `dUSDC` depeg, an oracle/settlement failure, or a contract
  bug hits the collateral itself, beneath the tranche structure.
- **Mitigation:** FULL collateralization at MaxIL plus the capped payoff bound *underwriting* loss to a
  known shape, and the junior buffer absorbs it first. Beyond that, your protection is the
  [trust model](/security/trust-model) and [invariants](/security/invariants), not a guarantee. Use
  <ClaimB /> as the canonical disclosure on any depositor-facing surface.
</Warning>

## My position

`depositor.getPosition(address)` returns a `Degraded<DepositorPosition>`. It shows your **senior** and
**junior** rows:

- **Value (NAV)** — your shares converted to assets via `convertToAssets` (the share price moves as the
  vault earns premium and pays settlements).
- **Shares** — your raw tranche shares.
- **Pending withdrawal** — any queued withdrawal with a live unlock countdown.

If you hold no shares, an empty-state invites a deposit.

<Note>
Your NAV is **mark-to-vault**, not a fixed balance. It can go **down**: when the pool pays an LP more in
settlements than it collected in premiums, the loss flows through share price — junior first. NAV up is
premium accrual; NAV down is underwriting loss. This is the direct, visible expression of claim B.
</Note>

## Deposit

The deposit panel defaults to the **junior** tranche. Pick a tranche, type an amount, click **Deposit**.
Under the hood it calls `depositor.deposit(tranche, raw, { autoApprove: true })`.

<Steps>
<Step title="Pick a tranche">
Junior (first-loss, most of the premium) or senior (buffered from underwriting loss, less premium). The
default is junior. `TRANCHE_ENUM = { senior: 0, junior: 1 }`.
</Step>
<Step title="Enter an amount in dUSDC (6-dec raw)">
`dUSDC` is the numéraire — **6 decimals**. `1 dUSDC = 1_000_000n`. A 10,000 dUSDC deposit is
`10_000_000_000n` raw. See [addresses & units](/sdk/addresses-units).
</Step>
<Step title="Auto-approve to the ConvexityVault (the spender gotcha)">
With `autoApprove: true`, the SDK reads the ERC-20 `allowance`, and if it is short, sends
`approve(convexityVault, amount)` — **the spender is the `ConvexityVault`, not `InflexionCore`**. Three
different actions in the app use three different spenders; mixing them up is the most common integration
bug. See the table below.
</Step>
<Step title="deposit(tranche, amount)">
The SDK then sends `deposit(tranche, amount)` against the vault. On success the panel shows:
*"Deposit confirmed. Capital is NOT guaranteed."* — the disclosure is repeated even at the moment of
confirmation.
</Step>
</Steps>

```ts SDK — deposit to the junior tranche
import { createInflexionSdk } from '@inflexion/sdk'

const sdk = createInflexionSdk({ publicClient, walletClient })
const dep = sdk.depositor

// Deposit 10,000 dUSDC (6-dec raw) into the JUNIOR tranche; auto-approve to the VAULT (not Core).
const res = await dep.deposit('junior', 10_000_000_000n, { autoApprove: true })
// res.status === 'sent' | 'deferred-no-wallet' | 'error'
```

<Warning>
**The recurring spender gotcha — three different spenders.**

| Action | Spender / vault | Approval flow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LP `buyProtection` (premium) | **InflexionCore** | NPM `approve(core, tokenId)` + dUSDC `approve(core, maxPremium)` |
| **Depositor `deposit`** (this page) | **ConvexityVault** | dUSDC `approve(convexityVault, amount)` then `deposit(tranche, amount)` |
| MM collateral | **UnderwriterVault** | separate ERC-20 approve + deposit |

Approving the wrong spender means the deposit reverts on transfer. On `/earn` it is always the
**ConvexityVault**. The hint text in the deposit panel says so explicitly.
</Warning>

<Tip>
**Build calldata without sending** — for an external signer or a multisig, use `depositor.buildApprove(amount)`
and `depositor.buildDeposit(tranche, amount)` to get `{ to, data, value }`. With no `walletClient`,
`deposit` returns `WriteResult { status: 'deferred-no-wallet' }` carrying the unsigned tx — the write
degrades, it never crashes at construction.
</Tip>

## Withdraw (request → cooldown → withdraw)

Withdrawals are **two-step and cooldown-gated**. You cannot exit instantly — the cooldown is what keeps
locked collateral instantly liquid for settlement while still letting depositors leave in an orderly way.

<Steps>
<Step title="Step 1 — request">
`depositor.requestWithdrawal(tranche, shares)` queues your shares. A **Max** button fills your owned
share balance. A pending banner then shows the queued shares plus an unlock countdown.
</Step>
<Step title="Wait out the cooldown">
The cooldown length is read live via `getWithdrawalCooldown` and shown inline. The Step-2 button stays
disabled until `secondsRemaining === 0n`.
</Step>
<Step title="Step 2 — withdraw">
`depositor.withdraw(tranche)` completes the exit and converts your queued shares back to `dUSDC` at the
current share price.
</Step>
</Steps>

```ts SDK — cooldown-gated withdrawal
// 1. request (queues shares)
await dep.requestWithdrawal('junior', 5_000_000_000n)

// 2. …after the on-chain withdrawalCooldown elapses…
const w = await dep.withdraw('junior')
// A junior withdraw can revert JuniorBelowLocked → w.status === 'error', surfaced honestly
```

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation: a junior withdrawal can revert `JuniorBelowLocked`.**

- **Limit:** even after the cooldown, a **junior** withdrawal can revert with `JuniorBelowLocked`. The
  copy warns: *"A junior withdraw can revert `JuniorBelowLocked` if it would breach senior protection"*.
- **Why:** this is the waterfall doing its job. The contract enforces `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` so
  that junior can never drain below the locked collateral and leave senior under-buffered. If your
  withdrawal would break that, it is rejected.
- **Mitigation:** the SDK surfaces the revert as an `error` `WriteResult` (not a thrown exception), the
  `useTx` hook trims viem's revert dump to a single readable line, and the withdrawal stays queued — you
  can complete it once locked collateral is released by settlements (or once more junior capital is
  deposited). Senior withdrawals are not subject to this gate.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Rebalancing between tranches** is a staged write: `depositor.rebalance(from, to, shares)` sends step 1
(`requestWithdrawal`) immediately and returns steps 2 (`withdraw`) + 3 (`deposit`) **unsent**, to be
executed across the cooldown. The cooldown applies to a tranche switch too.
</Note>

## NAV history — the depositor-risk surface (honest pending)

The NAV-history chart is the **depositor-risk time series**: how the vault's net asset value per share
has moved over time — the visible record of premium accrual vs underwriting drawdown. It is rendered from
`data.getNavHistory({ bucket: '1d' })`.

Today it is **pending**, and the page says so plainly rather than faking a curve.

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation: NAV history is pending.**

- **Limit:** the NAV-history chart shows a typed `PendingNote`, not a line, right now.
- **Why:** NAV history is **subgraph / API-backed** — it is reconstructed from indexed deposit /
  withdraw / settlement events, not from a single live `eth_call`. The subgraph deploy is pending
  (events have been emitted on-chain since deploy block `274081134`), so `getNavHistory` returns an
  `ApiPending` envelope.
- **Mitigation:** the SDK never fabricates history. The `ApiPending` envelope names the **exact future
  route** (`/pool/nav-history`) and the query it will be served from, so the UI renders
  *"coming from `/pool/nav-history`"* and the surface lights up automatically once the subgraph indexes —
  zero re-derivation needed. A pending state is a deliberate trust signal, not a bug:
  [pending ≠ error](/sdk/degradation).
</Warning>

## The quant risk charts

Everything visible on `/earn` is, at bottom, a **risk surface** — the page is built to let a depositor
*see the risk they are taking before they take it*:

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Utilization & the regime badge — how committed the pool is, right now">
**Utilization** = `locked / total` shows what fraction of the pool is currently backing open swaps;
**Locked** (amber) is `Σ MaxIL` of every active protection. The **regime badge**
(`calm` / `normal` / `stressed`) reads σ_ref against the load bands. Together they tell a depositor
whether they are entering a quiet book or a stressed one — high utilization in a
stressed regime means more of your capital is at work against larger expected payouts. This is LIVE via
RPC.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The load stack — the premium your capital earns, decomposed">
σ_ref → Base load (regime floor) → Util skew (convex above the knee) → Disp skew (HHI dispersion) →
Total load, clamped to `maxLoadBps` (I10). This is the markup over the on-chain fair value that the pool
charges LPs — i.e. the gross premium that flows to depositors. The shape of this curve is *why* you are
paid: the load rises with utilization and concentration, pricing the marginal risk your capital absorbs.
Full derivation: [the load stack](/math/load-stack). This is LIVE via RPC; the deterministic load math
runs client-side through the parity-locked TS port because the on-chain `CvammPricing` library is
delegatecall-only.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="NAV history — premium accrual vs underwriting drawdown over time">
The cumulative answer to "did the premiums outrun the payouts?" — the depositor-risk surface proper.
**Pending the subgraph** (framed above). When live it carries claim B.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Where the deeper risk telemetry lives — /data">
Net gamma (the total convexity the protocol is short, pool + every MM), pool-vs-MM spread, and the
clearing-load history are part of the [data moat](/concepts/data-moat) and surface on
[/app/data](/app/data). Net gamma in particular is the systemic-risk readout a serious depositor wants:
how much convexity the whole protocol is short, and whether `Σ free` covers `Σ locked`. Signal 1
(clearing-load surface) is LIVE; the rest are structural-now, dynamic-with-volume.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Info>
**What is LIVE vs pending on this page:**

- **LIVE via RPC, no subgraph needed:** vault state (total / locked / free / utilization), the regime
  badge, the full load stack, σ_ref, your position (NAV / shares), deposit, request, withdraw.
- **Pending the subgraph:** NAV history (framed above), and the deeper time-series moat signals on
  [/app/data](/app/data).
</Info>

## Live proof — depositor capital paying an LP, on-chain

The 2026-06-05 redeploy ran a full create → settle lifecycle. On **Path A**, the `ConvexityVault` (the
pool you deposit into) underwrote and paid an LP entirely from depositor capital:

- swapId 1 &middot; LP tokenId 3220 &middot; V0 **\$270,531.28** &middot; MaxIL **\$1,669.24** &middot; premium
  **\$9.70** (0.58% of MaxIL) &middot; settled payout **\$148.64** from the `ConvexityVault`.

That single swap is claim B in miniature: the pool collected \$9.70 of premium and paid out \$148.64 of
realized IL — a net underwriting loss on that swap, absorbed by the pool's capital (junior first). Across
a healthy book, premiums are sized by the load stack to cover expected payouts with margin; on any
*individual* swap, the pool can and does pay more than it collected. That is the business you are in.
