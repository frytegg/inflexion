The moat's credibility rests on one thing: **the signals must not be circular**.
A dataset that only echoes the protocol's own published
formulas back at the observer is not information — it is a tautology dressed as
alpha. This page exists to demonstrate that we held that line. It shows exactly why
the tempting "implied volatility by inverting `fairRate`" framing is circular, states
the decision rule that separates real signal from echo, and gives the decision table
every one of [the five signals](/moat/five-signals) is checked against.

<Info>
**The discipline, in one sentence.** A number is informative only if its degrees of
freedom come from an **actor's free choice** — the MM's `loadBps` or the LP's
geometry/duration — measured *against* the protocol's transparent mechanical
baseline, **never derived from it**.
</Info>

## The circular idea we deliberately dropped

An earlier draft of the moat framed the headline signal as "recover the market's
**implied volatility** by inverting our published `fairRate` from the charged
premium". It sounds like an options desk:
observe a price, invert your pricing model, read out the implied vol the market is
trading. We **dropped it as circular** — and the reason is worth showing in full,
because the rigor is the point.

### The mechanical identity that makes it a tautology

Every premium the protocol charges is, *by construction*, this identity:

$$
\frac{\text{premium}}{\text{MaxIL}} \;=\; \mathrm{fairRate}(\sigma_{\text{ref}})\,\bigl(1 + \text{load}\bigr)
$$

There are only two unknowns on the right, and **the protocol publishes both of
them**:

- $\mathrm{fairRate}$ is the protocol's own closed form — the exact
  [Φ-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum) computed on-chain by the Stylus
  `FairValueOracle`. It is not a market estimate; it is a deterministic function we
  evaluate and emit.
- $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ is the protocol's own published realized-vol oracle — a
  poke-based EWMA of Chainlink-tick log-returns, readable on-chain via
  `VolOracle.sigmaRef(token)` ([σ_ref](/math/sigma-ref)).

So suppose you "invert `fairRate`" to back out the volatility implied by an observed
premium. What do you get? You feed in a premium that *we computed* using a
$\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ that *we published*, run our published $\mathrm{fairRate}$
backwards, and — to within the dealer load stack — you recover exactly the
$\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ you could have read directly off-chain for free:

$$
\underbrace{\mathrm{fairRate}^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{premium}}{\text{MaxIL}\,(1+\text{load})}\right)}_{\text{"implied} \ \sigma\text{" you back out}} \;=\; \underbrace{\sigma_{\text{ref}}}_{\text{what we already published}}
$$

<Warning>
**This is the circular defect, named.** Inverting `fairRate` from a charged premium
recovers **nothing but our own published $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ plus our own dealer
load stack** — it tells an outside observer of our oracle exactly what they already
knew.

- **Why it fails:** $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ is an *input* we publish, not an output the
  market discovers. Inverting a function to recover its own published input is a
  tautology, not a market-implied quantity. There is no new information in the round
  trip.
- **The fix (path forward):** reframe the entire moat around **actor behavior** — the
  free choices the protocol does *not* compute — measured against the transparent
  mechanical baseline, never derived from it. That fix is
  the rest of this page, and the foundation of [the five signals](/moat/five-signals).
</Warning>

### A real implied vol would have to come from somewhere we don't control

The contrast sharpens the point. On a real options venue, implied vol is informative
*because the price is set by someone other than the pricer* — the market quotes,
you invert *your* model, and the gap between your model's vol and the quoted vol is
the market's view. Here, the "price" you would invert is the price **we** computed
from **our** published vol. The only genuinely external thing in the premium is the
**load the actor chose to charge** — and that is exactly what we keep. Everything
else is the protocol talking to itself.

## The non-circularity decision rule

The fix is a single rule:

<Note>
A signal is non-circular **iff** its informative degrees of freedom come from the
MM's `loadBps` **or** the LP's geometry/duration choice — measured **against** the
transparent mechanical baseline, **not derived from it**.
</Note>

Unpack the two clauses, because both are load-bearing:

<Steps>
<Step title="The degrees of freedom must be an actor's free choice">
The number must vary because **a human (or an MM's algorithm) decided something the
protocol does not compute**. Two such free choices exist in the system:

- **The MM's signed `quote.loadBps`** (Path B) — an external actor's freely chosen
  markup, capped only by `loadParams.maxLoadBps` (invariant I10), never computed by
  the protocol.
- **Which `tokenId` an LP brings, and the duration chosen** — LP demand choice; the
  protocol does not pick the geometry.

If a number is a deterministic function of protocol state — `fairRate`, the pool's
load stack — it has **no free degrees of freedom**. Inverting it recovers our own
formula. That is the circular failure mode.
</Step>

<Step title="It must be measured against the baseline, not derived from it">
The behavioral leg only becomes legible when you place it *next to* the transparent
mechanical baseline (the [pool's `totalLoad`](/math/load-stack)) and read the
**difference**. The subtraction — behavioral minus mechanical — is precisely what
removes the circular component. Derive the signal *from*
the baseline and you are back to inverting our own formula; measure it *against* the
baseline and the residual is the actor's view.
</Step>
</Steps>

This rule is why an MM quote is **per-market, never per-NFT** matters here: because
[MaxIL is the unit of risk](/concepts/maxil) and the load is quoted as a percentage
of MaxIL, the MM's `loadBps` is a clean, range-agnostic actor choice — informative
when bucketed by geometry rather than noisy.

## The decision table

Every candidate number is run through the rule. This is the table the moat is
audited against:

| Source of the number | Circular? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MM-signed `quote.loadBps` (Path B) | **NO** | An external actor's free choice; the protocol does not compute it. Capped only by `loadParams.maxLoadBps` (I10). |
| Which `tokenId` an LP brings + duration chosen | **NO** | LP demand choice; the protocol does not pick the geometry. |
| Path-A pool load `totalLoadWad(σ_ref, util, conc)` | **YES** | Pure deterministic function of protocol state-machine outputs. Inverting it recovers our own formula. **Keep as the mechanical baseline / "price-to-beat", not a behavioral signal.** |
| `fairRate(a, b, σ_ref, T)` | **YES** | Our own published closed form. Inverting it recovers our own $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$. |

Read the table as two columns of fate. The **NO** rows are the only things sold as
signals — they are the free choices of LPs and MMs. The **YES** rows are not
discarded; they are **demoted to the baseline** the **NO** rows are measured
against. The circular quantities still do essential work — they are the transparent
yardstick — they just never appear on the data product labeled as "the market's
view."

<Tip>
**A quick litmus test for any new metric.** Ask: *"If I knew every public protocol
getter, could I compute this number without observing a single fill?"* If yes, it is
mechanical (a **YES** row) — keep it as the baseline. If it requires observing what
an actor actually chose, it is behavioral (a **NO** row) — it can be a signal.
</Tip>

## The two corollaries that fall out of the rule

The decision rule has two consequences that recur in every signal. Internalize them
once here; [the five-signals page](/moat/five-signals) applies them per signal.

### Corollary 1 — the mechanical pool load is the "price-to-beat", not a signal

The Path-A pool load is a **YES** row — fully circular on its own. But it is not
deleted; it is **kept as the transparent yardstick the behavioral MM load is
subtracted from**. Its entire job is to be the
*price-to-beat*: the mechanical quote the MM either undercuts or retreats above. The
spread — behavioral MM load minus mechanical pool load — is what [Signal 2](/moat/five-signals)
extracts, and the subtraction is precisely what removes the circular component.

This is why the pool load surface is the **one live SDK read today**:
`getCurrentLoadSurface` recomputes the price-to-beat client-side from public getters
via the parity-locked `CvammPricing` TypeScript port. The mechanical baseline being
**fully reproducible from public getters** is exactly what makes the behavioral MM
load *measurable against it*.

$$
\text{spread} \;=\; \underbrace{\text{poolLoad}}_{\text{mechanical baseline (YES, kept)}} \;-\; \underbrace{\text{mmLoad}}_{\text{behavioral signal (NO)}}
$$

### Corollary 2 — cap-bound fills carry zero load information and MUST be excluded

When a premium is truncated by the cap — `premium == MaxIL` — the number you observe
is **the cap, not the actor's load**. The load was clipped by the geometry of MaxIL,
so the fill carries **zero load information** and must be excluded from the load
surfaces (Signals 1, 2, and 3).

This is not an off-chain heuristic that depends on brittle floating-point equality.
Deriving `premium == MaxIL` off-chain is fragile under rounding, so the protocol
emits an **explicit `cappedAtMaxIL` boolean** on the `SwapPriced` event so every
consumer can filter cap-bound fills cheaply and unambiguously:

```solidity filename="InflexionCore.sol (SwapPriced, abridged)"
event SwapPriced(
    uint256 indexed swapId,
    uint8   path,            // 0 = cvAMM pool (A), 1 = MM (B)
    uint256 fairPremium,     // the WAD baseline the load multiplies
    /* ... pool-load decomposition fields ... */
    uint256 totalLoadWad,    // Path A: clamped pool load; Path B: MM loadBps in WAD
    uint256 sigmaRefWad,     // the σ_ref the swap was priced against
    bool    cappedAtMaxIL    // true ⇒ premium hit the cap ⇒ ZERO load info
);
```

```typescript filename="exclude cap-bound fills from any load surface"
// A cap-bound fill reports the cap, not the actor's load — drop it.
const loadFills = pricedEvents.filter((e) => !e.cappedAtMaxIL);
```

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation.** The clearing **load level** is contaminated, and
cap-bound fills are uninformative — naked, these look like defects. Framed, they are
the discipline:

- **Limit:** the observed load is contaminated by liquidity, smart-contract-risk,
  capital-lock, and inventory-skew premia; and a cap-bound fill reports the cap, not
  the load.
- **Why:** the cap is [load-bearing for the no-bad-debt guarantee](/concepts/no-bad-debt)
  — truncating the payoff at MaxIL is what makes Path A solvent — so the cap
  legitimately overwrites the load on those fills.
- **Mitigation:** trade the **spread** (MM minus pool, against forward vol), not the
  level; and **exclude `cappedAtMaxIL == true`** fills via the explicit on-chain flag.
</Warning>

## How the rule classifies the five signals

The decision rule and its two corollaries produce the non-circularity verdict for
each of [the five signals](/moat/five-signals). The verdicts below are exact — each
states which leg is behavioral and which is the kept baseline.

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Signal 1 — clearing LOAD over a transparent σ_ref: PARTIAL (Path B only)">
The **Path-B** load (`QuoteFilled.loadBps`) is an MM free choice → **non-circular**.
The **Path-A** load (`SwapPriced.totalLoadWad = f(σ_ref, util, conc)`) is a
deterministic function of protocol state → **circular**; keep it as the mechanical
baseline the MM is measured against, do not sell it as a behavioral signal. **Exclude
cap-bound fills.**
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Signal 2 — pool-vs-MM load SPREAD + win-rate: YES (as a spread)">
Each side is well-defined: pool load (mechanical, `SwapPriced` on the Path-A
candidate) and MM load (behavioral, `QuoteFilled.loadBps`). The **difference**
isolates the actor's view relative to the transparent baseline — the subtraction is
exactly what removes the circular component. This is the
purest expression of Corollary 1.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Signal 3 — TERM STRUCTURE of convexity (slope across 7/30/90d): PARTIAL — and watch the subtlety">
For **Path B**, the slope of `QuoteFilled.loadBps` across durations is behavioral →
non-circular. For **Path A**, the load stack is **duration-INDEPENDENT by
construction** — `baseLoadWad`/`utilSkewWad`/`dispSkewWad` take no `durationSeconds`,
so a Path-A "term structure of load" is a **flat line**.
All maturity dependence lives in `fairRate(...,T)`, our own $\sigma_{\text{ref}}\sqrt{T}$.
Do not conflate the *published `fairRate` term structure* (mechanical) with the
*behavioral load term structure* (Path B). **Exclude cap-bound fills.**
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Signal 4 — moneyness / DEMAND skew by geometry: PARTIAL">
**Realized** demand by geometry is an LP choice (which `tokenId`/duration to bring) →
non-circular for the fills we see. But on-chain we observe **only realized
purchases**, biased by quote availability; **true demand including UNFILLED interest
is off-chain** (relayer `/quote` requests, SDK `previewPremium` pings). The latent
half cannot be on-chain by design — invariant **I7** guarantees an unchosen quote
touches no nonce/capacity.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Signal 5 — net convexity / GAMMA supply: YES (as a supply/quantity gauge)">
The aggregate is built from actor positions; the **quantity** — how much convexity
the protocol is short — is a real-world fact, not a price computation. No inversion of
any published formula is involved, so there is no circular component. The per-swap
Greeks are finite-differenced off the deployed `ILMath.computeIL` /
`FairValueOracle.fairRate`, anchored to protocol math with no parallel model.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Why this rigor is itself part of the moat

Anyone can claim "we have an implied-vol surface." The defensible move is to **show
the math of why the naive version is circular and then ship the version that isn't**.
That is what separates a real microstructure dataset from a re-skinned echo of our
own oracle. The honest framing — and the reason this page exists — is that the moat is
credible *because* we can derive, line by line, why inverting `fairRate` recovers
nothing, and where the genuine information actually enters: the MM's `loadBps` and the
LP's geometry, measured against a baseline we publish in full.
