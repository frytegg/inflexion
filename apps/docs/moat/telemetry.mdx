Two of the [five behavioral signals](/moat/five-signals) need data that **never reaches the chain by design**. An LP who priced a position but did not buy, and an MM quote that lost or was withdrawn, leave **no on-chain trace** — because invariant [I7](/security/invariants) guarantees an *unchosen* quote touches no nonce and no capacity. So the **latent** half of Signal 4 (demand) and the **dynamic** half of Signal 2 (quote competition) live in **off-chain engine telemetry**, captured from the very first interaction — because they are **unreconstructable retroactively**.

This page is the deep treatment of that pipeline: why these two signals can never be on-chain, the two append-only JSONL sinks, the PII-free bucket scheme, the best-effort write contract, the SDK preview ping, and how the API later reads the sinks. For where these two halves sit in the full taxonomy, see [the five signals](/moat/five-signals); for the relayer they ride on, see [the engine](/integrate/engine).

## Why two signals can NEVER be on-chain — invariant I7

The data moat rests on a structural fact: **on-chain, Inflexion only ever observes realized fills.** Everything that did *not* clear is invisible to the chain — and that is not an accident, it is a guarantee.

Invariant [**I7 — capacity authority**](/security/invariants) states that on-chain `consumedNotional[quoteId] ≤ maxNotionalV0`, and that a cancelled bitmap-nonce bit cannot fill. The corollary is that an *unchosen* quote — one that lost the route, expired, or was withdrawn — **touches no nonce and no capacity**. It mutates no state, so it leaves no event, no storage write, nothing.

That makes two of the five signals fundamentally off-chain:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Signal 4 latent half — the demand that did not clear" icon="magnifying-glass-chart">
    On-chain we see only **realized purchases** — `SwapCreated` fires when an LP actually
    buys. But that is biased by quote availability: a geometry queried 100× but filled 10×
    because it was too expensive reads as *low demand* when it is really *high demand at the
    wrong price*. The **true demand including unfilled interest**
    lives in relayer `GET /quote` requests and SDK `previewPremium` calls that never hit the
    chain. The realized half is reconstructable from on-chain events later; the latent half
    is gone forever if not logged now.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Signal 2 dynamic half — the quotes that lost" icon="arrows-left-right">
    A Path-B fill reveals the *winning* MM's load (via [`QuoteFilled`](/moat/primitives)) and
    the *losing pool quote* it beat (via [`SwapPriced`](/moat/primitives)) — the spread of one
    fill. But the **competitive distribution** — who else quoted, how wide they were, who
    widened or withdrew under stress — touches no chain state because those quotes were never
    chosen (I7). Only the realized fill is on-chain; the full competing field is captured only
    in telemetry.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
**These two signals are unreconstructable retroactively — so they must be captured from the first interaction.**

- **Limit:** unfilled interest and losing quotes leave no on-chain trace. If they are not
  logged in real time, they cannot be recovered — there is no archive to backfill from.
- **Why:** putting unfilled interest on-chain would both **cost gas** and **break I7** — an
  unchosen quote must not consume capacity or burn a nonce, or the capacity-authority
  invariant no longer holds. The chain is deliberately the wrong place
  for this data.
- **Mitigation / path-forward:** capture it off-chain from day one. The two JSONL sinks are
  **live now** and have been writing since the first interaction — long before any `/data`
  endpoint, subgraph, or API exists to serve them. The capture *is* the point; the serving
  comes later.
</Warning>

This is the design boundary, drawn precisely:

| Half of the signal | Where it lives | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal 4 **realized** demand | On-chain (`SwapCreated` + geometry decode) → subgraph | A purchase is a state change; the chain records it |
| Signal 4 **latent** demand | Off-chain `DEMAND_LOG` | Priced-but-not-bought touches no chain state (I7) |
| Signal 2 **structural** spread | On-chain (`SwapPriced` + `QuoteFilled`) → subgraph | One fill's pool-vs-MM spread is two emitted events |
| Signal 2 **dynamic** competition | Off-chain `COMPETITION_LOG` | Losing/withdrawn quotes touch no chain state (I7) |

## The pipeline — diagram in prose

The telemetry pipeline is a thin, append-only path that runs *alongside* the relayer, never *in* its trust path. End to end:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The actor interacts off-chain">
    An MM streams a firm quote to the engine over WebSocket, **or** an LP previews a premium
    through the SDK, **or** a taker reads `GET /quote` for a market. None of these is an
    on-chain transaction — they are pure off-chain reads/pushes against the relayer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The relayer handles the interaction normally">
    It verifies the EIP-712 signature, applies a light freshness gate, and stores the
    cheapest live quote — its [actual job](/integrate/engine#the-websocket-protocol-mm-relayer).
    The store keeps only the *latest* quote per `(market, MM)`, so by itself it forgets the
    competing field.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The TelemetrySink writes a record — best-effort">
    The `TelemetrySink` appends one JSONL line to the
    relevant sink: a `CompetitionRecord` for **every** inbound quote (winner or loser), a
    `DemandRecord` for **every** `GET /quote` and **every** preview ping. The write is
    best-effort: a telemetry failure **never** breaks the quote ack, the preview, or the
    `/quote` request.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The API reads the sinks and serves aggregates">
    Post-redeploy, [`@inflexion/api`](/integrate/api-overview) reads the JSONL files (not a
    subgraph entity, not a contract event) and serves `GET /data/demand-requests` (latent)
    and `GET /data/quote-competition` (dynamic), each carrying the maturity disclaimer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

```
  MM quote (WS)  ──┐
  /quote  (HTTP) ──┼──►  engine relayer  ──►  TelemetrySink.appendJsonl  ──►  DEMAND_LOG (JSONL)
  previewPremium ──┘     (verify+freshness)    (best-effort, never throws)     COMPETITION_LOG (JSONL)
                                                                                       │
                                                                                       ▼
                                                              @inflexion/api reads files (no subgraph,
                                                              no event) ──► /data/demand-requests
                                                                           /data/quote-competition
```

<Note>
**This is a structured log/telemetry pipeline — NOT a subgraph entity and NOT an on-chain
event.** The
subgraph indexes the *on-chain* halves of the signals; the telemetry sinks capture the
*off-chain* halves the chain can never carry. They are complementary layers, joined only at
the API.
</Note>

## `DEMAND_LOG` — Signal 4, the latent half

Written on **every `GET /quote` request** and **every `POST /telemetry/preview` ping**. The defining field is `filled`, which is **always `false`** at write time — this log is precisely the demand the chain never sees. Realized fills are reconstructed separately, later, from on-chain `SwapCreated`/`SwapPriced`.

The `DemandRecord` schema:

```jsonc DemandRecord
{ "ts": 1900000000,
  "marketId": "0x…",            // bytes32, lowercased
  "widthBucket": "tight",       // tight | medium | wide | full | unknown
  "distanceBucket": "at-edge",  // at-edge | near | mid | deep | unknown
  "durationBucket": "week",     // hour | day | week | month | longer | unknown
  "previewedPremium": "777",    // optional decimal string; absent for a bare /quote
  "filled": false,              // ALWAYS false — the latent / unfilled half
  "source": "preview" }         // "preview" | "quote-request"
```

Two write paths feed it:

- **`logQuoteRequest(marketId, now)`** — emitted by `GET /quote`. A bare lookup carries no
  geometry, so all three buckets are `'unknown'` and `source` is `'quote-request'`.
  This captures *interest in a market* even when the requester
  supplies no position.
- **`ingestPreview(input, now)`** — emitted by `POST /telemetry/preview`. It validates and
  normalizes the loosely-typed body: `normalizeMarketId` requires bytes32 hex,
  `normalizePremium` coerces a string/number/bigint into a bigint-safe decimal string or
  drops it; `source` is `'preview'`. It returns `{ ok, logged, reason? }`. This is the rich
  path — it carries the actual geometry buckets the LP previewed.

## `COMPETITION_LOG` — Signal 2, the dynamic half

Written for **every** inbound WS quote — **winners AND losers/stale/withdrawn-equivalent** (`logQuote`), called from both the WS handler and `POST /quote`. The `CompetitionRecord` schema:

```jsonc CompetitionRecord
{ "ts": 1900000000,
  "marketId": "0x…",            // bytes32, lowercased
  "mm": "0x…",                  // MM address, lowercased
  "loadBps": 500,
  "validUntil": "1900000010",   // uint64 as decimal string
  "accepted": true,             // true iff stored as a live candidate (verify+freshness passed)
  "reason": "bad-signature" }   // present only when accepted=false (mirrors the WS rejection)
```

<Info>
**Why log the losers at all.** The quote [`store`](/integrate/engine#the-quote-store) keeps
only the *latest* stored quote per `(market, MM)` — so by itself it loses the competition:
who else quoted, how wide, who widened or withdrew under stress. The chain only ever sees the
realized fill (I7), so the **full competing field is captured only here**. `accepted` marks
whether the relayer stored the quote as a live candidate (signature + freshness valid); a
*rejected* quote still competed and is still logged, with `reason` mirroring the WS rejection.
</Info>

This `accepted=false` row is what makes the dynamic half real: an MM that quotes wide and gets passed over, or whose stale quote is dropped, is exactly the behavior that — across competing MMs — reads as *the market widening under stress*. None of it is on-chain. None of it is reconstructable after the fact.

## PII-free buckets — the labels, never the geometry

The telemetry sinks hold **no PII, no raw geometry, no `tokenId`, and no addresses on the demand path** — only coarse buckets plus the `marketId`. The privacy property is structural: **the SDK computes the buckets client-side and POSTs only the labels.** The raw range never leaves the LP's machine.

The three bucket dimensions:

| Bucket | Values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `WidthBucket` | `tight \| medium \| wide \| full \| unknown` | tick-span of the LP range |
| `DistanceBucket` | `at-edge \| near \| mid \| deep \| unknown` | how near spot is to going out of range |
| `DurationBucket` | `hour \| day \| week \| month \| longer \| unknown` | $\text{expiry} - \text{createdAt}$ |

Unknown or invalid input coerces to `'unknown'` via `asWidthBucket` / `asDistanceBucket` / `asDurationBucket` — a malformed bucket never becomes a fake data point, it becomes an honest `unknown`. These are the same geometry primitives the moat buckets every signal by — **width × distance-to-edge × duration** — but reduced to labels before they ever leave the client:

$$
\text{width} = \log\!\frac{P_b}{P_a},
\qquad
\text{distance-to-edge} = \frac{\min(P_0 - P_a,\; P_b - P_0)}{P_0},
\qquad
\text{duration} = \text{expiry} - \text{createdAt}
$$

<Note>
**Why bucketing makes the data both private and useful.** Because [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) is
the unit of risk and makes positions *fungible within a market*, the informative dimension
is not the exact range — it is the **geometry class** (tight near-edge week vs wide centered
month). Bucketing throws away the identifying detail while keeping precisely the dimension
the [demand-skew signal](/moat/five-signals) reads. Privacy and signal point the same way
here, not against each other.
</Note>

## Best-effort writes — graceful degradation as a first-class property

The writer (`appendJsonl`) is **best-effort: it never throws.** A full disk, a bad path, or an unconfigured sink must not break a quote ack, a preview, or a `/quote` request.

<Warning>
**A telemetry write can silently fail — by design, this never propagates to the caller.**

- **Limit:** a write to `DEMAND_LOG` or `COMPETITION_LOG` can fail (disk full, bad path) or
  be skipped entirely (no sink configured). When it does, that data point is lost.
- **Why:** telemetry is a **side channel**, not the relayer's job. The relayer's contract is
  to verify and serve quotes; making a quote ack depend on a successful log write would
  couple a convenience surface to the critical path and could break quoting on a disk error.
  Graceful degradation is a first-class property of the whole access layer.
- **Mitigation / path-forward:** the writer is wrapped so it never throws; on failure the
  surface reports `logged:false` rather than erroring, and the interaction completes
  normally. The two sinks **must be configured from the first deploy** (`DEMAND_LOG` /
  `COMPETITION_LOG` env vars) — because the data is
  unreconstructable, an unconfigured sink is the only real loss, and that is operational,
  not a code path failure.
</Warning>

Sink status is observable, never silent: `get demandEnabled` / `get competitionEnabled` report whether each sink path is configured, and `GET /health` surfaces both:

```json
{ "ok": true,
  "markets": 3,
  "telemetry": { "demand": true, "competition": true } }
```

A `false` here is the honest signal that a sink is unconfigured — capture is degraded, and it shows.

## The SDK preview ping

The LP-side trigger for the latent-demand half is `LpClient.previewPremium`, which fires a best-effort, fire-and-forget `POST ${engineBaseUrl}/telemetry/preview` that **never blocks or fails the preview**. The LP gets their premium estimate whether or not the ping lands; the ping is pure upside for the moat.

The endpoint reads at most **4096 bytes** and accepts only bucketed geometry plus `marketId` — no PII:

<CodeGroup>
```bash POST /telemetry/preview (curl)
# Day-one demand ping — buckets only, no PII, no tokenId, no address
curl -s -X POST http://localhost:8787/telemetry/preview \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"marketId":"0x...","widthBucket":"tight","distanceBucket":"at-edge","durationBucket":"week","previewedPremium":"777"}'
# → 202 {"ok":true,"logged":true}
# logged:false when no DEMAND_LOG sink is configured
```

```typescript SDK (conceptual)
// LpClient.previewPremium computes the buckets client-side, then fires
// the ping in the background — it does NOT await it, and a failed ping
// never affects the returned premium.
const preview = await lp.previewPremium({ tokenId, durationSeconds });
// preview = { fairPremium, poolPremium, mmLoadBps?, ... }
// side effect (fire-and-forget): POST /telemetry/preview with
//   { marketId, widthBucket, distanceBucket, durationBucket, previewedPremium }
```
</CodeGroup>

Outcomes: a valid `marketId` returns `202 { ok:true, logged:true|false }` — `logged:false` when no sink is configured; a missing or invalid `marketId` returns `400 { ok:false, reason:"marketId (bytes32 hex) required" }`. It **always 202-acks fast and never blocks the preview**.

<Tip>
The `GET /quote` path *also* writes a `DemandRecord` (with `'unknown'` buckets) before it
answers, so even a taker who never previews through the SDK leaves a latent-demand trace for
the market they looked at. The preview ping is the *rich*
demand source; the `/quote` log is the *broad* one.
</Tip>

## How the API serves the two halves

The two sinks are read by [`@inflexion/api`](/integrate/api-overview), which reads the JSONL **files** directly — not a subgraph entity, not a contract event — and serves aggregated views:

| Sink | API route | Signal | Aggregation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `DEMAND_LOG` | `GET /data/demand-requests` | 4 (latent half) | `demandSurface` → per `(market × width × distance × duration)`: `{ count, previews, quoteRequests, firstSeen, lastSeen }` |
| `COMPETITION_LOG` | `GET /data/quote-competition` | 2 (dynamic half) | `competitionSurface` → per `(market × MM)`: `{ quotes, accepted, rejected, minLoadBps, maxLoadBps, avgLoadBps, lastSeen }` |

The reader degrades as gracefully as the writer: an absent or unreadable sink yields `[]` and the handler emits a typed `pending('telemetry-sink-absent', …)`; a malformed JSONL line is skipped so a partial last write never breaks the read; **the reader never throws**.

Crucially, the **latent demand half is LIVE now** — `/data/demand-requests` returns the latent half from the telemetry sink even while the realized half is subgraph-`pending`, each carrying its own availability flag. Signal 4 is the one intrinsically split surface, and telemetry is why half of it is already real.

```bash GET /data/quote-competition (winners AND losers)
curl -s 'http://localhost:8088/data/quote-competition?marketId=0x...&since=1900000000'
# → {"available":true,"competition":[{"mm":"0x...","quotes":12,"accepted":10,"rejected":2,
#     "minLoadBps":480,"avgLoadBps":510,"maxLoadBps":540,...}],"enabled":true,"disclosure":"..."}
# sink absent → {"available":false,"reason":"telemetry-sink-absent","detail":"COMPETITION_LOG not configured..."}
```

## Maturity — what telemetry gives you today

<Maturity />

The telemetry **capture** is live and complete from the first interaction — that is the part that *had* to ship first, because it is unreconstructable. What matures with volume is the **read**: a single MM's competition log is one quoter, not a market; the dynamic forward-vol signal of Signal 2 needs **≥3 competing MMs** before the dispersion of loads becomes a market view rather than one undercut. Likewise, latent demand sharpens into a *leading indicator* only once the request stream has enough volume to separate demand from price-sensitivity.

<Note>
**The honest framing.** The two off-chain halves of the moat are *being recorded right now*,
structured and PII-free, from interaction one. The dynamics they will eventually reveal are a
function of MM competition and flow — but the irreversible decision (to capture before there
was anything to serve) is already made and already running.
</Note>
