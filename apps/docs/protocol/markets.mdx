A **market** in Inflexion is a `(token0, token1, fee, durationSeconds)` tuple — a
specific Uniswap v3 pair, a specific fee tier, and a specific contract maturity. The
deploy registers **9 markets for one pair**: 3 fee tiers crossed with 3 durations, all
underwritten by a **single [`ConvexityVault`](/protocol/vaults)** with fungible USDC
capital. Each market has a deterministic identity — its `marketId`, a tight-packed
`keccak256` hash that is **byte-for-byte identical** across the contract, the SDK, and
the subgraph.

<Note>
The market grid is intentionally small and dense: **9 product lines, one treasury.**
The duration is baked into the `marketId`, so the *same* position geometry prices
differently in the 7d, 30d and 90d markets — the
[S-curve in $\sigma^2 T$](/concepts/fair-value) does the work, while
[MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) (pure geometry) is identical across all three. One pair, one
pool of collateral, three prices for the same risk.
</Note>

## Why 9 markets over one vault

The two axes are the two things that change the *risk* of underwriting a position
without changing its *geometry*:

- **Fee tier** selects which Uniswap v3 pool the LP's position lives in. Different fee
  tiers have different tick spacing and different liquidity profiles, so they are
  genuinely different markets even for the same token pair.
- **Duration** is the contract maturity. A longer maturity gives the price more time to
  diffuse, so the same range is more likely to realize a larger fraction of its
  [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil). This is the entire reason the cvAMM publishes **three
  different prices for the same position** — see [the grid](#the-9-markets-grid).

Crucially, **all 9 markets are backed by the same `ConvexityVault`** with **fungible
capital**. One vault per pair acts like a single insurer's
treasury backing nine product lines — capital flows to wherever the demand is, and
intra-pair diversification (different fees, different durations) lowers the pool's
aggregate tail. The per-market locked amount is tracked in
`lockedByMarket[marketId]`, but the free capital that backs new fills is shared.

<Tip>
Adding a token pair multiplies the grid: each pair gets its **own** `ConvexityVault`
and its **own** 9-market grid. The dWETH/dUSDC pair is the live pair; the registry
architecture (`registerMarket`) generalizes to any oracle-priced numéraire pair.
</Tip>

## The 9 markets grid

The deploy registers **9 markets = 3 fee tiers x 3 durations** for the dWETH/dUSDC
pair. The axes:

- **Fee tiers** (`uint24`): `500` (0.05%), `3000` (0.30%), `10000` (1.00%).
- **Durations**: `7d` (604,800 s), `30d` (2,592,000 s), `90d` (7,776,000 s).

The frontend builds the grid as `FEES.flatMap(fee => DURATIONS.map(...))`, iterating
**fee-outer, duration-inner**, which fixes the index
layout of `MARKETS[0..8]`:

| | **7d** (604,800 s) | **30d** (2,592,000 s) | **90d** (7,776,000 s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **fee 500 (0.05%)** | `MARKETS[0]` — demo `0x67c4…9d69` | `MARKETS[1]` | `MARKETS[2]` |
| **fee 3000 (0.30%)** | `MARKETS[3]` | `MARKETS[4]` | `MARKETS[5]` |
| **fee 10000 (1.00%)** | `MARKETS[6]` | `MARKETS[7]` | `MARKETS[8]` |

`MARKETS[0]` is the live, three-way-parity-pinned market:
`0x67c4bee1ee037851fbe2a8ecfdd0b8ae3d358283e940750c268621f776479d69` (dWETH, dUSDC,
fee `500`, `604800` s = 7d) — it matches the on-chain address registry's
`marketId_fee500_7d`, and `DEMO_MARKET_ID = demo.marketId_fee500_7d` sanity-checks
against `MARKETS[0]`.

<Warning>
**The short 300s lifecycle market is NOT one of the 9.** The live create→settle run
used a *separate, extra* market (`0xb8bbd684…`) registered only to exercise the full
lifecycle without a 7-day wait. **Why** the extra market:
a 7d minimum maturity makes a same-session settle impossible. **Mitigation:** the
300s market shares the exact same code path and settlement core — it is the 9-market
grid run at an accelerated clock, not a different product.
</Warning>

### Same geometry, three prices

Because the duration is part of the `marketId`, a single LP position fills against a
**different marketId per duration** and is priced **per duration** — while its
[MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) is identical across all three (MaxIL is pure geometry, frozen
at creation, [L-independent in the fair-rate sense](/math/maxil-derivation)). All three
prices share the **same MaxIL** but a **different `fairRate`**:

$$
\text{FairPremium}(T) = \text{fairRate}(\sigma_{\text{ref}}^2\, T)\cdot \text{MaxIL},
\qquad
\text{fairRate} = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[\min(IL,\ \text{MaxIL})\right]}{\text{MaxIL}}
$$

`fairRate` is an **S-curve in $\sigma_{\text{ref}}^2\, T$** — it carries all of the
volatility and time dependence, and $\approx 0$ for a calm/short market, $\to 1$ for a
violent/long one. MaxIL carries none of it. The
7d / 30d / 90d markets are exactly the three points $T \in \{604800, 2592000, 7776000\}$
on that one curve. The duration axis of this grid **is** the
[convexity term structure](/concepts/data-moat) the data moat publishes as Signal 3.

## The marketId derivation

`marketId` is the join key that ties the **contract**, the **SDK**, and the
**subgraph** together, so its byte layout is **load-bearing** — a single field-width
mismatch produces a different, non-matching hash and silently breaks every cross-system
lookup.

$$
\text{marketId} = \texttt{keccak256}\big(\texttt{abi.encodePacked}(\text{token0},\ \text{token1},\ \text{fee},\ \text{durationSeconds})\big)
$$

with `token0 = dWETH`, `token1 = dUSDC` (ordered so `token0 < token1` by address). The
ABI types are `(address, address, uint24, uint32)`.

<Warning>
`abi.encodePacked` is **TIGHT packing — no 32-byte ABI padding.** The preimage is
exactly **20 + 20 + 3 + 4 = 47 bytes**. **Why this matters:**
if any reimplementation used standard (padded) ABI encoding — e.g. the subgraph's
`ethereum.encode`, which pads every operand to 32 bytes — it would hash a **128-byte**
preimage and produce a **different `marketId`**, silently de-syncing the indexer from
the chain. **Mitigation:** all three implementations use raw big-endian field widths,
and two parity tests pin the result (see [three-way parity](#three-way-parity)).
</Warning>

### Where it lives in code

<CodeGroup>
```solidity registerMarket
// Owner registers each market; marketId is derived, never supplied
bytes32 marketId = keccak256(
    abi.encodePacked(cfg.token0, cfg.token1, cfg.fee, cfg.durationSeconds)
);
```

```solidity create cross-check
// On every create, the contract re-derives the marketId from the LIVE
// token0/token1/fee read off positions(tokenId) plus the market's duration,
// and requires it equals the requested marketId else `MarketMismatch`.
// The LP cannot lie about which market they are filling.
```

```ts SDK / frontend mirror
import { encodePacked, keccak256 } from "viem";

function computeMarketId(
  token0: `0x${string}`,
  token1: `0x${string}`,
  fee: number,            // uint24
  durationSeconds: number // uint32
): `0x${string}` {
  return keccak256(
    encodePacked(
      ["address", "address", "uint24", "uint32"],
      [token0, token1, fee, durationSeconds]
    )
  );
}
```
</CodeGroup>

On-chain, the `marketId` is **derived, never trusted from calldata**: `registerMarket`
hardens it three ways:

1. **The oracle token must be one of the pair.**
2. **Decimals are read on-chain**, never trusted from calldata — one wrong digit would
   mis-scale every price ~10x.
3. **Price-config immutability** — the oracle orientation is frozen once set, so
   re-registering a market can never re-price an active swap.

Path A markets must additionally be opted in via `setCvammEnabled`.

### The subgraph's hand-rolled replication

The subgraph cannot import the contract, so it re-derives the `marketId` in AssemblyScript
inside `deriveMarketId`: it concatenates the raw big-endian bytes
of each field **at its on-chain width** — address → 20 bytes, `uint24 fee` → 3 bytes,
`uint32 durationSeconds` → 4 bytes — then `crypto.keccak256`.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why a hand-rolled uintBytesBE, not ethereum.encode">
    The subgraph deliberately uses a hand-rolled `uintBytesBE`,
    **not** `ethereum.encode`, because `ethereum.encode` ABI-pads every operand to 32
    bytes and would produce a *different, non-matching* hash.
    This is the same `abi.encodePacked` vs padded-ABI trap, flagged at the
    implementation level.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The uint32 sign-safety subtlety (durationSeconds ≥ 2³¹)">
    `durationSeconds` is a `uint32` on-chain but arrives in AssemblyScript as an `i32`.
    A value ≥ 2³¹ arrives **negative** after `BigInt.toI32()` and sign-extends to
    `i64`. Because `uintBytesBE` emits only the low `width` bytes and `& 0xff` masks
    each one, the sign bits (which live *above* `width`) are never written — so the
    `uint32` preimage stays bit-for-bit correct across the full on-chain domain.
    None of the live durations approach 2³¹ s, but the encoder
    is correct across the entire type domain regardless.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How the mapping recovers the marketId from a SwapCreated event">
    A `SwapRecord` carries **no marketId**, so the subgraph
    reconstructs it: (1) it reads `token0/token1/fee` from the NPM `positions()`
    enrichment call, (2) reads `expiry`/`createdAt` from `InflexionCore.swaps()` to
    compute `durationSeconds`, then (3) calls `deriveMarketId` — reusing the NPM bind,
    not re-binding. It needs **all four** fields, so both
    the geometry decode and the swap-record read must succeed.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Three-way parity

The 47-byte tight-packed preimage must stay identical across all three systems, or the
join key breaks:

| System | Implementation |
| --- | --- |
| **Contract** | `abi.encodePacked` in `registerMarket` + the create cross-check |
| **SDK** | `computeMarketId` (also mirrored in the frontend) |
| **Subgraph** | `deriveMarketId` (hand-rolled `uintBytesBE`) |

All three are pinned by **two parity tests** to the live market `marketId`
`0x67c4bee1ee037851fbe2a8ecfdd0b8ae3d358283e940750c268621f776479d69`
(dWETH, dUSDC, fee `500`, `604800` s = 7d):

- An on-chain parity test covers the contract side.
- An SDK parity test covers the SDK / width-contract side.

That pinned value matches the on-chain address registry exactly. **Any
field-width change that breaks the match fails those tests** — the parity is enforced
by CI, not by convention. See [marketId parity](/integrate/marketid-parity) for the
integrator-facing checklist.

<Info>
**Why the duration is *inside* the id, not a side parameter.** Folding
`durationSeconds` into the hash makes each (geometry, duration) pair its own first-class
market with its own price, its own capacity, and its own row in every aggregate. It is
what lets the [data moat](/concepts/data-moat) read a clean term structure: the 7d / 30d
/ 90d rows of one geometry are three distinct `marketId`s that the
[subgraph](/integrate/subgraph) keys on directly.
</Info>

## How a market is used downstream

The `marketId` threads through the whole protocol as the single coordinate that names a
product line:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Quote — Path B">
    A [SignedQuote](/protocol/path-b-mm) carries `marketId` (**never a `tokenId`**) —
    an MM quotes a `(load, MaxIL-ratio band, capacity)` for an entire market, and any
    in-band position in that market fills against it. This
    is the mechanical consequence of [MaxIL making positions fungible](/concepts/maxil).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create — cross-check">
    Every `create*` entry point re-derives the `marketId` from the live position
    geometry and requires it equals the requested one,
    so the rail can only fill the market the LP actually owns a position in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lock — per-market accounting">
    On Path A, `ConvexityVault.lockCollateral(marketId, maxIL)` increments
    `lockedByMarket[marketId]` and the O(1) HHI accumulator, while the **free** capital
    that backs the lock is shared across all 9 markets.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Index — the join key">
    The [subgraph](/integrate/subgraph) keys `Market`, `MarketStateSnapshot`, and the
    per-fill `Swap` rows on `marketId`, and the API joins the
    on-chain facts to off-chain telemetry on that same key.
  </Step>
</Steps>
