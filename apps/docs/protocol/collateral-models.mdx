FULL and PARTIAL are not two pools and not two products. They are **one dial** on the
**one** [`ConvexityVault`](/protocol/vaults) — a choice about *how much collateral an
underwriter posts per unit of [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil)*. Turn the dial all the way to
collateral $=$ MaxIL and you get FULL: structurally no bad debt, no monitoring, no
liquidation. Turn it down and you get PARTIAL: more positions per dollar of capital, but
real bad-debt risk that must be *engineered away* with floors, fees, buffers, and circuit
breakers — none of which can be invented; all of which come out of the Monte Carlo.

<Note>
**Today the dial only turns to FULL.** `CollateralModel` is `{FULL, PARTIAL}` in the
shipped enum, but every create path requires `model == FULL` and PARTIAL reverts
`UnsupportedModel`. PARTIAL is a *designed-in,
gated-off* roadmap mode — the gate is the quant, not the code.
</Note>

## The dial, defined

Define the **leverage** of a swap as the ratio of MaxIL to the collateral the underwriter
actually locks:

$$
\text{leverage} \;=\; \frac{\text{MaxIL}}{\text{collateral}}, \qquad
\text{collateral}_{\text{FULL}} = \text{MaxIL}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{leverage} = 1.
$$

- **FULL** posts $\text{collateral} = \text{MaxIL}$, so leverage is exactly $1$. The
  underwriter sets aside the entire maximum possible payout up front.
- **PARTIAL** posts $\text{collateral} = r \cdot \text{MaxIL} + \text{buffer}$ for some
  ratio $r < 1$ (`partialRatioBps` in the [SignedQuote](/concepts/two-rails)), so leverage
  is $> 1$ and the same capital underwrites more notional. The shortfall is what the rest
  of the PARTIAL machinery exists to cover.

Both modes share the **identical settlement core** — the same `settle`, the same
$\min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ cap, the same [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) geometry.
The dial lives entirely *upstream* of settlement, in how much is locked at create. What
changes downstream is only the question **"is the locked collateral always $\ge$ the
payout?"** — which FULL answers structurally and PARTIAL answers probabilistically.

## FULL — the launch mode and the no-bad-debt headline

FULL is the only mode live today, and it is the headline because of one line of algebra.
Because the contract locks $\text{collateral} = \text{MaxIL}$ at create
(on Path A and on Path B) and the payout is capped at MaxIL at
settle:

$$
\text{payout} \;=\; \min(\text{realized\_IL},\, \text{MaxIL}) \;\le\; \text{MaxIL} \;=\; \text{collateral}.
$$

So $\text{payout} \le \text{collateral}$ holds **by construction of the cap** — under
*every* price path, with no assumption about volatility, no monitoring, and no liquidation.
This is [invariant I1](/security/invariants), and it is *structural*: it does not depend on
the [volatility oracle](/protocol/oracles) being right, only on the cap equalling the lock.

<Info>
This is exactly why **FULL needs no risk engine.** Liquidation is mathematically
impossible — there is no margin to maintain, no health
factor to watch, no keeper to keep the position solvent. The collateral was correct the
moment it was locked and stays correct until settle. The price oracle is
**not** load-bearing for I1; it only affects pricing and depositor solvency.
</Info>

### FULL on both rails

The dial reads the same on FULL regardless of which [rail](/concepts/two-rails) opens the
swap, because FULL is enforced at create on both:

| | **Path A — cvAMM** | **Path B — MM** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collateral locked | `MaxIL`, from `ConvexityVault` | `MaxIL`, from the MM's `UnderwriterVault` |
| Model gate | implicit FULL (PARTIAL has no Path-A entry) | `require model == FULL` else `UnsupportedModel` |
| Vault solvency invariant | `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` | `locked ≤ deposited` per MM, I5 |
| Settlement | identical `settle` core | identical `settle` core |

On Path B the FULL gate is explicit: `createSwap` reverts `UnsupportedModel` for any
`model != FULL`, and `partialRatioBps` is required to be `0` in
FULL (the `SignedQuote` struct). On Path A there simply is no
PARTIAL entry point to reach.

## PARTIAL — the capital-efficiency mode, gated on the quant

PARTIAL would let an underwriter lock *less than* MaxIL per swap and so write more notional
per dollar of capital. That is genuinely valuable — but it **breaks the structural
guarantee**: once $\text{collateral} < \text{MaxIL}$, the inequality
$\text{payout} \le \text{collateral}$ no longer holds for free. A position that runs to its
MaxIL can now exceed the collateral behind it, and the difference is **real bad debt**.

PARTIAL therefore replaces a *proof* (FULL's one-line algebra) with a *control system*: a
stack of floors, fees, buffers, exposure caps, and circuit breakers tuned so that the
probability and magnitude of bad debt stay inside an explicitly chosen risk budget. None of
those numbers can be guessed.

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation — PARTIAL is not live today.**

- **Limit.** Every PARTIAL create path reverts `UnsupportedModel`. You cannot under-collateralize a swap on the live
  deployment.
- **Why.** PARTIAL's safety is *statistical*, not structural. The floor curve, fee curve,
  liquidation buffer, circuit-breaker thresholds, withdrawal delay, exposure caps, and
  first-loss size that keep bad-debt risk inside budget can only be derived from a
  **Monte-Carlo calibration** of the actual return/vol distribution
  ([the quant](/protocol/pricing-stack)). Shipping PARTIAL with *placeholder* constants
  would be shipping a control system tuned to a fictional world.
- **Mitigation.** PARTIAL is **gated** behind the quant and **fully params-driven**: every
  one of those constants is read from the calibrated risk-parameter set,
  never hardcoded. The enum value exists so the wiring is in place; the mode stays
  `UnsupportedModel` until the calibrated risk-parameter set carries a calibrated PARTIAL block. The gate opens
  by *data landing*, not by code being written.
</Warning>

### Why no PARTIAL constant may ever be hardcoded

This is the single most load-bearing rule in the PARTIAL design, and it is a direct
response to the [external audit](/security/audits). Hardcoding any PARTIAL primitive — a
liquidation buffer of "10%", a circuit breaker at "σ × 3", a first-loss slice of "5%" — is
exactly the failure the audit flagged: it bakes a guess about the world into the contract
and calls it a safety margin. The correct shape is a calibration pipeline, not a constant:

$$
\underbrace{\text{return/vol distribution}}_{\text{historical + simulated}}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{Monte Carlo}\;}\;
\text{calibrated risk-parameter set}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{owner setter}\;}\;
\text{on-chain PARTIAL parameters}
$$

The precedent is already shipped for the FULL load stack: `baseLoad` regimes, both skew
curves, and `maxLoadBps` are read from the calibrated risk-parameter set via `LoadParams`,
set by the owner through `InflexionCore.setLoadParams` — none hardcoded. PARTIAL extends the same pattern to its
full constant set.

### The PARTIAL parameter set — every constant is params-driven

Each of these is a constant *today's FULL mode does not need* and *PARTIAL cannot ship
without*. All are sourced from the calibrated risk-parameter set, none hardcoded.

| Constant | What it controls | Why it cannot be a guess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Floor curve** | Minimum collateral ratio $r$ as a function of geometry / vol regime — the lower bound on how thin PARTIAL may run | The thinnest safe ratio depends on the tail of the IL distribution for that geometry; only the Monte Carlo knows where it sits |
| **Fee curve** | The extra premium PARTIAL charges for the bad-debt risk it takes on | Must price the *expected shortfall* the under-collateralization creates — a distributional quantity |
| **Liquidation buffer** | Headroom held above the floor so a position can be unwound before it crosses into bad debt | Sized to the speed of adverse moves vs the unwind latency — pure simulation output |
| **Circuit-breaker thresholds** | Vol / drawdown levels at which new PARTIAL writing pauses | The level that meaningfully reduces tail risk without over-tripping is a calibrated trade-off |
| **Withdrawal delay** | Cooldown on depositor exits so capacity cannot evaporate mid-stress | Must outlast the worst plausible stress window in the simulated paths |
| **Exposure caps** | Maximum PARTIAL notional per market / concentration | The cap that keeps correlated MaxIL hits inside budget is a portfolio-level Monte-Carlo result |
| **First-loss size** | The junior slice that absorbs PARTIAL bad debt before it touches anyone else | Sized so $P(\text{loss} > \text{first-loss})$ stays inside the risk budget — a tail quantile |

The first-loss / junior interaction here is about *PARTIAL bad debt*, distinct from the
ordinary [tranche](/concepts/tranches) waterfall that operates in FULL today — see the
[tranches page](/concepts/tranches) for the SENIOR/JUNIOR mechanics that ship now. This
page is about the FULL/PARTIAL dial; it deliberately does not re-derive the tranche
accounting.

## FULL vs PARTIAL side by side

| | **FULL (live)** | **PARTIAL (roadmap, gated)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collateral per swap | $= \text{MaxIL}$ (leverage $1$) | $< \text{MaxIL}$ + buffer (leverage $> 1$) |
| No-bad-debt | **Structural** — `payout ≤ collateral` by construction (I1) | **Statistical** — kept inside a risk budget by the control stack |
| Bad-debt risk | None under the [qualified clause](/concepts/no-bad-debt) | Real — the reason for floors / fees / buffers / breakers |
| Monitoring | None — liquidation mathematically impossible | Active — circuit breakers, liquidation buffer, exposure caps |
| Constants needed | FULL load stack only (already params-driven) | Full PARTIAL set, **all** from the calibrated risk-parameter set |
| Capital efficiency | Lower — one MaxIL locked per swap | Higher — same capital underwrites more notional |
| Status in code | Enforced on every path | Reverts `UnsupportedModel` |
| Gate to ship | — (live) | Monte-Carlo-calibrated PARTIAL block |

The honest framing: **FULL trades capital efficiency for a proof; PARTIAL would trade the
proof for capital efficiency, and the price of that trade is a calibration the protocol
refuses to fake.**

## Who carries the risk in each mode

The no-bad-debt guarantee (Claim A) is about the **LP being paid**. It is a *separate*
claim from whether the **underwriter's** capital is safe (Claim B) — and the two never
merge. The dial changes the underwriter's risk profile, not the LP's:

- In **FULL**, the LP is paid under the qualified clause and the underwriter's only
  underwriting loss is the ordinary $\text{payout} = \min(\text{IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ out of
  collateral they posted — never *bad* debt, because they posted the full MaxIL.
- In **PARTIAL**, the LP is still paid up to the protocol's coverage, but the
  under-collateralization means some of that payout can exceed the individual swap's
  collateral, and the **first-loss / junior** capital is what stands behind the gap. That
  is precisely why PARTIAL's first-loss size is one of the calibrated constants above.

## Where this sits in the roadmap

FULL vs PARTIAL is the collateral leverage dial, and it is explicitly a
**roadmap** item: FULL is the only launch mode and the no-bad-debt headline; PARTIAL is
capital-efficient, carries real bad-debt risk, and is gated on the quant with every
constant from the calibrated risk-parameter set. The enum ships
(`CollateralModel{FULL, PARTIAL}`), the wiring ships, and the gate stays closed until the
calibration lands.
