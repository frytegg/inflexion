`createSwapRouted` is the entry point an LP should
almost always use. In a **single transaction** it prices both underwriting rails off
the **same on-chain fair value**, gives the LP the **cheaper of the two**, and — this
is the load-bearing property — **falls back to the always-on pool whenever the MM quote
is not strictly better or not usable, without ever reverting on the MM's account.**

It is the concrete expression of the [floor/ceiling framing](/concepts/two-rails): the
[cvAMM pool (Path A)](/protocol/path-a-cvamm) is the **floor of liquidity** — always a
code-capped price — and the [MM rail (Path B)](/protocol/path-b-mm) is the **ceiling of
price** — a pure price-improvement layer that can only ever undercut the floor, never
degrade it. The router is what wires those two facts together for the LP.

## The one-line decision

The entire routing rule is one boolean:

```solidity
bool useB = usableB && premiumB < premiumA;   // strict-beat; tie → Path A
```

Everything else on this page is the machinery that makes those three sub-expressions
— `usableB`, `premiumB`, and `premiumA` — **honest, comparable, and side-effect-free
until exactly one rail is executed.**

<Note>
The router emits `SwapRouted(swapId, pathB, premiumA, premiumB)`
on every fill. The chosen rail, and **both** candidate
prices it was choosing between, are written to the chain — the cheapest-wins decision
is fully auditable after the fact, and feeds the [pool-vs-MM spread signal](/moat/five-signals)
of the data moat.
</Note>

## Step 1 — both rails priced off the *same* FairPremium

A fair comparison requires a common baseline. The router computes **one**
[`FairPremium`](/concepts/fair-value) for the position and prices both rails on top of
it, so the only thing that differs between `premiumA` and `premiumB` is the **load**,
never the fair value underneath.

$$
\text{FairPremium} = \text{fairRate}\cdot\text{MaxIL},
\qquad
\text{fairRate} = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[\min(IL,\,\text{MaxIL})\right]}{\text{MaxIL}}
$$

The shared read prologue (`_prepareSwap`) and the
fair-value read (`_fairPremium`) run once; the
[VolOracle is poked exactly once](/protocol/oracles) to refresh `σ_ref`, and both rails
read the result. The two candidate premiums are then the
same `FairPremium` with two different loads layered on:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{premium}_A &= \big\lceil\,\text{FairPremium}\cdot\big(1 + \min(\underbrace{\text{baseLoad} + \text{util\_skew} + \text{disp\_skew}}_{\text{totalLoad}},\ \text{maxLoad})\big)\big\rceil \\[4pt]
\text{premium}_B &= \big\lceil\,\text{FairPremium}\cdot\big(1 + \text{loadBps}/10^4\big)\big\rceil,\qquad \text{loadBps} \le \text{maxLoadBps}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both are additionally capped at `MaxIL` (never charge more than the maximum possible
payout), and the Path-A load sum is hard-clamped
by [invariant I10](/security/invariants), `min(..., maxLoad)`, **by construction in
code**. Path A is computed by `_pricePathAFromFair`; Path B's price comes back from the
usability mirror described next. See [the pricing stack](/protocol/pricing-stack) and
[the load stack](/math/load-stack) for how each load is built.

<Tip>
Pricing both rails off the same `FairPremium` is also what makes the
[clearing-load and pool-vs-MM spread signals](/moat/five-signals) non-circular: the
fair value is a fixed, published reference, so the *load* the market clears at is an
observable behavioral quantity, not a back-solved artifact.
</Tip>

## Step 2 — `usableB`: the non-reverting mirror of Path B

This is the heart of the routing safety story. The router does **not** call the
reverting Path-B gate. It calls `_quoteUsableAndPremiumB`,
a **`view` function** that mirrors *every* Path-B check and returns `(usableB, premiumB)`
— `(false, 0)` instead of reverting whenever any check fails.

It returns `(false, 0)` if the MM quote is any of:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Absent, wrong-model, or dust" icon="circle-xmark">
    No quote supplied, `model != FULL`, or the position/premium fails the
    `MIN_POSITION_V0 = $100` / `MIN_PREMIUM = $1` dust floors.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Over-load (I10)" icon="circle-xmark">
    `loadBps > maxLoadBps` — the same I10 ceiling the reverting path enforces, evaluated
    as a boolean here instead of a revert.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Expired or out-of-validity-band" icon="circle-xmark">
    `validUntil <= now`, or `secondsAhead` outside the `[5s, 15s]` window.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Bad price band or out-of-oracle-band (I9)" icon="circle-xmark">
    `priceBandBps` outside `[25, 500]`, or the live oracle has drifted beyond the band:
    `absBps(P_live, quotePrice) > priceBandBps` — see [price pinning](/security/price-pinning).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Out-of-ratio, over-capacity, or nonce-used (I7)" icon="circle-xmark">
    The LP's `ratioBps = maxIL·1e4/V0` outside the quote's MaxIL-ratio band;
    `consumedNotional[quoteId] + V0 > maxNotionalV0`; or the bitmap nonce already used.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Undercollateralized, zero-price, or bad signature" icon="circle-xmark">
    `availableBalance(mm) < maxIL`; a zero `quotePrice`; or the EIP-712 / EIP-1271
    signature failing verification (checked via STATICCALL).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

The critical property: `_quoteUsableAndPremiumB` is **`view`**. It *reads*
`isNonceUsed`, `consumedNotional`, `availableBalance`, and the signature (via
STATICCALL), but it **never calls `_useNonce`, never increments `consumedNotional`, and
never locks collateral**. A quote that is evaluated but **not chosen** mutates no
state whatsoever — it leaves **zero trace**.

<Warning>
**A bad / stale / absent MM quote falls back to the pool — it never reverts the LP's
transaction.**

- **Limit:** an MM quote passed to `createSwapRouted` can be absent, expired, stale,
  out-of-band, over-load, over-capacity, undercollateralized, zero-priced,
  wrong-model, or simply not cheaper than the pool.
- **Why it cannot harm the LP:** the router evaluates the MM quote through a read-only
  mirror (`_quoteUsableAndPremiumB`, `view`), so any failing condition merely sets
  `usableB = false` — it cannot revert and cannot mutate state.
- **Mitigation (by construction):** the swap silently routes to the always-on
  [cvAMM pool](/protocol/path-a-cvamm). Only **protocol-level** failures revert (see
  below). An LP using `createSwapRouted` is therefore **never worse off** than calling
  [`createSwapPathA`](/protocol/path-a-cvamm) directly.
</Warning>

## Step 3 — the route: strict-beat, ties to Path A

```solidity
bool useB = usableB && premiumB < premiumA;   // strict-beat; tie → Path A
```

Two design choices are deliberate and load-bearing:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Strict-beat (< not ≤)">
    The MM wins **only if it strictly undercuts** the pool. This is what makes the MM a
    genuine price-improvement layer: it captures flow only when it offers the LP a real
    saving, not when it merely matches.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ties resolve to Path A">
    When `premiumB == premiumA`, `useB` is `false` and the swap goes to the **pool** —
    the dependency-free, always-on rail. An MM **cannot divert flow by matching** the
    pool's price; it must beat it. This protects the floor: the rail with no relayer,
    no validity clock, and no off-chain dependency is the default.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The result is the LP always pays `min(premiumA, premiumB_if_usable)` and is *always*
served — the pool floor guarantees a fillable price exists, and the MM ceiling can only
push the LP's cost down.

## Step 4 — only the executed rail mutates state (I7 preserved)

After the boolean resolves, **exactly one** rail executes, and **only that rail mutates
nonce, capacity, or lock**:

<CodeGroup>
```solidity useB == true (Path B)
// _executePathB — the ONLY place Path-B state mutates
consumedNotional[quote.quoteId] += g.V0;     // I7 capacity, atomic, before any call
_useNonce(quote.mm, quote.nonce);            // I7 bitmap nonce consumed
underwriterVault.lockCollateral(quote.mm, g.maxIL);
// write SwapRecord{ mm: quote.mm, ... }; pull premium; take NFT; split 99% MM / 1% treasury
```

```solidity useB == false (Path A)
// _executePathA — pool rail
convexityVault.lockCollateral(marketId, g.maxIL);   // free + senior-protection check
// write SwapRecord{ mm: address(convexityVault), ... }; pull premium; take NFT
// split 99% into pool (tranche split via accruePremium) / 1% treasury
```
</CodeGroup>

This is the router-level statement of **[invariant I7 — capacity authority](/security/invariants)**:

> `consumedNotional[quoteId] ≤ maxNotionalV0`, and a cancelled bitmap-nonce bit can
> never fill.

Because the losing rail's usability is evaluated through a `view` mirror, a Path-B quote
that loses the route consumes **no capacity**, burns **no nonce**, and locks **no
collateral**. An MM's quote cannot be silently drained by an LP who ends up routing to
the pool — the on-chain ledger only ever moves for the rail that actually filled.

<Info>
On Path A, the lock additionally passes the pool's structural checks — `free ≥ amount`
and the senior-protection invariant `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`. So even the fallback rail enforces
[depositor-protection](/concepts/tranches) at lock time; the router never bypasses it.
</Info>

## What *does* revert — and why those are correct

The router does not revert on the MM. It **does** revert on **protocol-level**
failures, because those are not "the MM quote was bad" — they are "this swap cannot
exist at all":

| Revert condition | Why it is a hard failure, not a fallback |
| --- | --- |
| Pool not wired / market not `cvammEnabled` | There is **no floor to fall back to** — the always-on rail is the precondition of the whole router. |
| Not the position owner (`NotPositionOwner`) | The caller does not own the NFT being hedged — no rail can proceed. |
| Market mismatch (`MarketMismatch`) | The position's `(token0, token1, fee)` does not match the requested `marketId`. |
| Position out of range (`PositionOutOfRange`) | `Pa ≤ P0 ≤ Pb` fails — this is the [in-range convexity hedge](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge) gate; out-of-range entries are rejected on **both** rails. |
| Dust (`MIN_POSITION_V0` / `MIN_PREMIUM`) | A protocol-wide floor that applies regardless of rail. |
| Slippage (`PremiumExceedsSlippage`) | The cheaper of the two premiums still exceeds the LP's own `maxPremium` guard. |

In every one of these cases, the swap is invalid for *both* rails — so reverting is the
correct, honest outcome. The router's promise is narrow and precise: **a bad MM quote
never reverts; an invalid swap always does.**

## The LP-always-served guarantee, stated precisely

Putting the pieces together, `createSwapRouted` gives the LP a guarantee that no
single-counterparty design can:

<Steps>
  <Step title="A fillable price always exists">
    The pool is the always-on floor. Whenever the swap is valid at all (owner, in-range,
    market wired, above dust), the pool can fill it at a [code-capped (I10) price](/security/invariants).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The MM can only improve it">
    The MM rail is a read-only price-improvement layer. It wins only by strictly beating
    the floor; it can never make the LP pay more, and a tie keeps the LP on the pool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A misbehaving MM is invisible to the LP">
    Stale, leaked, undercollateralized, or absent MM quotes degrade to the pool with no
    revert and no state change. The LP's experience is identical to the MM not existing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The settlement core is the same either way">
    Whichever rail filled, `settle` is the [single untouched core](/protocol/lifecycle):
    pin the Chainlink round-at-T, compute IL with the **stored** `L` (I6), pay
    `min(realized_IL, MaxIL)` (I1 + I2) from the rail's own vault, return the NFT.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This is why the [no-bad-debt guarantee is independent of how the quote was
discovered](/concepts/no-bad-debt) — routing decides *who underwrites and at what
price*, never *whether the LP is paid*. That is enforced downstream at settlement
against the chosen counterparty's own collateral.

## Worked example — live on Arbitrum Sepolia

The 2026-06-05 redeploy ran a real routed create→settle against one 300s market:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Routed swap #2 — MM won">
    `createSwapRouted` priced both rails off one `FairPremium`:

    - `premiumA_cvamm` = **\$13.80** (pool, with its on-chain load stack)
    - `premiumB_mm` = **\$8.93** (MM, `loadBps` 1000)

    Since `usableB == true` and `8.93 < 13.80`, `useB` was `true` → **the MM won**.
    `MaxIL $3,215.65` was locked from the **MM's own** `UnderwriterVault` collateral,
    realized IL came to **\$245.66**, payout **\$245.66**, settled from the MM's
    collateral. The pool's nonce/capacity were untouched — only the executed rail moved.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Path A swap #1 — pool baseline">
    For contrast, swap #1 went straight to the pool via
    [`createSwapPathA`](/protocol/path-a-cvamm): V0 \$270,531.28, MaxIL \$1,669.24, premium
    **\$9.70 (0.58% of MaxIL)**, realized IL \$148.64, payout \$148.64 — settled from the
    `ConvexityVault`. Same settlement core, pool collateral.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

The `SwapRouted` event on swap #2 carries `(premiumA = 13.80, premiumB = 8.93, pathB =
true)` — the entire routing decision, both candidate prices included, is on-chain and
auditable.
