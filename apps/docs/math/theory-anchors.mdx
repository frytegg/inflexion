Inflexion does not invent a pricing theory. It *applies* two established results to a v3 LP position and then prices the exact, capped payoff with a closed-form Φ-sum. This page covers the two anchors, the short-gamma chain that ties them to the IL claim, and — emphatically — why they validate the approach but are **not** the on-chain pricer.

<Note>
**One-line takeaway.** The literature tells us an LP is short gamma and that its loss is the realization of that short-gamma cost. That is *why* the claim is priceable and hedgeable. The *price itself* comes from [the exact Φ-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum) — `E_Q[min(IL, MaxIL)]` evaluated live per quote — not from these papers. Anchors justify; the Φ-sum computes.
</Note>

## The chain in one diagram

The whole intellectual argument is a five-link chain. Each link is either a property of the v3 value function (proven in [the IL formula](/math/il-formula) and [the MaxIL derivation](/math/maxil-derivation)) or one of the two academic anchors.

<Steps>
  <Step title="V_lp is concave in price">
    Inside the range, the v3 LP value $V_{lp}(P) = L\,(2\sqrt{P} - \sqrt{P_a} - P/\sqrt{P_b})$ is **strictly concave**: $d^2 V_{lp}/dP^2 = -\tfrac14 L\,P^{-3/2} < 0$ for all $P>0$. Holding the entry basket, $V_{hold}(P)$, is affine. Concave value vs. an affine benchmark *is* the source of IL.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Concave value ⇒ the LP is short gamma">
    Negative second derivative of value with respect to price is the definition of negative gamma. A v3 LP is **structurally short gamma** — they have sold optionality to the pool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Short gamma ⇒ the LP pays LVR (MMR 2022)">
    Milionis-Moallemi-Roughgarden show the AMM's adverse-selection cost — *loss-versus-rebalancing* (LVR) — has a closed form **proportional to instantaneous variance**, equivalently the *theta* (time-decay) of the replicating short-option position. Short gamma is paid for in continuous theta bleed; that bleed is LVR.
  </Step>
  <Step title="LVR realized over the position ⇒ IL owed">
    Integrated over the life of the position, that short-gamma / LVR cost is what shows up at settlement as realized impermanent loss, $\text{realized\_IL} = \max(0,\,V_{hold} - V_{lp})$.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inflexion sells the offsetting long-convexity leg (LLS 2025)">
    Lipton-Lucic-Sepp show an IL-protection claim is **statically replicable by a strip of vanilla options** — so it has a model-light fair value and a concrete hedge. Inflexion sells the LP exactly that long-convexity leg, **capped at the in-range maximum** ([MaxIL](/concepts/maxil)), and prices it as the risk-neutral expectation of the capped payoff.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The compressed form:

> $V_{lp}$ concave ⇒ LP short gamma ⇒ pays LVR ⇒ owes IL; Inflexion sells the LP the *long-convexity* leg that offsets it, capped at the in-range maximum (MaxIL), and prices it with the exact risk-neutral expectation of that capped payoff (the Φ-sum).

## Anchor 1 — Lipton, Lucic & Sepp (2025): static replication

**Result.** An IL-protection claim on a concentrated-liquidity position is **statically replicable by a strip of vanilla options**.

**Why it matters for Inflexion.** Static replication is a strong statement: it means the claim's payoff can be reproduced, at expiry, by a fixed portfolio of vanilla calls/puts chosen *once* — no dynamic rebalancing required. Two consequences fall straight out:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Model-light fair value" icon="scale-balanced">
    A statically replicable payoff inherits the option strip's price. Its fair value does not depend on a bespoke, calibrated model — it is the value of a known portfolio of vanillas. This is the licence to publish a *fair value* at all.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A concrete hedge exists" icon="shield-halved">
    Replication is constructive: the same strip an underwriter could *buy* to neutralize the risk it has written. This is what makes the written risk a hedgeable book, not an actuarial bet — and it underpins the [pool hedge lever](/protocol/vaults) on the roadmap.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The v3 LP value function makes the replication tangible. Recall the three regimes (see the [IL formula](/math/il-formula)):

$$
V_{lp}(P) =
\begin{cases}
L\,(1/\sqrt{P_a} - 1/\sqrt{P_b})\cdot P & P < P_a \quad(\text{linear}) \\[4pt]
L\,(2\sqrt{P} - P/\sqrt{P_b} - \sqrt{P_a}) & P_a \le P \le P_b \quad(\text{concave}) \\[4pt]
L\,(\sqrt{P_b} - \sqrt{P_a}) & P > P_b \quad(\text{constant})
\end{cases}
$$

The concave middle arm is precisely a short-option payoff profile (capped on the upside, sloping into both tails), and the kinks at $P_a$ and $P_b$ are where the replicating strip's strikes sit. That this concave value can be written as a portfolio of vanillas is the LLS statement applied to v3.

## Anchor 2 — Milionis, Moallemi & Roughgarden (2022): LVR

**Result.** In *Automated Market Making and Loss-Versus-Rebalancing*, the AMM's adverse-selection cost — **LVR** — has a **closed form proportional to instantaneous variance**, equivalently the *theta* of the replicating short-option position.

**Why it matters for Inflexion.** LVR is the closed-form anchor for the *cost of short-gamma exposure*. Schematically, the instantaneous LVR rate scales with $\sigma^2$ and the position's curvature:

$$
\text{LVR rate} \;\propto\; \tfrac12\,\sigma^2\,P^2\,\big|\,\partial^2 V_{lp}/\partial P^2\,\big|
\;=\; \Theta_{\text{short option}}
$$

This is the precise sense in which a v3 LP "bleeds": holding a concave position through volatility costs theta continuously, and that theta is the LVR. Realized IL is the cumulative realization of this cost over the position's life. The variance-proportionality is also the bridge to the protocol's only stochastic input — the same $\sigma$ that drives LVR is the $\sigma_{ref}$ the [σ_ref oracle](/math/sigma-ref) feeds into the price.

<Tip>
**The two anchors are complementary, not redundant.** MMR gives the *cost* of being short gamma (the theta you bleed). LLS gives the *cure* — a static strip that buys the offsetting long-convexity. Inflexion sells the LP that cure. One paper tells you the disease is priceable; the other tells you it is hedgeable.
</Tip>

## Why "capped" is where Inflexion departs from the textbook

The anchors describe the *uncapped* IL claim. Inflexion does not sell that. It sells $\min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ — the in-range maximum, frozen at creation. The reason is structural, not cosmetic: beyond the range the LP has fully rotated into one asset, so $V_{lp}$ is *constant* while $V_{hold}$ keeps growing linearly, making absolute IL **unbounded** above $P_b$.

$$
\text{covered\_payoff} = \min(\text{realized\_IL},\, \text{MaxIL}), \qquad \text{MaxIL} = \max\big(IL(P_a),\, IL(P_b)\big)
$$

The cap truncates the replicating strip at the range boundaries. Everything *inside* the range is the convexity hedge the anchors describe; everything beyond it is **directional** loss (foregone spot upside), not the *impermanent* loss the LP set out to hedge. The cap is what makes the product fully collateralized and trustless — it *is* the [no-bad-debt guarantee](/concepts/no-bad-debt), not a defect. This is why Inflexion is an [in-range convexity hedge](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge), **not "IL insurance."**

## The anchors validate the approach — they are NOT the pricer

This is the load-bearing distinction on this page. State it cleanly.

<Warning>
**The papers justify *that* the claim is priceable. They do not *set the price*.** The on-chain price is computed by the exact closed-form Φ-sum — `FairPremium = E_Q[min(IL, MaxIL)]` — evaluated live per quote in the Stylus [FairValueOracle](/protocol/oracles). No coefficient in that formula is fitted to LLS or MMR; the anchors are *intellectual provenance*, not calibration inputs.

- **Limit:** the anchors are continuous-time, asymptotic, *uncapped* results — they describe the family of the claim, not the discrete capped payoff Inflexion actually settles.
- **Why:** Inflexion settles $\min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ in integer Q64.96 math at a single expiry, against a price the [oracle](/protocol/oracles) reports — not a continuous-time integral of an uncapped claim.
- **Mitigation:** the protocol prices the *exact* capped payoff directly. The capped v3 payoff is piecewise-linear/convex in the terminal price, so under risk-neutral GBM each arm integrates to a standard lognormal interval moment in the normal CDF $\Phi$. The result is a finite Φ-sum (≈6–10 terms, Black–Scholes class), verified ≡ the reference IL implementation to `max_abs_err ≈ 5.1e-11` — **no Monte Carlo, no lookup table, no fitted coefficients.** See [the Φ-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum).
</Warning>

### The only residual modeling assumption

The Φ-sum is *exact given the model*. The single modeling assumption is risk-neutral **GBM with $\mu = 0,\ r = 0$** (`measure: risk_neutral_gbm_mu0_r0`). The anchors do not remove this; nothing on-chain does. It is covered two ways:

  <Accordion title="1. The conservative σ_ref">
    The vol oracle publishes $\sigma_{ref} = \max(\sigma_{short}, \sigma_{long}, \text{floor})$ with a floor of `0.50` sitting *above* observed calm ETH vol (`~0.45`), so the pool is net-conservative to depositors. A wrong/too-low $\sigma$ can compress NAV but **can never create LP bad debt in FULL** — see [σ_ref](/math/sigma-ref) and [no-bad-debt math](/math/no-bad-debt-math).

  <Accordion title="2. Forward-vol premium is left as MM alpha">
    The residual forward-looking-vol premium — the gap between backward-looking realized vol and implied — is deliberately left as **market-maker alpha**. Forward-vol MMs price off implied vol and correct the pool's structural backward-looking bias. See [Path B — MM](/protocol/path-b-mm).
