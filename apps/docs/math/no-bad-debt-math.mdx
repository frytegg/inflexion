Inflexion's no-bad-debt guarantee is not a calibration result, a buffer, or a
heuristic. In FULL mode it is **three lines of algebra** that hold for *any* price
path and *any* — even wrong — volatility input. This page derives those three lines,
proves they are orthogonal to the premium and to every pricing oracle, extends the
same algebra to the dual-tranche waterfall that protects senior depositors, and shows
the heavy-run verification that `max_payout_frac = 1.000000` on every simulated path.

<ClaimA />

<Note>
**The whole proof in one sentence.** Collateral is locked at `MaxIL`; the payout is
[capped](/concepts/maxil) at `MaxIL`; therefore `payout ≤ collateral` by
construction — and nothing about *how* the premium was priced, or *what* the oracle
said, can change that.
</Note>

## 1. The three-line structural core

The settle-path guarantee, stated exactly as the code enforces it:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{collateral}_{\text{FULL}} &= \text{MaxIL}
   && \text{(locked at creation)} \\[2pt]
\text{covered\_payoff} &= \min(\text{realized\_IL},\, \text{MaxIL}) \;\le\; \text{MaxIL}
   && \text{(by construction of the cap)} \\[2pt]
\therefore\quad \text{payout} &\le \text{collateral} = \text{MaxIL}
   && \text{(no bad debt — invariants I1, I2)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Read line by line, this is the entire argument:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Collateral is locked at exactly MaxIL">
    At swap creation the underwriter posts collateral equal to the position's
    [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) — the boundary impermanent loss,
    $\text{MaxIL} = \max\big(IL(P_a),\, IL(P_b)\big)$, which is **pure geometry,
    frozen at creation**. Path A locks it from the pool; Path B locks the MM's own
    collateral. In FULL mode `collateral == MaxIL` exactly — not a
    multiple, not a fraction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The payout is capped at MaxIL">
    At `settle`, the protocol recomputes `realized_IL` on-chain from the
    Chainlink-pinned settlement price and applies the cap:

    ```solidity
    uint128 payout = realisedIL > s.maxIL ? s.maxIL : uint128(realisedIL);
    ```

    That ternary **is** the $\min$. The covered payoff is
    $\min(\text{realized\_IL},\, \text{MaxIL})$, so it can never exceed `MaxIL`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Therefore payout ≤ collateral">
    Combine the two: `payout ≤ MaxIL == collateral`. There is no price path — in
    range, below $P_a$, above $P_b$, or in the no-IL region — on which the payout can
    exceed the locked collateral. The underwriter's capital always covers the claim,
    with a non-negative residual returned at settle.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The two invariants this nails down:

- **I1 — No bad debt (FULL):** $\text{payout} \le \text{collateral} = \text{MaxIL}$.
- **I2 — Cap correctness:** $\text{payout} = \min(\text{realized\_IL},\, \text{MaxIL})$.

Both are enforced in `InflexionCore` and verified by a fuzz test
(`testFuzz_I1_I2_payoutCappedAtMaxIL`, 256 runs). Both vaults additionally re-check
`payout ≤ lockedAmount` as defense-in-depth.

<Warning>
**The cap is the guarantee — removing it breaks everything.** Above $P_b$ the LP has
rotated fully into token1, so $V_{\text{lp}}$ is **constant** while $V_{\text{hold}}$
keeps growing linearly with price — absolute IL is **unbounded** beyond the range.
**Why this is not a defect:** IL beyond the range boundary is
*directional* loss (foregone spot upside), not the *impermanent* loss the LP set out
to hedge. **Mitigation:** capping at MaxIL keeps the product fully collateralized and
trustless — which is exactly why Inflexion is an
[in-range convexity hedge](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge), **not "IL
insurance."** No fixed collateral could ever back an unbounded payoff.
</Warning>

## 2. Why the guarantee is orthogonal to the premium

The three lines above contain **no premium term, no load, no fairRate, and no
$\sigma$.** That is deliberate, and it is the single most important structural fact
about the protocol.

The premium is computed *upstream* of settle, in a completely separate phase (the
four-phase CEI structure):

$$
\text{premium} \;=\; \underbrace{\text{fairRate}\cdot\text{MaxIL}}_{\text{FairPremium}}\;\cdot\;\big(1 + \min(\text{baseLoad}+\text{util}+\text{disp},\ \text{maxLoad})\big)
$$

This sits in **PHASE 1 (pricing, READ)** and **PHASE 2 (checks)**. The no-bad-debt
cap lives in `settle`, which runs only after expiry. The
[invariant I10 price cap](/security/invariants) — overcharge is impossible by
construction — is *also* purely upstream:

<Info>
**I10 orthogonality.** The load clamp
$\text{total} = \min(\text{baseLoad}+\text{util}+\text{disp},\ \text{maxLoad})$ is a
mechanical deterministic clamp, so
$\text{premium} \le \text{FairPremium}\cdot(1+\text{maxLoad})$ holds for **all**
inputs on **both** paths. But I10 lives in pricing, strictly **upstream of `settle`**
— it does **not** touch `settle`, the MaxIL formula, or I1–I9. A mispriced premium is
an economic outcome for the *underwriter* (NAV), never an LP bad-debt event.
</Info>

The consequence is sharp: even if the premium were charged at **zero**, or at ten
times fair value, the LP would still be paid exactly $\min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ and the underwriter's `MaxIL` of collateral would still cover it. The
premium decides **whether underwriting is profitable**; it has **nothing to do with
whether the LP gets paid.** Those are two separate questions, answered by two
separate parts of the contract, in two separate phases.

## 3. Why the guarantee is oracle-independent

The same observation, sharpened to its most-probed corner. The no-bad-debt proof is
*structural* and holds **regardless of what any oracle said** — including a wrong,
stale, or maliciously-set one.

Walk the oracle dependencies:

| Oracle | What it feeds | Load-bearing for I1 (no bad debt)? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FairValueOracle` (Stylus Φ-sum) | `FairPremium` → the premium | **No** — pricing only, upstream of settle |
| `VolOracle` ($\sigma_{\text{ref}}$) | the load regime band | **No** — pricing only, upstream of settle |
| `OracleManager` (Chainlink round-at-T) | the settlement price $P_T$, hence `realized_IL` | Indirectly: needed for *liveness*, not for the *bound* |

The first two never enter `settle` at all. `FairPremium` and $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$
are solvency-load-bearing for **the I10 cap and depositor solvency only** — a wrong
$\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ makes the pool *under-charge load*, compressing NAV — but they
are **NOT** load-bearing for the FULL no-bad-debt invariant. In FULL, collateral =
MaxIL ≥ payout *regardless of what $\sigma$ was*.

The third — the Chainlink round-at-T — sets the settlement price, so it determines
*the value* of `realized_IL`. But the cap is applied **after** that value is
computed:

$$
\text{payout} \;=\; \min\big(\text{realized\_IL}(\underbrace{P_T}_{\text{any value}}),\ \text{MaxIL}\big)\;\le\;\text{MaxIL}.
$$

For **any** $P_T$ whatsoever — correct, glitched, or adversarial — the right-hand
side is $\le \text{MaxIL}$. The cap is the outermost operation, so the bound is
immune to what feeds it. The settlement price affects *how much* the LP is paid (up
to the cap), never *whether collateral covers it*.

<Tip>
**The crucial split, never merge it.** I1 (no bad debt) is structural and
oracle-independent. The oracles are load-bearing only for I10 and depositor solvency.
A vol-oracle fault can cost depositors money but can **never** create LP bad debt in
FULL. The heavy run makes this concrete: a lagging $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ underprices
coverage written at a stress *onset* (payout/premium = 1.14 at onset), and that
residual is a vol-risk-premium the `baseLoad` must carry — **not** a no-bad-debt risk.
See [σ_ref](/math/sigma-ref) for the precise load-bearing scope.
</Tip>

### What the cap depends on, exactly

To be precise about what *is* required for I1, separate from what is not:

- **Required (structural, by code):** the cap operation itself; collateral locked at
  MaxIL at creation; `L` stored once and never re-read (invariant I6 — an external
  `increaseLiquidity` on the custodied NFT cannot inflate the computed IL); the guarded
  subtraction $\text{realized\_IL} = V_{\text{hold}} > V_{\text{lp}} ?\ V_{\text{hold}} - V_{\text{lp}} : 0$ (invariant I3).
- **Required for the full clause (the qualifiers, not the bound):** capped payoff,
  solvent USDC, oracle/settlement *liveness* (so `settle` can run — see
  [the forks](/security/forks) and invariant I8), and no rehypothecation breach
  (locked collateral stays instantly liquid).
- **NOT required:** a correct premium, a correct `fairRate`, a correct
  $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$, or even a correct settlement price for the *bound* to hold
  (only for the *amount* within the bound).

This is why the static-analysis pass could confirm that "the FULL no-bad-debt
guarantee (I1) is structural and independent of the FairValue/Vol oracles" — `settle`
caps payout at `MaxIL == collateral`.

## 4. The dual-tranche waterfall math

I1 guarantees **LPs are always paid**. It says nothing about whether the *underwriter*
loses money — and on the pooled cvAMM rail (Path A), the underwriter is the
[two-tranche vault](/concepts/tranches). The same `payout ≤ locked` algebra extends
to a second structural invariant that protects senior depositors.

<ClaimB />

### 4.1 The senior-protection invariant

The structural senior protection is the invariant **`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`**,
enforced at **every** `lockCollateral` (the `SeniorProtectionBreached` revert). Chain
it with the per-swap no-bad-debt bound:

$$
\underbrace{\sum_i \text{payout}_i \;\le\; \sum_i \text{MaxIL}_i \;=\; \text{totalLocked}}_{\text{I1, summed over open swaps}}\;\le\;\underbrace{\text{juniorAssets}}_{\text{senior-protection invariant}}
$$

so

$$
\sum_i \text{payout}_i \;\le\; \text{totalLocked} \;\le\; \text{juniorAssets}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{junior absorbs all underwriting loss before senior is ever touched.}
$$

The first inequality is just I1 summed over every open swap — each payout is bounded
by its own locked MaxIL, so the total payout is bounded by `totalLocked`. The second
is the vault invariant. Together they mean the **junior-first-loss waterfall absorbs
all underwriting loss before senior capital is reached.**

### 4.2 The waterfall in code

`releaseAndDistribute` takes the junior loss first and only then any senior loss:

$$
\text{juniorLoss} = \min(\text{payout},\ \text{juniorAssets}),
\qquad
\text{seniorLoss} = \text{payout} - \text{juniorLoss}.
$$

By the invariant above, $\text{payout} \le \text{totalLocked} \le \text{juniorAssets}$, so $\text{juniorLoss} = \text{payout}$ and
$\text{seniorLoss} = 0$ at every settle. The $\min$ is therefore **defense-in-depth**
— under the invariant, senior is never reached.

### 4.3 The algebraic link to `u ≤ 1 − sf`

This is the **adaptive code form** of the "enforce $u \le 1 - sf$" rule, keyed to the
*actual* junior buffer rather than a fixed ratio (which is safer). The algebra: with
target senior fraction $sf = 0.60$, the junior fraction is $1 - sf = 0.40$, and on
utilization $u = \text{totalLocked}/\text{totalAssets}$,

$$
\text{totalLocked} \le \text{juniorAssets}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u \le 0.40 = 1 - sf.
$$

That is exactly the utilization bound under which the P1.13 senior-`P(loss) = 0`
calibration holds.

<Warning>
**Senior protection is calibrated, not unconditional.** **Limit:** `P(senior loss) =
0` holds only while $u \le 1 - sf$, and never against systemic failure (USDC depeg,
oracle failure, contract bug). **Why:** the bound `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`
guarantees underwriting losses are absorbed by junior, but it cannot guarantee
against an exogenous failure of the collateral asset or the chain. **Mitigation:**
$sf = 0.60$ is the *target* tranche ratio for UX/incentives, **not** the hard cap —
the hard, always-enforced constraint is the adaptive `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`
check at every lock, plus a withdrawal cooldown and a `JuniorBelowLocked` revert that
forbids drawing junior below `totalLocked`. Capital-not-guaranteed remains the umbrella
for **both** tranches.
</Warning>

### 4.4 The two claims, side by side

The math gives you two **distinct** guarantees, and they are **never merged**:

| | Claim A — LP no bad debt | Claim B — depositor / MM capital risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Statement** | LPs are always paid `min(IL, MaxIL)` in FULL | Capital is **not** guaranteed for either tranche |
| **Status** | Structural, code-enforced (I1), oracle-independent | Calibrated; junior is first-loss, senior takes the systemic tail |
| **Depends on** | The cap + the full clause (capped payoff, solvent USDC, liveness, no rehypothecation) | Realized vs. priced vol; utilization; the tranche buffer |
| **Failure mode** | Cannot fail in FULL under the clause | Depositor NAV can compress; junior worst ≈ −67% in the heavy run |
| **Code** | `InflexionCore` cap; vault re-checks | `ConvexityVault` senior-protection + waterfall |

This is also why Inflexion is **not Bancor**: the cvAMM pays claims in
**pre-locked USDC and mints nothing** — no token-inflation reinsurance, no death
spiral. LPs are always paid (Claim A); depositors can lose principal in a crash
(Claim B). Two claims, never one.

## 5. P1.13 verification — `max_payout_frac = 1.0`

The structural argument is airtight on paper. The P1.13 heavy run confirms it
empirically across an extreme price grid, and — crucially — independently of any
oracle.

Define the payout fraction as the payout normalized by its own collateral:

$$
\text{payout\_frac} \;=\; \frac{\min(\text{realized\_IL},\ \text{MaxIL})}{\text{MaxIL}}.
$$

If I1 holds for every path, this fraction must satisfy
$\text{payout\_frac} \le 1.0$ everywhere — the payout never exceeds the collateral.

**Result:**

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="max_payout_frac" icon="equals">
    **1.000000** — the cap binds exactly at the boundary and never exceeds it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Exceptions" icon="circle-check">
    **Zero.** No simulated path produced `payout > MaxIL`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Grid" icon="chart-line">
    A **±99%** terminal-price grid, plus a widened **−6…+6 log-move** grid.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

`max_payout_frac = 1.000000` across that grid, with **zero exceptions**,
**structurally and independent of any oracle.** The fraction reaches exactly 1.0
because for any path that drives `realized_IL ≥ MaxIL`, the cap binds and the payout
*equals* MaxIL — never more. This is the numerical fingerprint of "the cap is the
outermost operation."

The heavy-run model is deliberately **stronger than GBM** — the bar the protocol set
for itself:

- Student-t fat tails ($t_{df} = 4$),
- a 3-state Markov vol regime (calm 0.45 / normal 0.75 / stressed 1.30, persistence
  0.82),
- tail-dependent crash correlation → 1 (single pair, the whole book settles against
  the same move),
- Poisson deep-crash months (~0.8/yr, mean −45%),
- and a **lagging $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$** so coverage at a stress onset is underpriced
  exactly as it would be on-chain.

That the no-bad-debt fraction stays pinned at 1.0 under a model this hostile is the
point: I1 is structural, so **no stochastic model can break it** — a fat-tailed,
regime-switching, crash-correlated world produces the same bound as a calm one,
because the bound is algebraic, not statistical.

<Info>
**The adversarial self-audit verdict was GO:** a 6-agent workflow independently
re-ran the engine and confirmed the no-bad-debt
structural result (`max_payout_frac = 1.0`), that the measure split is correct
(premium risk-neutral at $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$, payout under the real measure), and
that the model is in fact **net-conservative to depositors** — the $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$
floor of 0.50 sits above calm ETH vol (~0.45), so the pool overcharges the ~27% of
calm months; all-months realized payout/premium = **0.654**.
</Info>

## 6. The invariant set, in one place

The settle-path invariants that make the three-line core hold at the formula level:

| Invariant | Statement | Where enforced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I1** | No bad debt (FULL): `payout ≤ collateral == MaxIL` | `InflexionCore` cap |
| **I2** | Cap correctness: `payout == min(realized_IL, MaxIL)` | `InflexionCore` cap |
| **I3** | Non-negativity / no underflow: guarded subtraction, never unchecked | `ILMath` |
| **I4** | LP never profits: `V_lp ≥ V_hold ⟹ payout == 0` | `ILMath` (the `: 0` arm) |
| **I5** | Vault solvency: `locked ≤ deposited` per MM and per pool | `UnderwriterVault`, `ConvexityVault` |
| **I6** | Liquidity immutability: settle uses the `L` stored at creation | `InflexionCore` |
| **I10** | Price cap (by construction, upstream of settle): `premium ≤ FairPremium·(1 + maxLoadBps)` | `CvammPricing` |
| **Pool** | Senior protection: `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` at every lock | `ConvexityVault` |

Note the asymmetry: **I1 depends on oracle/settlement liveness** (so `settle` can
run within its window), while **I10 is always-true by code** (a pure pricing clamp
with no liveness dependency). They live in different phases and answer different
questions — never conflate them. Full statements, the "why each holds," and the
fuzz-test citations are on [the invariants page](/security/invariants).
