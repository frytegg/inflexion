Everything Inflexion charges an LP factors into **four layered pieces** that multiply
into a single premium. They are deliberately orthogonal — each one isolates a
different kind of information — and that separation is what lets the protocol publish
an **exact, closed-form** price on-chain instead of a fitted curve.

<Note>
**One-line mental model.** MaxIL is pure geometry (the *how much capital*).
[`fairRate`](/math/fair-value-phi-sum) is pure stochastics (the *how risky*).
The [load stack](/math/load-stack) is pure inventory state (the *how scarce*). They
multiply:

$$
\text{premium} \;=\; \underbrace{\text{fairRate}}_{\text{how risky}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\text{MaxIL}}_{\text{how much capital}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{(1 + \text{load})}_{\text{how scarce}}
$$

`fairRate · MaxIL` is the [FairValue](/concepts/fair-value) published on-chain;
`(1 + load)` is the underwriter's charge over fair value, hard-capped by
[invariant I10](/security/invariants).
</Note>

## How the layers stack

Each layer carries exactly one kind of dependence — and *only* that kind. That is
the whole design: it lets the protocol publish three prices for one position by
moving a single dial.

| Layer | Formula | Depends on | Carries |
| ----- | ------- | ---------- | ------- |
| **IL** | $\max(0,\; V_{\text{hold}} - V_{\text{lp}})$ | $P_a, P_b, L, P_0, P_T$ | the realized settle quantity |
| **MaxIL** | $\max\big(IL(P_a),\, IL(P_b)\big)$ | geometry only ($P_a, P_b, L, P_0$) | *how much capital* |
| **fairRate** | $E_Q[\min(IL, \text{MaxIL})]\,/\,\text{MaxIL}$ | $a{=}P_a/P_0,\, b{=}P_b/P_0,\, \sigma_{\text{ref}},\, T$ | *how risky* |
| **FairPremium** | $\text{fairRate}\cdot \text{MaxIL}$ | the above | the published fair value |
| **load** | $\min(\text{baseLoad}+\text{util}+\text{disp},\; \text{maxLoad})$ | inventory state | *how scarce* |
| **premium** | $\text{FairPremium}\cdot(1+\text{load})$ | all of the above | the LP's actual charge |

Read top to bottom and the orthogonality is the point:

- **MaxIL carries no vol and no time.** It is pure geometry, frozen at creation,
  and *identical across the three durations* (7/30/90d) for a given position. It is
  also `L`-independent in the fair-rate sense — `fairRate` depends only on the
  *ratios* $a = P_a/P_0$, $b = P_b/P_0$, plus $\sigma$ and $T$, never on `L`. That
  is exactly what makes positions [fungible to an underwriter](/concepts/maxil): an
  MM quote is **per-market** (a load + an optional MaxIL-ratio band + capacity),
  **never per-NFT**.
- **`fairRate` carries *all* the vol/time dependence.** It is the
  [S-curve in $\sigma^2 T$](/math/fair-value-phi-sum) — the *fraction of MaxIL the
  claim is worth* under the risk-neutral measure. So one position has **one MaxIL**
  but **three FairPremiums** (7/30/90d); only `fairRate` moves between them.
- **The load carries only inventory state** — vol regime, utilization,
  concentration. It never touches `settle()`, the MaxIL formula, or the no-bad-debt
  invariants. It is bounded by code (I10).

<Tip>
The factorization `FairPremium = fairRate · MaxIL` is the single most important
structural choice in the protocol. Because **MaxIL carries none** of the vol/time
dependence, the cvAMM can publish three durations for the same position by computing
the geometry **once** and evaluating only the cheap S-curve three times — and a
market maker can quote a whole market with a single load number, because every
position in it is normalized by its own MaxIL.
</Tip>

## A worked walk-through of the multiply

One position priced end-to-end (from [the load-stack worked example](/math/load-stack);
full numbers on [the worked-example page](/math/worked-example)): a **50,000 USDC**
position, **±10% geometric** range, $\sigma_{\text{ref}} = 60\%$, `baseLoad = +15%`,
skews = 0.

Geometry first — **one MaxIL for all three durations**:

$$
\text{MaxIL} = 1{,}280\ \text{USDC} \quad (2.56\%\ \text{of }V_0)
$$

Then the S-curve does all the work — `fairRate` is the only thing that changes:

| duration | fairRate | FairPremium = fairRate·MaxIL | premium @ +15% load |
| -------- | -------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| 7d  | 44.9% | 574 USDC   | **661 USDC**  (1.32% of V₀) |
| 30d | 70.8% | 906 USDC   | **1,042 USDC** (2.08% of V₀) |
| 90d | 82.9% | 1,061 USDC | **1,221 USDC** (2.44% of V₀) |

Over 90d the ±10% band is touched ~75% of the time versus ~24% over 7d — the S-curve
prices exactly that. The collateral (MaxIL) is constant; only the risk dial moves.

## What's exact, and the one residual assumption

Two of these layers are **exact closed forms**, not approximations:

- **MaxIL** is exact geometry. `IL(P) = V_hold − V_lp` is **convex** on $[P_a, P_b]$
  (affine minus a strictly concave function), so its in-range max is always at an
  endpoint — proven for *any* entry $P_0$, centered or not. See
  [the MaxIL derivation](/math/maxil-derivation).
- **`fairRate`** is an exact finite **Φ-sum** (≈6–10 Black–Scholes-class terms),
  evaluated live per quote. It has **no calibrated coefficients, no Monte Carlo, no
  lookup table** — the capped v3 payoff is piecewise-linear/convex in the terminal
  price, and each arm integrates to a standard lognormal interval moment in the
  normal CDF $\Phi$. See [the Phi-sum page](/math/fair-value-phi-sum).

The single modeling assumption is risk-neutral **GBM with $\mu = 0,\ r = 0$**
(forward = $P_0$). The Φ-sum is exact *given* that model. The residual is covered two
ways: a deliberately **conservative** [$\sigma_{\text{ref}}$](/math/sigma-ref) (an
EWMA vol oracle with a hard floor that sits above calm ETH vol), and the
forward-vol premium left as **MM alpha** — forward-looking MMs price off implied vol
and correct the pool's structural backward-looking bias
([Pillar 3](/concepts/three-pillars)).

The closed form is verified ≡ the reference Python implementation (itself verified ≡ dense
quadrature ≡ Monte Carlo) to `max_abs_err ≈ 5.1e-11`, and the on-chain Stylus Φ-sum
matches 50-digit `mpmath` fixtures to **≤ 1e-12 on fairRate**. The Stylus
erf is integer fixed-point and machine-precise precisely because Stylus **bans WASM
floating point at activation**, which is why the Stylus oracle is production and the
Solidity `FairValueOracle` is only a CI cross-check.

## The two separate guarantees the math gives you

The math underwrites **two distinct claims** — and they must never be merged.

<ClaimA />

The no-bad-debt structural core is three lines, and it is **orthogonal to the
premium** and **oracle-independent** — it holds for any price path and any (even
wrong) $\sigma$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{collateral}_{\text{FULL}} &= \text{MaxIL} && \text{(locked at creation)} \\
\text{covered\_payoff} &= \min(\text{realized\_IL},\, \text{MaxIL}) \le \text{MaxIL} && \text{(by the cap)} \\
\therefore\quad \text{payout} &\le \text{collateral} = \text{MaxIL} && \text{(no bad debt: I1, I2)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The heavy Monte Carlo run confirmed `max_payout_frac = 1.000000` on every simulated path,
over a ±99% (and a widened −6…+6 log-move) grid, with zero exceptions. Full treatment on
[the no-bad-debt math page](/math/no-bad-debt-math).

The **second** claim is a different animal entirely — it is about the underwriter,
not the LP:

<ClaimB />

A wrong or too-low $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ can make the pool *under-charge load*, so
premium income stops covering payouts and NAV compresses — junior absorbs it first,
senior in the tail. That can cost depositors money but can **never** create LP bad
debt in FULL: collateral = MaxIL ≥ payout *regardless of what $\sigma$ was*. The vol
oracle is solvency-load-bearing for **the I10 cap and depositor solvency only**, not
for the FULL no-bad-debt invariant. Two separate guarantees, never merged.
