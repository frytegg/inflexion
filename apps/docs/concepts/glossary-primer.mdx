## The one-sentence frame

Inflexion lets a Uniswap v3 LP pay a fixed upfront **premium** to transfer the **in-range** impermanent-loss risk of a _specific_ position to an underwriter, who posts collateral; at expiry the protocol pays the LP $\min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$, trustlessly, from that collateral. It is an [in-range convexity hedge](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge), not "IL insurance" — the cap is the load-bearing piece, not a footnote.

## Prices and the position

<ParamField path="P" type="price (token0 in token1)">
  The price of the volatile asset in the numéraire — e.g. ETH in USDC. Everything downstream is a function of how `P` moves.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="P0" type="entry price">
  The oracle price at `createSwap` — **not** the LP's original mint price. The hedge covers IL accruing **from creation onward**; any IL borne before that stays the LP's (entry-snapshot semantics).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="P_T" type="settlement price">
  The price at expiry `T`, pinned to the Chainlink round active at `T` to kill settle-timing games. `realized_IL` is measured at `P_T`. See [price pinning](/security/price-pinning).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Pa, Pb" type="range bounds (price)">
  The position's lower (`Pa`) and upper (`Pb`) range bounds. Entry **requires** $P_a \le P_0 \le P_b$ — an out-of-range position is rejected at creation (`PositionOutOfRange`). This is what makes the risk a clean _in-range_ convexity exposure.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="L" type="Uniswap v3 liquidity">
  The liquidity of the position. **Read once at creation and stored** in the `SwapRecord`; settlement uses the stored `L`, never re-read from the NFT — so an external `increaseLiquidity` cannot inflate the payout (invariant [I6](/security/invariants)).
</ParamField>

## The two values that define IL

The whole product hinges on comparing two value paths at expiry.

<ParamField path="V_hold(T)" type="value if you had just held">
  What the entry tokens would be worth at `P_T` if the LP had _not_ provided liquidity: $V_{\text{hold}} = \text{amount0}_{\text{entry}} \cdot P_T + \text{amount1}_{\text{entry}}$. Affine (a straight line) in `P_T`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="V_lp(T)" type="value of the LP position">
  What the actual LP position is worth at `P_T`. Strictly **concave** while in range — and that concavity, sitting under the straight `V_hold` line, is exactly what makes IL convex (and therefore priceable like an option).
</ParamField>

The gap between them is the loss being hedged:

$$
\text{realized\_IL} \;=\; \max\!\big(0,\; V_{\text{hold}}(T) - V_{\text{lp}}(T)\big)
$$

Always computed as `V_hold > V_lp ? V_hold − V_lp : 0` — never an unchecked subtraction, so it can never go negative (invariant [I3](/security/invariants)). If the LP would have been better off in the position, the payout is just zero — [it is a hedge, not a lottery](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge) (invariant I4). See the [IL formula](/math/il-formula) for the full derivation.

<ParamField path="V0" type="position value at entry (numéraire)">
  The position's value in the numéraire (token1) at entry. It is the **capacity unit** — Path B capacity (`maxNotionalV0`) is denominated in `V0`, not collateral — and a dust floor (`MIN_POSITION_V0`) rejects tiny positions.
</ParamField>

## MaxIL — the cap *and* the unit of risk

This is the single most important term on the page.

<ParamField path="MaxIL" type="max in-range IL of this specific position">
  $\text{MaxIL} = \max\big(\text{IL}(P_a),\, \text{IL}(P_b)\big)$ — the largest in-range IL the position can suffer. It is **pure geometry** (computable from `Pa, Pb, L, P0`), **frozen at creation**, and **identical across all three durations**.
</ParamField>

MaxIL wears two hats, both load-bearing:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="The cap" icon="shield-halved">
    `covered_payoff = min(realized_IL, MaxIL)`, and FULL collateral `= MaxIL`. So the payout can never exceed the posted collateral on **any** price path — the no-bad-debt guarantee is structural, not oracle-dependent.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The unit of risk" icon="ruler">
    Pricing as a `% of MaxIL` makes the underwriter's return-on-collateral range-width-independent. That removes adverse selection against wide ranges and makes positions **fungible to an underwriter within a market**.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
MaxIL is a **normalization unit, not a risk metric**. Two positions with identical MaxIL can carry very different risk (distance-to-edge, delta) — so MaxIL alone never sets the price; the geometry does. Full treatment on [the MaxIL page](/concepts/maxil) and [its derivation](/math/maxil-derivation).
</Note>

Reference magnitudes for a geometric-symmetric range:

| Range | MaxIL as % of V0 |
| --- | --- |
| ±5%  | ≈ 1.27% |
| ±10% | ≈ 2.56% |
| ±20% | ≈ 5.23% |
| ±50% | ≈ 13.76% |

## Pricing: fairRate, FairPremium, sigma_ref, load

How the premium gets built, from fair value outward.

<ParamField path="fairRate" type="fraction of MaxIL the claim is worth">
  $\text{fairRate} = \mathbb{E}_{Q}[\min(\text{IL}, \text{MaxIL})] \,/\, \text{MaxIL}$ — the fraction of MaxIL the claim is worth under the risk-neutral measure. An **S-curve in $\sigma^2 T$**: near 0 when calm/short, toward 1 when violent/long. It carries **all** the vol/time dependence; MaxIL carries none. It has an **exact closed form** (no fitted coefficients) — see [the Φ-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="FairPremium" type="fair value of the claim">
  $\text{FairPremium} = \text{fairRate} \cdot \text{MaxIL}$ — **published on-chain** by the Stylus [FairValueOracle](/concepts/fair-value), which reads `σ_ref` from the VolOracle. The Φ-sum is **never reimplemented off-chain**; the SDK reads the on-chain value and never approximates it.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="sigma_ref (σ_ref)" type="reference volatility">
  $\sigma_{\text{ref}} = \max(\sigma_{\text{short}},\, \sigma_{\text{long}},\, \text{floor})$ — a poke-based, time-aware EWMA of Chainlink-tick log-returns over two horizons plus a hard floor. The **only** stochastic input to fairRate. The `max(...)` is deliberate: raw realized vol _understates_ risk right before a regime change, so the conservative max never collapses to a deceptively calm number just before a jump. See [σ_ref](/math/sigma-ref).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="load (totalLoad)" type="multiplier over FairPremium">
  The underwriter's charge **over** fair value: $\text{premium} = \text{FairPremium}\cdot(1 + \text{baseLoad} + \text{util\_skew} + \text{dispersion\_skew})$, hard-capped at $\text{FairPremium}\cdot(1+\text{maxLoad})$ (invariant [I10](/security/invariants)). Every load primitive comes from the calibrated risk-parameter set — **none is hardcoded**. See [the load stack](/math/load-stack).
</ParamField>

<Note>
The split: **FairPremium** is what the claim is _worth_; the **load** is what an underwriter _charges on top_. The pool (Path A) prices algorithmically with the full load stack; an MM (Path B) signs a firm quote at `FairPremium·(1 + loadBps)`. The LP gets [the cheaper of the two](/protocol/routing).
</Note>

## The underwriters and the two tranches

<ParamField path="Path A — the cvAMM pool" type="ConvexityVault">
  An always-on, signature-free pooled underwriter (a dual-tranche ERC-4626 vault). The **floor of liquidity**: it quotes a price into every market off FairPremium plus inventory skews. See [Path A](/protocol/path-a-cvamm).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Path B — MM competition" type="UnderwriterVault per MM">
  Sophisticated market makers compete with firm EIP-712 signed quotes **below** the pool, no last-look. The **ceiling of price**. See [Path B](/protocol/path-b-mm).
</ParamField>

The pool's capital is split into two tranches with very different risk:

<ParamField path="Senior tranche" type="Tranche enum 0">
  Base yield plus a small premium slice; **structurally protected from underwriting loss** while junior buffers (a "convexity savings account"). Protected, not risk-free — it still bears the systemic tail.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Junior tranche" type="Tranche enum 1">
  Captures most of the load for a high APY, but is **first-loss and unhedged** — it absorbs the entire underwriting tail first (a "pure vol-selling tranche").
</ParamField>

## Two separate guarantees — never merged

These are distinct claims. Both are true; neither implies the other.

<ClaimA />

The LP-side claim (no bad debt, structural and code-enforced) and the depositor-side reality (principal can be lost) live on different sides of the book. Read [no-bad-debt](/concepts/no-bad-debt) and [tranches](/concepts/tranches) for the full picture.
