The order flow Inflexion generates is a byproduct that is itself a product: **the
first structured view into the microstructure of the DeFi LP volatility-risk
premium**. No venue today prices the *in-range
convexity of a specific Uniswap v3 range*. Inflexion does, on-chain — and every fill
records **who paid what load, over a transparent realized-vol reference, for exactly
which position geometry, at which maturity**. The aggregate
of those choices is a dataset that **cannot exist anywhere else**, because no other
system is the mechanism that creates it.

<Info>
**Data is the moat, not a paywall.** The data is built passively from day one and
exposed as **free public APIs** (The Graph subgraph + REST). **Revenue is protocol
fees; the data is the competitive moat**. The defensibility is not
access control — it is that Inflexion is the only place this market clears, so it is
the only place the dataset accrues.
</Info>

This page is the conceptual overview. For the rigorous, detailed
treatment — the exact signal definitions, the on-chain events, the access map, and

## The flow as a product

Inflexion is the **first venue that prices the in-range IL convexity of a *specific*
Uniswap v3 range** ([the Phi-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum) reads the exact geometry
from `positions(tokenId)`, never a band midpoint). Because the price is computed
on-chain and the clearing record is emitted on-chain, every fill is a labeled data
point in a market that has never had a price before:

- **The load paid** — the markup over the protocol's [published fair value](/concepts/fair-value).
- **Over a transparent reference** — the on-chain realized-vol oracle [σ_ref](/math/sigma-ref).
- **For exactly which geometry** — the position's range and maturity (width ×
  distance-to-edge × duration), recovered by decoding the swap's `tokenId`.

That is the raw material of a **DeFi volatility-risk-premium surface for Uniswap
LPs** — the first on-chain dataset of this
market's microstructure. Quant funds, vol-arb desks, macro funds,
DeFi risk desks, structured-product issuers, and data platforms (Nansen / DefiLlama /
Kaiko / Dune) are the audience; they consume the free public API.

## The circular idea we deliberately dropped

The moat's credibility rests on **not being circular**. An earlier draft framed it as
"infer implied vol by inverting `fairRate`" — that idea is **DROPPED as circular**.
It is worth being explicit about *why*,
because the rigor is the point.

Every premium in the protocol is, by construction:

$$
\frac{\text{charged\_premium}}{\text{MaxIL}} \;=\; \text{fairRate}(\sigma_{\text{ref}}) \cdot (1 + \text{load})
$$

`fairRate` is the protocol's **own** published closed form (the
[Phi-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum), computed by the Stylus `FairValueOracle`), and
`σ_ref` is the protocol's **own** published realized-vol oracle. So inverting
`fairRate` from the charged premium recovers nothing but **our own published `σ_ref`
plus our own dealer [load stack](/math/load-stack)** — it tells an outside observer
of our oracle exactly what they already knew. That is not a market-implied vol; it is
a tautology.

<Warning>
**Do not present "invert `fairRate` to read implied vol" as a signal.**

- **Limit:** the inversion is mechanically circular — `charged/MaxIL =
  fairRate(σ_ref)·(1+load)` only returns the protocol's own `σ_ref` and load stack.
- **Why:** both inputs are protocol-computed, not external. Inverting a function with
  the function's own published inputs cannot yield independent information.
- **Mitigation / path-forward:** reframe the entire moat around **actor behavior** —
  the free *choices* the protocol does *not* compute (an MM's `loadBps`, an LP's
  geometry/duration choice), measured **against** the transparent mechanical baseline,
  never derived from it.
</Warning>

The decision rule, stated verbatim: a signal is
non-circular **iff** its informative degrees of freedom come from the MM's `loadBps`
or the LP's geometry/duration choice — measured against the transparent mechanical
baseline, not derived from it. The full non-circularity table, and the worked proof,
live on [circular vs behavioral](/moat/circular-vs-behavioral).

## The five non-circular behavioral signals

Each signal carries an explicit non-circularity verdict. One line each here; the
exhaustive definitions, sourcing, and per-signal maturity are on
[the five signals](/moat/five-signals).

### The shape every signal shares

The reason the set is non-circular is structural. Four of the five separate a
**behavioral** leg (an actor's free choice) from a **mechanical** baseline (the
protocol's transparent price-to-beat), and the *subtraction* is what isolates the
actor's view:

```
        BEHAVIORAL (the signal)              MECHANICAL (the baseline / price-to-beat)
        ───────────────────────              ─────────────────────────────────────────
Load:   QuoteFilled.loadBps  (Path B)   vs   SwapPriced.totalLoadWad  (Path A)
Vol:    the MM's forward view (implied)  vs   SwapPriced.sigmaRefWad   (backward EWMA)
Demand: which tokenId/duration LP picks  vs   (no mechanical counterpart — pure choice)
```

Path A is **never sold as a behavioral signal** — it is the transparent yardstick the
behavioral MM load is measured against. Two corollaries recur everywhere: the mechanical pool load is kept as the *price-to-beat*,
not a signal; and **cap-bound fills carry zero load information** and are excluded —
when `premium == MaxIL` the load was truncated by the cap, which is why the on-chain
`SwapPriced` event carries an explicit `cappedAtMaxIL` boolean.

## Structures day-one, dynamics with volume

This is the honest framing, and it is **mandatory** on every data-product surface.
The distinction is between the *static structures* (shippable from launch) and the
*full dynamics* (which require competition and flow).

What that means concretely:

| Layer | What it is | When it is real |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **On-chain moat events** | `SwapPriced` + `QuoteFilled` + the `CvammPricing.loadComponents` breakdown | **LIVE on-chain since the 2026-06-05 redeploy** — the dataset begins at the redeploy `startBlock` |
| **Off-chain telemetry** | `DEMAND_LOG` (Signal 4 latent half) + `COMPETITION_LOG` (Signal 2 dynamic half) | **CAPTURING NOW** — from the first interaction logged (it is unreconstructable retroactively, so capturing it now is the entire point) |
| **History / aggregate surfaces** | the subgraph that indexes the on-chain events into bucketed aggregates | **LIVE** — the subgraph is deployed and indexing on The Graph Studio; history/aggregate surfaces are served live via the hosted API |

<Info>
**The subgraph is deployed and indexing — history and aggregate surfaces are LIVE,
served via the hosted API.**

The subgraph is deployed on The Graph Studio and has indexed the on-chain history from
the redeploy `startBlock`, so every *history* / *aggregate* moat surface is served live
through the hosted REST API ([inflexion-backend.onrender.com](https://inflexion-backend.onrender.com)),
wired to the subgraph + an RPC + the telemetry sinks. The *current* (non-historical)
load surface remains available directly via the SDK multicall, and the off-chain
telemetry halves are capturing now.

Graceful degradation stays a design property: **if** `SUBGRAPH_URL` were unset, every
history / aggregate surface would return a typed `pending` body — never an error (the
body even embeds the exact GraphQL query, so re-wiring is a one-line swap; see
[degradation](/sdk/degradation)). On the free hosting tier the backend sleeps after
~15 min idle, so the first request after a cold start may be slow.
</Info>

So the leading-indicator and forward-vol *dynamics* (the dispersion of Signal 1, the
competitive distribution of Signal 2) need **≥3 competing MMs** and ~30–50
fills/bucket — weeks-to-months at launch flow. The **structures** — the term-structure shape, the demand skew by
geometry, the net-gamma surface — are queryable from launch. What ships first is the
architecture and the initial view, not yet a mature dataset.

## The honest limits (frame, never expose naked)

These limits belong on every data-product page; each carries its *why* and its
*mitigation*.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The clearing load is contaminated — trade the spread, not the level">
    **Limit:** the load mixes the convexity premium with liquidity, smart-contract
    risk, capital-lock cost, and inventory-skew premia.
    **Why:** a single observed load is a sum of several premia, not a clean
    vol-risk number.
    **Mitigation:** compare the **spread** (MM − pool, vs implied/forward vol), not the
    level — the subtraction cancels the shared contamination.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The implied-vol-inversion idea was dropped as circular">
    **Limit:** "invert `fairRate` for an implied-vol surface" returns only the
    protocol's own `σ_ref` + load.
    **Why:** both inputs are protocol-computed (covered above).
    **Mitigation:** the five signals are **behavioral** (MM `loadBps` + LP
    geometry/duration), measured against the mechanical baseline.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cap-bound fills carry zero load information">
    **Limit:** when `premium == MaxIL`, the observed number is the cap, not the actor's
    load.
    **Why:** the load was truncated by the cap, destroying the signal.
    **Mitigation:** filter `SwapPriced.cappedAtMaxIL == true` out of the load surfaces.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The signal is reflexive as the protocol scales">
    **Limit:** the protocol's own pricing influences the surface it measures.
    **Why:** this is intrinsic to being the venue that creates the market.
    **Mitigation:** normal for any derivative market — read levels in context, and
    lean on cross-venue spreads (vs Deribit).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Calibration lags at launch — and that is the moat">
    **Limit:** there is no historical realized-IL dataset to calibrate against on
    day one.
    **Why:** one has never existed — no venue priced this risk before.
    **Mitigation:** the protocol **is the mechanism that creates** the dataset. The
    absence of a prior dataset is exactly why owning the flow is defensible.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
