Inflexion makes exactly one guarantee to the LP about getting paid, and it is a
strong one: in **FULL mode** the protocol **cannot produce bad debt** — the LP's
capped payout is always backed by collateral already locked. But the guarantee is
**conditional**, and the conditions are not decorative. Said without them, the claim
is simply wrong.

This page states the guarantee correctly, shows *why* each clause is load-bearing,
and draws the one distinction that matters most: **no-bad-debt (Claim A) is a promise
to the LP, never a promise to the underwriter.** Depositor and MM capital is a
separate question with a separate, honest answer.

<ClaimA />

## The structural core: `payout ≤ collateral == MaxIL`

Everything rests on a single arithmetic fact. At creation, the underwriter locks
exactly **`MaxIL`** of collateral. At settlement, the protocol pays the LP

$$
\text{payout} \;=\; \min(\text{realized\_IL},\ \text{MaxIL}).
$$

Because `collateral == MaxIL` in FULL mode and the payout is capped at `MaxIL`,

$$
\text{payout} \;=\; \min(\text{realized\_IL},\ \text{MaxIL}) \;\le\; \text{MaxIL} \;=\; \text{collateral}
$$

for **any** price path — in-range, below `Pa`, above `Pb`, and the no-IL region.
This is **invariant [I1](/security/invariants)**, and it is a *construction*, not a
hope: there is no sequence of prices, no settlement timing, and no σ value that can
make a capped payout exceed the collateral that equals the cap.

| Quantity | Definition | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Collateral locked (FULL) | `== MaxIL`, locked at creation | `InflexionCore` (Path A and Path B) |
| Payout cap | `payout = realisedIL > maxIL ? maxIL : realisedIL` | `InflexionCore` |
| Defensive re-check | `payout ≤ lockedAmount` at distribution | `ConvexityVault`, `UnderwriterVault` |

[MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) is the maximum *in-range* impermanent loss of the specific
position — pure geometry, frozen at creation. It is convex on `[Pa, Pb]`, so its
maximum while price stays in range is attained at a boundary,
`MaxIL = max(IL(Pa), IL(Pb))`. That is exactly why the
cap is the right collateral amount: it is the worst case the product covers. See
[the MaxIL derivation](/math/maxil-derivation) and [the no-bad-debt math](/math/no-bad-debt-math)
for the full proof.

<Note>
**`realized_IL` is recomputed on-chain at settle, never caller-supplied.** The
settlement price `sqrtPTX96` is derived on-chain from the Chainlink round pinned at
expiry `T` via `_oracleSqrtPriceX96`, then fed to
`computeIL` together with the **stored** liquidity `L` (invariant
[I6](/security/invariants)). A permissionless settler cannot set the payout anywhere
in `[0, MaxIL]` — the inputs are not theirs to choose. This is what makes I1, I2, and
[I4](/security/invariants) hold against an adversarial settler.
</Note>

## The full clause — and why every word is load-bearing

The guarantee is exact **only** under four conditions. Each one, if removed, breaks a
different link in the chain `payout ≤ collateral`. A caveat is only honest when it
comes with its *why* and its *mitigation*, so here is each clause with both.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="(a) Capped payoff — payout = min(realized_IL, MaxIL)">
    **Why it is load-bearing.** Remove the cap and beyond-range IL is **unbounded**.
    Above `Pb` the LP is fully rotated into one asset at constant value while a hold
    position keeps growing linearly with price, so *absolute* IL has no ceiling.
    No fixed amount of collateral can back an unbounded liability —
    the product could not be fully collateralized, so it could not be trustless.

    **Why the cap is the right product, not a defect.** At the range boundary the LP
    has already fully rotated; loss beyond that point is *directional* (foregone spot
    upside), not the *impermanent* loss the LP set out to hedge. Capping at MaxIL is
    what keeps the hedge fully collateralized and trustless. This is an
    [**in-range convexity hedge**](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge), not unbounded
    "IL insurance," and the cap is the line between the two.

    **Mitigation / path-forward.** LPs who want protection beyond the range simply
    re-cover after re-ranging — a new in-range position, a new MaxIL, fresh collateral.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="(b) Solvent collateral asset (USDC)">
    **Why it is load-bearing.** Collateral, premium, and payout are all denominated in
    USDC (`dUSDC`, 6 decimals, the [numéraire](/reference/decimals-units)). The
    non-oracle token in every pair is assumed to be a USD-stable numéraire.
    If that asset loses its peg or its redeemability,
    `payout ≤ collateral` can still hold *in token units* while failing in real value.

    **Why this is a disclosed shared risk, not a protocol bug.** A USDC depeg is a
    systemic event that hits every party denominated in USDC equally — it is not
    something the protocol's settlement logic can or should try to absorb. We disclose
    it rather than pretend it away.

    **Mitigation / path-forward.** Use a collateral asset with a credible peg and
    instant redeemability; the design is asset-agnostic at the numéraire layer, so a
    different solvent stable can be configured per market.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="(c) Oracle / settlement liveness">
    **Why it is load-bearing.** `settle()` pins the price to the Chainlink round at
    expiry `T`. If the oracle *never* produces a usable round, funds cannot settle —
    not because the math fails, but because settlement cannot run. Note the subtlety:
    **the *amount* I1 caps is oracle-independent; the *act* of settling needs the
    oracle live.** A wrong σ cannot violate I1, but a permanently dead oracle could
    strand funds.

    **Why it does not become a permanent lockup.** This was an audited risk
    ([Fork 1](/security/forks)). The fix is invariant [I8](/security/invariants): the
    price stays pinned to the round-at-T for fairness, a glitched "lone spike" (a print
    differing from **both** neighbours by ≥ `LONE_SPIKE_BPS = 500` / 5%) is deferred,
    and a `LIVENESS_WINDOW = 86,400s` (24h) **backstop unconditionally accepts the
    round** after the window. A missing neighbour round across a Chainlink `phaseId`
    boundary is read tolerantly via `_tryRound` (try/catch, not revert), so it degrades
    to the backstop rather than locking. Implemented in
    `OracleManager.getSettlementPrice`.

    <Warning>
    **Limit → Why → Mitigation (the I8 window).** The *spec-literal* staleness
    `require` fires before the backstop can recover the round, so the **actual usable
    settlement window is `[expiry + LIVENESS_WINDOW, expiry + MAX_STALENESS)` ≈ 1 hour
    wide** with current Sepolia constants (90,000 − 86,400 = 3,600s), not the full
    additive sum the I8 wording suggests. **Why:** the two guards were tuned
    independently. **Mitigation:** past `expiry + MAX_STALENESS` funds still settle via
    a *newer* Chainlink round (Arbitrum majors tick frequently), just not via the
    round-at-T; the window is *symmetric* for LP and MM, preserving fairness. Surfaced
    in the `OracleManager` NatSpec, not papered over.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="(d) No rehypothecation breach">
    **Why it is load-bearing.** `payout ≤ collateral` is only meaningful if the
    collateral is **actually there when settle runs**. Route locked collateral to a
    utilization-gated venue (Aave / Compound) and a withdrawal could *revert in a
    crash* — exactly when payouts are due — turning a solvent-on-paper position into a
    failed settlement.

    **Why this is a hard rule, not a preference.** Locked collateral must stay
    instantly liquid. This is a hard rule and audit fix #3:
    only **idle** capital may earn yield, and only to
    **instantly-redeemable** wrappers (sDAI / tokenized T-bills) — never to anything
    whose redemption depends on pool utilization.

    **Mitigation / path-forward.** The yield adapter interface requires
    `isInstantlyRedeemable()`; locked collateral is never routed to a gated venue by
    construction. Idle-only, instant-only.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

> **Stated without these qualifiers, the guarantee is not correct.** "Bad debt
> impossible" — full stop — is incorrect. The exact
> claim is always: *no bad debt in FULL mode under capped payoff + solvent USDC +
> oracle/settlement liveness + no rehypothecation breach.*

## I1 is structurally oracle-independent

This is the most important nuance on the page, and the one that makes the guarantee
robust. **The no-bad-debt invariant (I1) does not depend on any pricing oracle being
right.**

In FULL mode, `collateral == MaxIL` and `payout = min(realized_IL, MaxIL) ≤ MaxIL`
**regardless of what σ was, regardless of what the [FairValueOracle](/concepts/fair-value)
priced, regardless of what the VolOracle estimated.** A wrong `σ_ref` cannot violate
I1, because I1 is about the *cap and the collateral*, both of which are fixed pure
geometry — they were never functions of σ.

What the pricing oracles *are* load-bearing for:

| Oracle | Load-bearing for | NOT load-bearing for |
|---|---|---|
| `FairValueOracle` (Φ-sum) | the [I10 price cap](/security/invariants), [depositor solvency](/concepts/tranches) | **I1 (FULL no-bad-debt)** |
| `VolOracle` (σ_ref EWMA) | I10, premium correctness, [depositor solvency](/concepts/tranches) | **I1 (FULL no-bad-debt)** |
| `OracleManager` (Chainlink round-at-T) | settlement *liveness* and the realized-IL *value* | the *amount* I1 caps (capped structurally) |

<Tip>
**The crucial split.** A vol-oracle fault can cost *depositors* money — bad pricing
compresses pool NAV, the junior tranche absorbs first, the senior takes the systemic
tail — but it can **never** create *LP* bad debt in FULL. The pricing stack governs
who profits and who loses on the *underwriting* side; it does not govern whether the
LP is paid. That separation is exactly the two-claims rule below.
</Tip>

The Slither cvAMM review confirmed this independence directly: manual review
verified that **the FULL no-bad-debt guarantee (I1) is structural and independent of
the FairValue / Vol oracles** — `settle` caps payout at `MaxIL == collateral`
regardless of any pricing input.

## Two separate guarantees — never merged

Claim A (LP no-bad-debt) and Claim B (depositor/MM capital) are **two different
promises to two different parties.** Both are true; neither implies the other; merging
them is a category error.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Claim A — LPs are always paid" icon="shield-check">
    No bad debt in FULL, **code-enforced (I1)**. Structural and oracle-independent:
    `settle` caps payout at `MaxIL == collateral` regardless of any pricing oracle.
    Qualified by the full clause above.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Claim B — capital is NOT guaranteed" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    The underwriter is a **volatility seller**. Junior is first-loss; senior is
    structurally protected from *underwriting* loss only, never from the systemic
    tail. Applies to **both** the cvAMM pool and any MM.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<ClaimB />

A worked way to hold the two apart:

- **A bad price path** moves money *from the underwriter to the LP* — the LP is paid
  `min(IL, MaxIL)` out of locked collateral. Claim A holds (LP paid); Claim B is
  *realized* (the underwriter took the loss it was paid a premium to take). Nothing is
  broken; this is the product working.
- **A wrong σ / mispricing** can make underwriting *systematically unprofitable* over
  time — premiums too low for the risk. Claim B bites (depositors can lose principal);
  Claim A is **untouched** (every individual LP is still paid up to MaxIL from
  collateral that equals MaxIL).
- **A USDC depeg or dead oracle** is a *shared* systemic failure outside both the
  capped-payoff and the FULL-collateral mechanisms — it sits in the qualifying clause
  of Claim A, and it is part of the "systemic tail" that even the senior tranche bears
  in Claim B.

> Inflexion is **not Bancor.** The cvAMM pays claims in **pre-locked USDC and mints
> nothing** — no token-inflation reinsurance, no death spiral. In FULL the pool cannot
> become insolvent against its LPs (collateral = MaxIL ≥ payout) and cannot be run
> (withdrawal cooldown + locked/free accounting). See [what this is not](/what-this-is-not).

## Senior protection is a calibration result, not a third guarantee

It is tempting to read the senior tranche as "safe." It is not — and conflating
structural senior *protection* with a *guarantee* would be a third claim we do not
make.

The pool enforces **`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`** at *every* `lockCollateral`
(the `SeniorProtectionBreached` revert). Since every
payout `≤` its MaxIL `=` its locked amount, total payouts `≤ totalLocked ≤
juniorAssets`, so the **junior-first-loss waterfall absorbs all underwriting loss
before senior is ever touched** (in `releaseAndDistribute`).

<Warning>
**Limit → Why → Mitigation (senior P(loss) = 0).** Senior `P(loss) = 0` is a
**calibration** result — it holds while utilization stays within the buffer
(`u ≤ 1−sf`), **not** a structural guarantee. **Why:** the `totalLocked ≤
juniorAssets` invariant protects senior from *underwriting* loss, but no invariant
shields any tranche from the *systemic* tail (USDC depeg, oracle failure, contract
bug). **Mitigation:** the dual-tranche structure lets each depositor pick a risk dose
honestly; **capital-not-guaranteed remains the umbrella for both tranches**.
See [tranches](/concepts/tranches).
</Warning>

## How the guarantee is verified, not just asserted

I1 is not a comment in the code — it is fuzzed over the whole price domain.

<CodeGroup>
```solidity I1 + I2 — the payout cap (InflexionCore)
// realisedIL recomputed on-chain from the Chainlink-pinned P_T and the STORED L
uint128 payout = realisedIL > s.maxIL ? s.maxIL : uint128(realisedIL);
//               └─────────────────── min(realized_IL, MaxIL) ───────────────┘
// collateral == s.maxIL was locked at creation ⇒ payout ≤ collateral, any path.
```

```solidity Defensive re-check at distribution (ConvexityVault)
require(payout <= lockedAmount, "payout exceeds locked"); // defense-in-depth
// UnderwriterVault mirrors this for Path B.
```
</CodeGroup>

| Invariant | What it asserts | Verified by |
|---|---|---|
| I1 / I2 | `payout ≤ MaxIL == collateral`, `payout == min(IL, MaxIL)` | `testFuzz_I1_I2_payoutCappedAtMaxIL` (256 runs) |
| I3 | guarded subtraction, never underflow | `testFuzz_I3_ilNonNegative` (256 runs) |
| I4 | `V_lp ≥ V_hold ⇒ payout == 0` | `test_il_atEntryIsZero` + I3 fuzz |
| I5 | `locked ≤ deposited` per MM and per pool | `invariant_I5_lockedLeDeposited` — 256 runs × 128,000 vault-op calls × 0 reverts |
| I6 | settle uses `L` stored at creation | `testFuzz_I6_settleUsesStoredLiquidity` |
| I8 | settle always succeeds in the backstop window | `OracleManager` invariant suite — 5 fuzz tests, ~1,280 runs |

The supporting invariants close the vectors that *could* otherwise break the core
inequality from the side: [I3](/security/invariants) (no underflow when `V_lp >
V_hold`), [I4](/security/invariants) (the LP never profits from the swap — payout is
`0` whenever IL is `0`), [I5](/security/invariants) (`locked ≤ deposited`, so the
collateral is genuinely there), and [I6](/security/invariants) (settle uses the `L`
*stored at creation*, so an external `increaseLiquidity` on the custodied NFT cannot
inflate the payout above MaxIL).

## The guarantee, proven live

The 2026-06-05 redeploy ran real create→settle lifecycles on both rails, and in both
cases the payout came from the **counterparty's own collateral**, never protocol funds:

- **Path A (cvAMM):** swap #1 — premium \$9.70 on MaxIL \$1,669.24 (0.58% of MaxIL),
  realized IL \$148.64, payout \$148.64, paid from the `ConvexityVault`.
- **Path B (routed → MM):** swap #2 — `createSwapRouted` picked the MM (\$8.93 vs the
  cvAMM's \$13.80); MaxIL \$3,215.65 was locked from the **MM's own** `UnderwriterVault`,
  realized IL \$245.66, payout \$245.66 settled from that collateral.

In each case `payout ≤ collateral == MaxIL` held, and the LP was paid from collateral
locked at creation — the guarantee, end to end. See [the two rails](/concepts/two-rails)
and [the full lifecycle](/protocol/lifecycle).
