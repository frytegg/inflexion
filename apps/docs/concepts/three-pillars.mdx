<Info>
**Read this first.** Everything else in these docs sits on top of three pillars. Once you hold them, the rest of the protocol — the math, the lifecycle, the vaults, the data moat — reads as elaboration. The three are: **(1)** an on-chain *published* fair value (the truth), **(2)** the cvAMM pool (the floor of liquidity, always on), and **(3)** market-maker competition (the ceiling of price). One settlement core sits underneath both rails.
</Info>

Inflexion is a collateralized bilateral derivatives market on Arbitrum One — live on [Arbitrum Sepolia](/reference/deployments) (chainId `421614`) — that lets a Uniswap v3 LP pay a fixed upfront premium to transfer the **in-range** impermanent-loss risk of a specific position to an underwriter. The underwriter posts collateral and is paid for taking the risk; at expiry the protocol pays the LP $\min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$, trustlessly, from that collateral. This is an [in-range convexity hedge](/concepts/in-range-convexity-hedge), not "IL insurance" — and the cap is load-bearing.

The three pillars are how the protocol is organized. They map to three questions any market must answer:

The one-liner that ties them together:

> **Floor + ceiling, one settlement core.** The cvAMM is the floor of liquidity (it always quotes a code-capped price); the MMs are the ceiling of price (they win only by beating it); and a single, rail-agnostic settlement core pays the LP the same way regardless of who underwrote.

## Pillar 1 — On-chain published FairValue (the truth)

The protocol does not just settle claims — it **computes and publishes the fair value of the claim on-chain** before anyone trades. That published number is the reference price every other part of the system is measured against.

$$
\text{FairPremium} \;=\; \text{fairRate} \cdot \text{MaxIL}
$$

The genius is the factorization: the two terms carry orthogonal information.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="MaxIL — pure geometry">
    The maximum in-range IL of the *specific* position, computable at creation from $(P_a, P_b, L, P_0)$. **Frozen at creation, identical across all three durations.** Duration changes nothing about MaxIL.
  </Card>
  <Card title="fairRate — all the vol/time">
    $\text{fairRate} = E_Q[\min(\text{IL}, \text{MaxIL})] / \text{MaxIL}$ — the fraction of MaxIL the claim is worth under the risk-neutral measure. **fairRate carries ALL the vol/time dependence; MaxIL carries none.**
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Because the duration knob lives entirely inside `fairRate`, the cvAMM publishes **three different prices for the same position** — one per duration — all backed by the **same** MaxIL collateral.

### fairRate is an S-curve in $\sigma^2 T$

`fairRate` is an S-curve in $\sigma_{\text{ref}}^2 \cdot T$: it is $\approx 0$ in calm/short regimes (price rarely leaves a wide range) and saturates toward $1$ in violent/long regimes (price almost surely exits the range). The single stochastic input is $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ — see [the sigma-ref estimator](/math/sigma-ref).

| Range width | 7d | 30d | 90d |
| ----------- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| ±5% | 69.5% | 84.8% | 91.3% |
| ±10% | 44.9% | 70.8% | 82.9% |
| ±20% | 18.2% | 47.3% | 67.4% |

<sub>fairRate at σ = 60% (risk-neutral, from the Monte Carlo reference model) — note the ~2–4× movement across durations for a single width, and that it is MaxIL that stays fixed.</sub>

### It is an exact closed form — not a fitted surface

This is the claim that makes Pillar 1 a *truth* and not a guess. The capped v3 payoff $\min(\text{IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ is **piecewise** — a below-$P_a$ linear arm, a convex $c_1 P - 2\sqrt{P} + c_0$ arm inside $[P_a, P_b]$, and an above-$P_b$ linear arm, split by the two cap-crossing prices. Each arm integrated against the lognormal density of $P_T$ is a standard interval moment in the normal CDF $\Phi$. So the whole expectation collapses to a finite **$\Phi$-sum** (~6–10 terms, Black–Scholes class):

- **No Monte Carlo, no lookup table, no calibrated coefficients.** It is evaluated live, per quote.
- **L-independent.** The $\Phi$-sum runs in normalized units ($P_0 = 1, L = 1$), so it depends only on $a = P_a/P_0$, $b = P_b/P_0$, $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$, and $T$.
- **Verified exact** against the reference model — closed form ≡ quadrature ≡ MC — to ~5×10⁻¹¹ across width × σ × T.

<Warning>
**The $\Phi$-sum is NEVER reimplemented off-chain** — this is a hard project rule, not a preference.

- **Why:** an off-chain reimplementation would be a second, drifting source of truth — the whole point of Pillar 1 is that there is exactly *one* published price the chain itself produces.
- **Mitigation / where the truth lives:** the **production pricer is the Stylus `FairValueOracle`** ([address](/reference/deployments)), machine-precise to ~6.7×10⁻¹⁵. The Solidity `FairValueOracle` (Φ via Abramowitz–Stegun, ~1e-7) is a CI cross-check, **not** a second production oracle. The SDK reads the on-chain `FairPremium` and never approximates it.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Theory anchors vs. the pricer — a distinction to keep straight.** Two 2025 academic results explain *why* this claim is priceable and hedgeable: **Lipton–Lucic–Sepp (2025)** (an IL-protection claim is statically replicable by a strip of vanilla options) and **Milionis–Moallemi–Roughgarden (2022)** (LVR has a closed form ∝ instantaneous variance). They are the [theory anchors](/math/theory-anchors), **not** the pricer — the exact $\Phi$-sum is the pricer. Do not conflate them.
</Note>

Pillar 1 is the load-bearing one for the other two: the cvAMM and every MM price *off the same published `FairPremium`*. Read it in full at [Fair value](/concepts/fair-value) and [the $\Phi$-sum](/math/fair-value-phi-sum); MaxIL has its own page at [MaxIL — the cap and the unit of risk](/concepts/maxil).

## Pillar 2 — The cvAMM (the floor of liquidity)

A market that needs a counterparty to *show up* before it can quote has a cold-start problem. Pillar 2 removes it. The cvAMM is a **pooled passive underwriter** — `ConvexityVault`, an ERC-4626-style USDC vault with a **dual-tranche SENIOR/JUNIOR structure from launch** ([deployed](/reference/deployments)). It quotes algorithmically on-chain off the published `FairPremium`, posts collateral from the pool, and is **always quoting**. It is Path A — signature-free, fully on-chain (`createSwapPathA`).

It solves four problems at once:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Cold-start" icon="power-off">
    There is always a price, with **no MM present**. The protocol is complete and solvent on Path A alone.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Overcharge" icon="lock">
    The price is **capped in code** by invariant I10 — $\text{premium} \le \text{FairPremium}\cdot(1+\text{maxLoad})$ — not by trust.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Intra-pair diversification" icon="chart-pie">
    One pool writes many positions whose exits do **not** all cluster at the same price.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Depositor viability" icon="layer-group">
    Each depositor picks an honest risk dose — senior savings account vs. junior vol-seller. See [tranches](/concepts/tranches).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### The price is capped in code, not by trust

The cvAMM quote is `FairPremium` lifted by a transparent, on-chain load stack and then **hard-clamped**:

$$
\text{premium} = \text{FairPremium}\cdot\bigl(1 + \underbrace{\text{baseLoad} + \text{util\_skew} + \text{dispersion\_skew}}_{\text{clamped at }\text{maxLoad}}\bigr)
$$

`totalLoad = min(baseLoad + util_skew + dispersion_skew, maxLoad)`, so the depositor pool can never overcharge the LP beyond the contractual cap. That clamp is **invariant I10** — see [the invariants](/security/invariants) and [the load stack](/math/load-stack). None of these primitives is hardcoded; the regimes and curves come from the calibrated risk-parameter set.

This is why the cvAMM is the **floor of liquidity**: it guarantees a fair, code-capped price exists for every position in every market, at all times, with no external dependency. Deep dive at [Path A — the cvAMM](/protocol/path-a-cvamm).

## Pillar 3 — MM competition (the ceiling of price)

If Pillar 2 guarantees a price always exists, Pillar 3 guarantees that price can be *beaten*. Sophisticated market makers compete via **firm EIP-712 signed quotes** (with EIP-1271 contract-signer support) priced **below** the pool — Path B (`createSwap`). There is **no last-look**: a quote is a firm bearer instrument, protected only by deterministic on-chain checks at fill (oracle-anchored price band, short validity window, bitmap nonce). See [firm quotes, no last-look](/protocol/path-b-mm).

MMs matter for **two load-bearing reasons** — state both:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hedged MMs export short-gamma risk OUT of the system">
    A closed pool can only *relocate* short-gamma risk — without MMs the protocol becomes a closed pocket of ETH short gamma circulating against itself. An MM who hedges on the global options market (Deribit / Panoptic) makes the **whole system's risk smaller**, not just moved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forward-looking-vol MMs correct the pool's backward bias">
    The pool prices off realized $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ — a structurally **backward-looking** estimator. MMs price off implied / forward vol. They are the mechanism that incorporates forward information the pool structurally cannot see.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The MM rail is the **ceiling of price**: because routing always picks the cheaper rail, an MM only ever wins a fill when it **genuinely beats the capped pool price**. It cannot divert flow by merely matching the pool. This is also why the residual forward-vol premium the pool's backward-looking $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$ misses is deliberately left as **MM alpha** — that blindness is the incentive that keeps MMs in the two-sided market.

Pillar 3 is **optional and additive**. The protocol is complete with Pillar 2 alone; every MM that arrives only ever lowers the LP's price.

## Floor + ceiling, one settlement core

The three pillars are not three products. They are two *upstream rails* — the pool (floor) and the MMs (ceiling) — feeding **one downstream settlement core**.

```
                       Pillar 1: FairPremium  (the truth, published on-chain)
                                  │
              ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
        Pillar 2: cvAMM                          Pillar 3: MMs
     (floor — always quotes,                 (ceiling — win only by
      code-capped via I10)                    strictly beating the floor)
              │                                       │
              └───────────────────┬───────────────────┘
                       createSwapRouted  →  cheaper rail wins, never reverts
                                  │
                       ONE settlement core  →  pays LP min(IL, MaxIL)
                          (rail-agnostic, non-custodial, I1–I9)
```

Every difference between Path A and Path B lives **upstream of settle**, in how the swap is priced and how collateral is locked. The settlement core — the `settle()` function, the $\min(\text{IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ cap, the MaxIL formula, the [invariants I1–I9](/security/invariants) — is **identical regardless of which rail opened the swap**. The FULL no-bad-debt guarantee is therefore independent of *how the quote was discovered*; it is enforced at on-chain settlement against the counterparty's own locked collateral.

| | **Path A — cvAMM (floor)** | **Path B — MM (ceiling)** |
|---|---|---|
| Counterparty | `ConvexityVault` (pool) | a single MM |
| Signature | none (signature-free) | EIP-712 / EIP-1271 firm quote |
| Collateral home | `ConvexityVault` (pooled, dual-tranche) | `UnderwriterVault` (per-MM) |
| Pricing | on-chain load stack, I10 clamp | `loadBps` over FairPremium, `≤ maxLoadBps` |
| Premium split | 99% into pool (tranche split), 1% treasury | 99% MM, 1% treasury |
| Validity clock / nonces / band | none | `validUntil` + bitmap nonce + oracle band |
| Relayer needed | no (pure on-chain) | yes (off-chain quote book) |
| Role | **floor of liquidity** (always quotes) | **ceiling of price** (wins only by beating pool) |

### How `createSwapRouted` picks the cheaper rail

The router (`createSwapRouted`) is where floor and ceiling meet. It is the single best illustration of the whole architecture:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pool must be wired (the floor must exist)">
    The market must be `cvammEnabled` — there must be something to fall back to. Otherwise there is no floor and the call reverts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Price both rails off the SAME FairPremium">
    A single VolOracle poke refreshes $\sigma_{\text{ref}}$, then both rails are priced off the **same** published `FairPremium`: `premiumA = _pricePathAFromFair`, `(usableB, premiumB) = _quoteUsableAndPremiumB`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate the MM quote WITHOUT touching state">
    `_quoteUsableAndPremiumB` is the **`view`, non-reverting mirror** of Path-B's gates. It returns `(false, 0)` for any defect — absent / wrong-model / over-load / expired / out-of-band / nonce-used / under-collateralized / bad-sig / dust — and **never mutates a nonce, capacity, or lock**, so an unchosen quote leaves zero trace (preserving [capacity invariant I7](/security/invariants)).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Route to the strictly cheaper rail (ties go to the floor)">
    `useB = usableB && premiumB < premiumA` — the MM wins **only if it STRICTLY beats** the pool. A **tie resolves to Path A**, the dependency-free always-on rail, so an MM cannot divert flow by merely matching.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A bad MM quote falls back to the pool — it NEVER reverts">
    An absent / expired / stale / over-band / over-load / under-collateralized MM quote simply **falls back to the floor**. Only protocol-level failures (market / owner / range / dust / slippage / pool-unwired) can revert the call. Only the executed rail mutates nonce / capacity / lock.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The route is auditable on-chain: the router emits `SwapRouted(swapId, pathB, premiumA, premiumB)`, so the cheapest-wins decision is part of the [data moat](/concepts/data-moat).

<Tip>
**This is the whole thesis in one function.** The LP always gets the cheaper of `{pool, best MM quote}`; the pool guarantees a price always exists; the MMs guarantee it can be beaten; and a bad MM quote can never deny the LP a fill, because the floor is always there to catch it.
</Tip>

## The no-bad-debt guarantee — stated correctly

The cap from Pillar 1 (MaxIL) plus FULL collateralization is what makes the LP-payout guarantee a *construction* rather than a hope: $\text{collateral} = \text{MaxIL} \ge \text{payout} = \min(\text{realized\_IL}, \text{MaxIL})$ by construction. But it holds only under its full qualifying clause — never state it unqualified.

<ClaimA />

This is a separate claim from depositor risk (above). LPs are always paid in FULL (structural, code-enforced invariant I1); depositors and MMs can lose principal (volatility sellers in a crash). Both are true; neither implies the other — see [no bad debt](/concepts/no-bad-debt) and [the math](/math/no-bad-debt-math).
