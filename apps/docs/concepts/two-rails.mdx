Inflexion has exactly **two ways** an underwriter can be put on the other side of an
LP's hedge, and exactly **one way** the hedge is settled. The two upstream rails
differ only in how the price is discovered and where the collateral lives; the
**downstream settlement core is identical** regardless of which rail opened the swap.

This separation is the architectural spine of the protocol. It is why the cvAMM can
be the floor of liquidity and the MMs the ceiling of price, why a bad MM quote can
never harm an LP, and why the no-bad-debt guarantee is independent of how the quote
was discovered.

## One settlement core, two upstream rails

Every difference between Path A and Path B lives in `createSwap` pricing and
locking — strictly **upstream of `settle`**. The settlement core (`settle`, the
`min(IL, MaxIL)` cap, the [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil) formula, and [invariants](/security/invariants)
I1–I9) is **identical regardless of which rail opened the swap**.

<Note>
The shipped `SwapRecord` does not carry a `path` field. `settle` infers the rail
purely from the stored counterparty — `s.mm == address(convexityVault)` means it was
Path A, anything else means Path B. The settlement math
does not branch on the rail; only the **vault dispatch** does (pool vs MM collateral).
</Note>

```
        Path A (createSwapPathA)              Path B (createSwap)
        signature-free, on-chain              firm EIP-712 signed quote
                  │                                     │
        on-chain load stack,                  loadBps over FairPremium,
        I10-clamped, capped at MaxIL          ≤ maxLoadBps, capped at MaxIL
                  │                                     │
        lock from ConvexityVault              lock from UnderwriterVault (per-MM)
                  │                                     │
                  └──────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                                 ▼
                  ════════ ONE SETTLEMENT CORE ════════
            settle(swapId, hintRoundId)
            • pin Chainlink round at expiry T
            • IL with the STORED L (I6)
            • payout = min(realized_IL, MaxIL)   (I1 + I2)
            • dispatch payout from the rail's vault
            • return the NFT to the LP
```

Both rails are priced off the **same on-chain published fair value** — the
[`FairPremium`](/concepts/fair-value) — computed once per create:

$$
\text{FairPremium} = \text{fairRate} \cdot \text{MaxIL}, \qquad
\text{fairRate} = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[\min(IL,\,\text{MaxIL})\right]}{\text{MaxIL}}
$$

The router pokes the [VolOracle](/protocol/oracles) once and prices **both** rails
off the same `FairPremium` — there is exactly one fair
value per position, and the rails only differ in the load applied on top of it.

## Path A — the cvAMM rail (`createSwapPathA`)

Path A is the **pooled passive underwriter**: the dual-tranche `ConvexityVault`. It
is always quoting, signature-free at the point of sale, and entirely on-chain.

- **Counterparty:** the `ConvexityVault` (pooled, dual-tranche SENIOR/JUNIOR — see
  [tranches](/concepts/tranches)).
- **Pricing:** the on-chain [load stack](/math/load-stack), hard-capped by invariant
  I10, then additionally capped at MaxIL.
- **No keeper, no signed quote, no validity clock, no relayer.** Execution
  (`_executePathA`) locks collateral from the pool,
  pulls the premium, takes the NFT into the `ILVault`, and splits the premium **99%
  into the pool** (across tranches) and **1% to the treasury**.

The load applied on top of `FairPremium` is computed mechanically from the pool's
own state:

$$
\text{premium}_A = \text{FairPremium}\cdot\bigl(1 + \min(\underbrace{\text{baseLoad} + \text{util\_skew} + \text{disp\_skew}}_{\text{totalLoad}},\ \text{maxLoad})\bigr)
$$

The `min(..., maxLoad)` is the I10 clamp: the pool **cannot** overcharge beyond
`FairPremium · (1 + maxLoad)` — by construction in code, not by trust. None of these
load primitives are hardcoded; they come from the calibrated risk-parameter set.

<Tip>
Path A is the reason there is **no cold-start problem**. The cvAMM always quotes a
code-capped fair price with no market maker present, so an LP can always be priced
and protected — the pool is the floor of liquidity.
</Tip>

## Path B — the MM rail (`createSwap`)

Path B is the **firm signed-quote rail**. A market maker streams an EIP-712 (or
EIP-1271 contract-signed) quote and posts collateral in its own `UnderwriterVault`.

- **Counterparty:** a single MM, collateralized in the per-MM `UnderwriterVault`.
- **The quote is PER-MARKET, never per-NFT.** The `SignedQuote`
  carries a `marketId`, a `loadBps`, a MaxIL-ratio band,
  and capacity — but **no `tokenId`**. This is the direct mechanical consequence of
  [MaxIL being the fungible unit of risk](/concepts/maxil): an MM expresses "I write
  positions whose MaxIL is 2%–7% of V0" once, and **any** LP position in that market
  fills against it.
- **Pricing:** the contract derives
  $\text{premium}_B = \lceil\text{FairPremium}\cdot(1 + \text{loadBps}/10^4)\rceil$,
  capped at MaxIL, and requires `loadBps ≤ maxLoadBps` (I10 on Path B).

Because a signed payload is a **bearer instrument** that survives past an off-chain
cancel, Path B is firm — there is **no last-look**. The MM cannot reject at
settlement; its protections are three deterministic on-chain checks at fill (no MM
discretion):

<Steps>
  <Step title="Oracle-anchored price band (I9 / Fork 2)">
    At fill, `require absBps(P_live, quote.quotePrice) ≤ priceBandBps` reusing the
    same on-chain price read. This kills the dominant
    gap-on-stale-quote pickoff. See [price pinning](/security/price-pinning).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Short validity window">
    `validUntil` must be `[5s, 15s]` ahead (default 8s) — bounds the leakage interval.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Selective bitmap nonce">
    Permit2-style bitmap (word `<<` 8 `|` bit): cancel one quote by flipping one bit,
    never cancel-all (`cancelNonces`).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Execution (`_executePathB`) is the **only** place
Path-B state mutates: it increments `consumedNotional[quoteId]` (capacity authority,
I7), consumes the nonce, locks the MM's collateral, writes the record, pulls the
premium, takes the NFT, and splits the premium **99% to the MM, 1% to the treasury**
(no tranche split on Path B).

## `createSwapRouted` — the cheaper of `{pool, MM}`

The third entry point, `createSwapRouted`, is how an
LP gets the **best of both rails in a single transaction**. It prices both rails off
the same `FairPremium` (single poke) and routes to whichever is cheaper.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pool must be wired">
    The market must be `cvammEnabled` — the always-on floor must exist as a fallback,
    else there is nothing to fall back to and the call reverts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Price both rails, once">
    `premiumA = _pricePathAFromFair` and
    `(usableB, premiumB) = _quoteUsableAndPremiumB` off the **same** `FairPremium`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Route — strict-beat, ties to Path A">
    `useB = usableB && premiumB < premiumA`. An MM wins **only if it strictly beats**
    the pool; a **tie resolves to Path A** — the dependency-free always-on rail. An MM
    cannot divert flow merely by matching the pool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute only the chosen rail">
    **Only the executed path mutates state**, then
    emit `SwapRouted(swapId, pathB, premiumA, premiumB)` so the cheapest-wins routing
    is auditable on-chain.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### The non-reverting mirror — why a bad MM quote never hurts the LP

The router does not call the reverting Path-B gate. It calls
`_quoteUsableAndPremiumB`, a **`view` mirror** of every
Path-B check that returns `(false, 0)` instead of reverting. It reads only —
`isNonceUsed`, `consumedNotional`, `availableBalance`, and the signature via
STATICCALL — and **never mutates a nonce, capacity, or lock**, so an unchosen quote
leaves **zero trace** (I7 preserved).

<Warning>
**A bad MM quote falls back to the pool — it never reverts the LP's transaction.**

- **Limit:** an MM quote can be absent, expired, stale, out-of-band, over-load, over-
  capacity, undercollateralized, zero-priced, wrong-model, or simply not cheaper.
- **Why it cannot harm the LP:** the router evaluates the MM quote through a read-only
  mirror, so any of those conditions just makes `usableB = false`.
- **Mitigation (by construction):** the swap silently routes to the always-on pool.
  Only **protocol-level** failures (market inactive, not the position owner, position
  out of range, dust, slippage, or the pool being unwired) revert. An LP using
  `createSwapRouted` is never worse off than going straight to Path A.
</Warning>

This is what makes the floor/ceiling framing concrete: the MM rail is a pure
**price-improvement layer**. Nothing about correctness, solvency, or liveness depends
on it; it can only ever undercut the pool, never degrade the guarantee the LP already
has from the pool.

## Side-by-side

| | **Path A — cvAMM** | **Path B — MM** |
|---|---|---|
| Entry point | `createSwapPathA` | `createSwap` |
| Counterparty | `ConvexityVault` (pool) | a single MM |
| Signature | none (signature-free at sale) | EIP-712 / EIP-1271 firm quote |
| Quote granularity | n/a (on-chain pricing) | **per-market, never per-NFT** (no `tokenId`) |
| Collateral home | `ConvexityVault` (pooled, dual-tranche) | `UnderwriterVault` (per-MM) |
| Pricing | on-chain load stack, I10 clamp | `loadBps` over FairPremium, `≤ maxLoadBps` |
| Premium split | 99% into pool (tranche split), 1% treasury | 99% MM, 1% treasury |
| Validity clock / nonces / band | none | `validUntil` + bitmap nonce + oracle band (I9) |
| Relayer needed | no (pure on-chain) | yes (off-chain quote book) |
| Role | **floor of liquidity** (always quotes) | **ceiling of price** (wins only by beating pool) |

## Proven live on both rails

The 2026-06-05 redeploy ran a real create→settle on **both** rails against one 300s
market:

- **Path A** (swap #1): V0 \$270,531.28, MaxIL \$1,669.24, premium **\$9.70 (0.58% of
  MaxIL)**, realized IL \$148.64, payout \$148.64 — settled from the `ConvexityVault`.
- **Routed → Path B** (swap #2): `createSwapRouted` saw `premiumA_cvamm $13.80` vs
  `premiumB_mm $8.93` (MM `loadBps` 1000) and **chose the MM** because it strictly
  beat the pool. MaxIL \$3,215.65 was locked from the **MM's own collateral**, realized
  IL \$245.66, payout \$245.66 — settled from `UnderwriterVault`.

This concretely demonstrates the routing: the cheaper rail wins, the chosen rail's
own collateral pays, and the settlement core is the same in both cases.
