The cvAMM ([Path A](/concepts/two-rails)) is more than an always-on underwriter — it
is a **depositor product**. When you deposit USDC into the `ConvexityVault` you become
the counterparty to LPs hedging their impermanent loss: you **sell convexity** and earn
the volatility-risk premium for taking the in-range IL risk LPs want to shed.

But selling volatility is not a savings account. A volatility seller earns a steady
premium in calm markets and pays out in crashes. So a single undifferentiated pool
forces every depositor onto the same risk/return point — and that point is too risky
for a conservative depositor and too tame for a vol specialist. Inflexion's answer is a
**dual-tranche vault from launch**: one pool, two claims on it.

## One vault, two tranches

There is **one** `ConvexityVault` per pair, with fungible USDC backing all nine
[marketIds](/protocol/markets) for that pair (3 fee tiers × 3 durations). It is
**dual-tranche SENIOR / JUNIOR from launch** — not a roadmap upgrade, live at the
[deployed address](/reference/deployments).

The vault keeps the two tranches accounted separately end to end:

| Per-tranche state | Shared pool state |
|---|---|
| `seniorAssets` / `juniorAssets` | `totalLocked` (collateral locked across all open swaps) |
| `seniorShares` / `juniorShares` | `lockedByMarket[marketId]` (per-market lock) |
| `seniorBalanceOf` / `juniorBalanceOf` | `sumLockedSq` (O(1) Herfindahl accumulator for the [dispersion skew](/math/load-stack)) |

Two derived quantities drive everything downstream:

$$
\text{totalAssets} = \text{seniorAssets} + \text{juniorAssets},
\qquad
\text{freeAssets} = \text{totalAssets} - \text{totalLocked}
$$

`freeAssets` is what is available to underwrite the next swap; `totalLocked` is the sum
of collateral (each equal to a swap's [MaxIL](/concepts/maxil)) locked against open
positions.

## The premium waterfall — money in

Every premium paid on Path A is split before it ever touches a depositor. First the
protocol takes its cut, then the remainder is divided across the two tranches.

<Steps>
  <Step title="99% to the pool, 1% to the treasury">
    On `_executePathA` the premium is split **99% accrued into the vault**
    (`accruePremium`) and **1% to the treasury**. There is
    no per-tranche split at this layer — that happens inside the vault.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The senior/junior split (accruePremium)">
    Inside `accruePremium` the pool's share is divided by
    a single immutable parameter, `seniorPremiumShareBps`:

    $$
    \text{toSenior} = \text{amount}\cdot\frac{\text{seniorPremiumShareBps}}{10^4},
    \qquad
    \text{toJunior} = \text{amount} - \text{toSenior}
    $$

    `seniorPremiumShareBps` is **immutable** and read from the
    [calibrated risk-parameter set](/reference/constants), never hardcoded.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Because senior is **protected** from underwriting loss (next section), it accepts a
**smaller slice of premium** — that is the price of safety. Junior takes the **larger
slice** as compensation for standing in front of every loss. This is the structural
reason the two tranches have genuinely different risk/return, not a cosmetic label:
senior is a low-yield "convexity savings account," junior is the high-APY vol-seller.

<Note>
The premium split is purely a **distribution** of income. It does **not** touch the
no-bad-debt math, the [I10 price cap](/security/invariants), or `settle` semantics —
those are orthogonal. The tranche structure changes who earns and
who loses inside the pool; it never changes what the **LP** is paid.
</Note>

## The loss waterfall — money out

When a swap settles with a positive payout, the LP is paid from locked collateral and
the loss is applied to depositors **junior-first**. The waterfall lives in
`releaseAndDistribute`:

$$
\text{juniorLoss} = \min(\text{payout},\ \text{juniorAssets}),
\qquad
\text{seniorLoss} = \text{payout} - \text{juniorLoss}
$$

Junior assets absorb the loss up to their full balance; only what spills over — if
anything — reaches senior. The waterfall first requires `payout ≤ lockedAmount`
(`PayoutExceedsCollateral`) and `lockedAmount ≤ lockedByMarket[marketId]`, then unlocks
the collateral, applies the junior-first split, transfers `payout` to the LP, and emits
`JuniorLoss`.

<Tip>
The `seniorLoss` arm is **defense-in-depth**. By the senior-protection invariant below,
`seniorLoss` is provably always **0** — the `min` can never bind against senior. The arm
exists so that even a future change that weakened the invariant would still fail
*closed* (junior-first) rather than silently socializing losses onto senior.
</Tip>

## The senior-protection invariant — why senior is structurally shielded

The reason `seniorLoss` is always 0 is a single structural invariant, enforced at
**every** `lockCollateral` via the `SeniorProtectionBreached` revert:

$$
\boxed{\ \text{totalLocked} \le \text{juniorAssets}\ }
$$

The proof that this protects senior is two lines. Every payout is capped at its swap's
MaxIL, and that swap's MaxIL is exactly the collateral locked for it. So:

$$
\sum \text{payouts} \ \le\ \text{totalLocked} \ \le\ \text{juniorAssets}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{payout} \le \text{locked} \le \text{junior}
$$

Total underwriting loss can never exceed total locked collateral, and total locked
collateral can never exceed the junior buffer — so **junior absorbs all underwriting
loss before senior is ever touched** (see [the no-bad-debt math](/math/no-bad-debt-math)).

This is the **adaptive code form** of the roadmap's `u ≤ 1−sf` rule, keyed to the
*actual* junior buffer rather than a fixed ratio — which is strictly safer, because the
guard tracks reality (if junior shrinks, lock capacity shrinks with it). With the
calibrated target ratio `sf = 0.60`, the junior fraction is `1 − sf = 0.40`, so
`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` is exactly the utilization bound `u ≤ 0.40 = 1 − sf`
under which the depositor calibration holds.

<Warning>
**Senior protection is *calibrated*, not unconditional — and never covers the systemic tail.**

- **Limit:** "senior `P(loss) = 0`" is a calibration result, not a structural
  guarantee. It holds only while utilization stays `u ≤ 1 − sf` (the regime the
  invariant enforces) and only against **underwriting** loss. It does **not** shield
  senior from systemic failure — a USDC depeg, an oracle/settlement fault, or a contract
  bug.
- **Why:** `sf = 0.60` is a *target* tranche ratio for UX and incentives, not the hard
  cap. The hard cap is the adaptive `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` invariant. Senior
  protection from underwriting loss follows from that invariant; tail risk from a broken
  *assumption* (peg, oracle, code) is outside any in-protocol waterfall.
- **Mitigation:** the invariant is checked on **every** lock and is the same accounting
  that powers the [run defense](#the-run-defense) and prices the
  [utilization skew](/math/load-stack) up before over-commitment. Capital-not-guaranteed
  remains the umbrella for **both** tranches — see [ClaimB](#two-claims-never-merged)
  above and the [risk disclosure](/risk-disclosure).
</Warning>

## The run defense

Both tranches share a deposit/withdraw model designed so the pool **cannot be run**
even though depositor capital is at risk:

- **Deposits are instant.** Capital can always join.
- **Withdrawals are cooldown-gated.** `requestWithdrawal` → `withdraw` only after
  `withdrawalCooldown` elapses.
- **Junior cannot be drawn below `totalLocked`.** The `JuniorBelowLocked` revert
  forbids withdrawing junior capital that is currently buffering live coverage.

The same locked/free accounting that prevents a run also prices the
[`util_skew`](/math/load-stack) **up** as the pool nears full commitment — so marginal
coverage gets more expensive *before* the pool over-commits, and the buffer that
protects senior is never quietly withdrawn out from under open swaps.

## The quant result — what each tranche actually earns and risks

The headline output of the [quant](/math/overview) is the depositor risk/return
profile. The figures below come from the **P1.13 heavy run** — a deliberately
adversarial model that is *stronger than GBM*: Student-t fat tails (`t_df = 4`), a
3-state Markov vol regime (calm 0.45 / normal 0.75 / stressed 1.30), tail-dependent
crash correlation → 1 (a single-pair book settles against the same move), Poisson
deep-crash months (~0.8/yr, mean −45%), and a **lagging `σ_ref`** so coverage written
at a stress onset is underpriced exactly as it would be on-chain.

The monthly P&L identity makes the volatility-seller nature explicit:

$$
\text{monthly\_return} = \text{utilization}\cdot\frac{\sum_i\bigl(\text{premium}_i - \text{payout}_i\bigr)}{\sum_i \text{MaxIL}_i}
$$

$$
\text{premium}_i = \text{fairRate}(\sigma_{\text{ref}})\cdot(1+\text{baseLoad})\cdot\text{MaxIL}_i
\quad\text{(income, risk-neutral at }\sigma_{\text{ref}}\text{)}
$$

$$
\text{payout}_i = \min(\text{IL}_i,\ \text{MaxIL}_i)
\quad\text{(claim, real measure)}
$$

Premium is collected under the **risk-neutral** measure at the conservative
[`σ_ref`](/math/sigma-ref); payout is paid under the **real** measure. The edge is the
gap between them — the volatility risk premium — earned in calm months and given back in
crashes.

### Bare-pool figures (unhedged, untranched)

These are the **single-asset bare pool** numbers — before tranching, before the optional
roadmap hedge — at base loads (calm/normal/stressed = 20/30/50%), gross of
demand/competition/fees. Lead with the **geometric 3y CAGR**, never the
arithmetic mean.

| Operating point | 3y CAGR (med / p10 / p90) | P(3y NAV&lt;1) | P(losing month) | 1-in-100 month | worst month | P(3y DD&gt;50%) | no bad debt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Disciplined `u = 0.40`** | **122% / 50% / 247%** | 0.3% | **26.5%** | **−20.1%** | **−26.8%** | **2.7%** | ✓ |
| Fully-deployed `u = 0.60` | 209% / 72% / 491% | 0.5% | 26.5% | −30.1% | −40.1% | 18.0% | ✓ |

How to read these:

- **3y CAGR** — the volatility risk premium compounds strongly in the median, with a
  wide distribution. The p10 column (50% at `u = 0.40`) is the honest downside of the
  *median run*, not the worst case.
- **P(losing month) ≈ 26.5%** — roughly one month in four is red. This is **intrinsic to
  volatility selling** (you lose whenever realized vol exceeds the implied vol you priced
  at), not a flaw to be tuned away.
- **1-in-100 month / worst month** — the tail of a single bad month. At `u = 0.40` a
  1-in-100 month is −20.1% and the worst observed is −26.8%; both worsen materially at
  `u = 0.60`.
- **P(3y DD &gt; 50%)** — the probability of a peak-to-trough drawdown over 50% across three
  years. **Utilization is the dominant drawdown lever:** `u = 0.40 → 2.7%` vs
  `u = 0.60 → 18.0%`.
- **no bad debt ✓** — across **every** simulated path, `min(IL, MaxIL)/MaxIL ≤ 1.0`
  (`max_payout_frac = 1.000000`). The LP guarantee holds structurally and
  oracle-independently even while the depositor P&L swings.

<Warning>
**The honest headline: an unhedged, untranched single-pair ETH pool CANNOT meet the spec safety targets at any *marketable* load.**

- **Limit:** the binding targets — 1-in-100 monthly loss `≤ 10%` and P(losing month)
  `≤ 15%` — are unreachable for a bare single-asset pool at any load with
  `premium ≤ MaxIL`.
- **Why:** these are **intrinsic to single-asset volatility selling** — you lose whenever
  realized > implied, and a synchronized crash pays the whole correlated book at once. No
  load setting changes that; raising load past `premium ≤ MaxIL` would mean charging more
  than the maximum possible payout, which the [I10 cap](/security/invariants) forbids.
- **Mitigation:** the targets were **not loosened**. The *structural levers* are the
  honest answer — and tranching is the launch one (next section). The targets were met by
  changing the structure, not the bar.
</Warning>

### What tranching does to the picture

The dual-tranche structure is the **launch** lever that turns the bare-pool profile into
two genuinely different products:

| Tranche (`sf = 0.60`, `u = 0.40`, base loads) | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Senior** | **`P(loss) = 0`, worst month `0%`**, gross CAGR ~197% — the "convexity savings account." Holds **jointly with `u ≤ 1 − sf`**, against underwriting loss only. |
| **Junior** | **worst `−67%`** — the high-APY first-loss vol tranche that earns the larger premium share for buffering senior. |

The senior tranche converts the bare pool's −26.8% worst month and 26.5% loss-month rate
into a **smooth, protected** return *while the junior buffer holds and utilization stays
disciplined*. The junior tranche concentrates the bare pool's downside (worst −67%) in
exchange for the larger premium slice. **Neither is risk-free** — junior can lose most of
its principal in a crash, and senior takes the systemic tail once the junior buffer is
exhausted or an assumption (peg / oracle / code) breaks.

<Note>
There is a third, **roadmap** lever — a pool-side options hedge (`h ≈ 0.60`) that tightens
the *tail* (1-in-100 → ~−9.6%, P(DD&gt;50%) → ~0%) without much CAGR change. It is
**tail-tightening, not solvency** (it carries basis risk from approximating a fixed v3
position with a perpetual options hedge), and it is **not** required for the no-bad-debt
guarantee. Tranching is the launch structure; the hedge is a future enhancement.
</Note>

### The mandatory disclosure tone

The quant fixes the exact language depositor surfaces must use:

> "You earn the volatility risk premium in calm markets and absorb losses in crashes. In
> FULL the pool cannot become insolvent and cannot be run, but YOUR CAPITAL IS NOT
> GUARANTEED."

Never call it "stable" or "modest APY." APY figures are gross of demand, competition, and
fees and are model-illustrative — present the **risk panel** as the robust part and lead
with the geometric 3y CAGR, never the arithmetic mean. The model is in fact
**net-conservative** to depositors: the `σ_ref` floor of 0.50 sits *above* calm ETH vol
(~0.45), so the pool overcharges the ~27% of months that are calm, and the heavy run's
all-months realized payout/premium came in at **0.654**.

## Two claims, never merged

The tranche structure is where the two load-bearing disclosures sit side by side. They
are **separate claims** and must never be combined.

The LP-facing guarantee is structural and oracle-independent — it holds regardless of how
the depositor P&L moves:

<ClaimA />

The depositor-facing reality is the opposite: capital is at risk for **both** tranches,
junior first.

<ClaimB />

The crucial split: the LP's no-bad-debt guarantee ([Claim A](/concepts/no-bad-debt)) is
*structural* — `collateral = MaxIL ≥ payout` by construction — and is **orthogonal to
every pricing oracle and to the tranche split**. The depositor's capital-at-risk
([Claim B](/concepts/tranches)) is the economic substance of being a volatility seller.
Senior protection sits *inside* Claim B (it reduces, but does not eliminate, junior-then-
systemic risk); it is not a separate guarantee and never reaches across to the LP side.
