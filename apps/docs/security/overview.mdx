Inflexion's security story is small at its core and large at its edges. The core is **one structural fact**; everything else — ten fuzzed invariants, two resolved audit forks, a bounded off-chain matcher, junior-first-loss tranching — is the scaffolding that protects the *rest* of the system around that fact.

This page states the headline claim correctly (with its qualifiers, every time), separates the two claims that must never be merged, and gives you a reading order for the rest of the security documentation.

## The one structural fact

In **FULL mode**, for every swap, collateral is locked at exactly `MaxIL` at creation, and coverage at settlement is `min(realized_IL, MaxIL)`. Therefore:

$$
\text{payout} \;=\; \min(\text{realized\_IL},\; \text{MaxIL}) \;\le\; \text{MaxIL} \;=\; \text{collateral}
$$

This holds **for any price path** — in-range, below `Pa`, above `Pb`, and the no-IL region — because it is an algebraic property of a `min`, not a heuristic or a calibration. It is enforced by a single line in `settle`:

```solidity
uint128 payout = realisedIL > s.maxIL ? s.maxIL : uint128(realisedIL);
```

with the collateral having been locked at `MaxIL` at creation on both Path A and Path B, and both vaults defensively re-checking `payout ≤ lockedAmount`. This is invariant **I1**, verified by Foundry fuzz over the whole price domain.

<Info>
**The most important consequence: the no-bad-debt proof is oracle-independent.**

`settle` caps the payout at `MaxIL == collateral` *regardless of any pricing oracle*. The [FairValueOracle](/concepts/fair-value) and `VolOracle` are load-bearing for the **I10 price cap** and for **depositor solvency** — they are **not** part of the I1 proof. You can imagine both pricing oracles returning garbage and I1 still holds: the LP can never be paid more than the collateral that was locked for them.
</Info>

## The headline claim, stated correctly

The no-bad-debt guarantee is exact **only** under its full clause. State it with the qualifier every time — a stripped version is not just imprecise, it is wrong.

<ClaimA />

Why each clause is load-bearing — caveats framed with their *why*, not exposed naked:

| Clause | Why it is load-bearing | What carries it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **(a) Capped payoff** | Remove the cap and IL above `Pb` is *unbounded* (the LP is fully in token1 at constant value while hold value grows linearly with price); no fixed collateral can back it. The cap is what makes `payout ≤ collateral` hold. | `min(IL, MaxIL)` enforced in `settle` |
| **(b) Solvent USDC** | Collateral, premium, and payout are all denominated in `dUSDC` (6 decimals, the numeraire). A depeg is a *disclosed shared risk*, not a protocol bug. | The `dUSDC` numeraire assumption |
| **(c) Oracle / settlement liveness** | `settle` pins the Chainlink round-at-T. If the oracle never produces a usable round, funds can't settle. **Mitigation:** [Fork-1](/security/forks) bounds this with a 24h liveness backstop so funds are never *permanently* locked (I8). | `OracleManager.getSettlementPrice` |
| **(d) No rehypothecation breach** | Routing locked collateral to a utilization-gated venue (Aave/Compound) could make a withdrawal revert in a crash, breaking the guarantee. **Mitigation:** locked collateral stays instantly liquid — idle capital only, only to instantly-redeemable wrappers. | `IYieldAdapter.isInstantlyRedeemable()`; the locked-collateral liquidity rule |

## Two claims that must never be merged

These are **separate** guarantees with **separate** truth conditions. Conflating them is the single most common framing error when describing this protocol.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
<Card title="Claim A — LPs are always paid" icon="shield-check">
No bad debt in FULL mode. **Structural and oracle-independent** (collateral = MaxIL ≥ payout), qualified by the clause above. This is invariant I1.
</Card>
<Card title="Claim B — depositors / MMs can lose capital" icon="triangle-exclamation">
The pool is a volatility *seller*. **Capital is not guaranteed for either tranche.** Junior is first-loss; senior is protected from *underwriting* loss only — never from the systemic tail.
</Card>
</CardGroup>

<ClaimB />

The crucial split: Claim A's proof is structural and orthogonal to every pricing oracle. The pricing oracles matter for the **I10 price cap** and **depositor solvency** — Claim B's territory — but not for Claim A. Keep them apart.

## One-paragraph summary

Inflexion's security rests on one structural fact: in FULL mode collateral is locked at `MaxIL` and coverage is `min(realized_IL, MaxIL)`, so `payout ≤ collateral` by construction (I1/I2) — a proof that is *oracle-independent* and holds under any price path, qualified only by capped payoff + solvent USDC + oracle/settlement liveness + no rehypothecation breach. Around that core, ten Foundry-fuzzed invariants (I1–I10) and a multi-LLM spec audit (12 fixes plus two resolved forks — the Chainlink round-walking liveness backstop for I8, and the deterministic oracle-anchored price band, *not* last-look, for I9) close every audited vector. The off-chain matcher (Path B only) is strictly bounded: it cannot steal, forge, or force-stale, and [Path A](/protocol/path-a-cvamm) is fully on-chain and immune. The cvAMM adds a code-capped fair price ([I10](/concepts/no-bad-debt), by construction) plus structural junior-first-loss senior protection (`totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets`) — while honestly disclosing that depositor capital is *not* guaranteed for either [tranche](/concepts/tranches).
