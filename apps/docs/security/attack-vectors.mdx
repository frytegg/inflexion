This is the adversarial index of Inflexion's security: a one-row-per-vector ledger of every attack the protocol has been threat-modeled against, each mapped to its **mitigation** and to the **exact place it is enforced** — a numbered invariant, never a hand-wave. It is the companion to [the invariants](/security/invariants) (what is guaranteed and why), [the forks](/security/forks) (the two open audit items, now closed), and [the trust model](/security/trust-model) (what the off-chain matcher can and cannot do).

Where a vector touches a deeper page, the row links to it. The full enforcement set is on the live deployment — see [the deployment registry](/reference/deployments) for addresses.

<Info>
**How to read this ledger.** Every row follows the same shape: **vector → mitigation → where enforced.** "Where enforced" is a Foundry-fuzzed invariant (`I1`–`I10`). Nothing here is "trust us" — each mitigation is code, and most are also driven by a fuzz test on [the invariants page](/security/invariants).
</Info>

## The master ledger

The fifteen vectors below are grouped by what they attack: LP payout integrity, oracle/settlement, quote & capacity integrity, economic griefing, depositor/custody safety, and reentrancy. Audit-finding tags (`F-#N`) reference the external audit summarized on [the audits page](/security/audits).

## The vectors, in a little more depth

The table is the canonical artifact; the accordions below add the *why* for the rows that most reward it.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="MM bad debt, settler price choice, and entry-price gaming — the LP-payout cluster">
These three rows share one root defense: **the LP can supply neither the geometry, the entry price, nor the settlement price.**

- **MM bad debt** is closed structurally — collateral is `MaxIL` and the payout is `min(realized_IL, MaxIL)`, so `payout ≤ collateral` for *any* price path (the [no-bad-debt math](/math/no-bad-debt-math)). The cap is the load-bearing piece; remove it and beyond-`Pb` IL is unbounded.
- **Settler price choice** was a real hole: a permissionless `settle` once computed payout from a caller-supplied `sqrtP_T`, so a settler could set payout anywhere in `[0, MaxIL]`. The fix derives `P_T` on-chain from the Chainlink round-at-T, restoring **I2** *and* **I4** (an LP who would have profited vs hold gets exactly zero).
- **Entry-price gaming** is bounded the same way: `P0` is the on-chain round at the LP's chosen block, clamped to within the MM's `priceBandBps`. The residual entry-timing freedom is bounded and blunted because premium scales with MaxIL.

See [price pinning](/security/price-pinning) for the full closed-hole writeup.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Lone-spike, permanent lockup, and sequencer downtime — the oracle/liveness cluster">
The settlement oracle is the most subtle surface, because it must be **both** correct (no glitched price pins a wrong payout) **and** live (funds never lock permanently). [Fork-1](/security/forks) resolves the tension:

- A glitched **lone spike** — a round differing from *both* neighbours by ≥ 5% — is deferred, but only until the **24h `LIVENESS_WINDOW`** elapses, after which the backstop accepts the round unconditionally.
- A **missing neighbour round** across a Chainlink `phaseId` boundary is read tolerantly via `_tryRound` (try/catch returning `ok=false`), so it degrades to the backstop rather than reverting forever.
- **Sequencer downtime** is gated separately by `_requireSequencerHealthy` (uptime + 1h grace, fails-closed on a future `updatedAt`).

<Warning>
**Documented design observation (not a bug):** the spec-literal staleness `require` fires before the backstop can recover the round, so the *actual* usable round-at-T window is `[expiry + LIVENESS_WINDOW, expiry + MAX_STALENESS)` ≈ 1 hour wide (90,000 − 86,400 = 3,600s on Sepolia), not the full additive sum the I8 wording suggests.
**Why it's acceptable:** the 1h window is *symmetric* for LP and MM (preserves fairness), and past `expiry + MAX_STALENESS` funds still settle via a *newer* Chainlink round — Arbitrum majors tick frequently — just not via the round-at-T.
**Mitigation / transparency:** surfaced in the `OracleManager` NatSpec and covered by a boundary test, not papered over. See [the invariants page](/security/invariants) for the full I8 note.
</Warning>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Stale-quote pickoff, forged quote, replay, and capacity — the quote-integrity cluster">
Path-B quotes are **bearer instruments** — signed bytes that survive in any hand that copied them, beyond the off-chain engine's cancel. Four mechanisms, all on-chain, bound what a leaked or forged quote can do:

- **Forgery** is impossible — every quote is EIP-712 signed and verified via `SignatureChecker.isValidSignatureNow` (ECDSA + EIP-1271).
- **Stale-quote pickoff** (the dominant pickoff, [Fork-2](/security/forks)) is killed by the **oracle-anchored band**: `createSwap` auto-voids the quote if the live oracle drifted beyond `priceBandBps`. Deterministic, no MM discretion ⇒ **not last-look** (a [hard protocol rule](/security/trust-model)).
- **Replay after cancel** is blocked by **Permit2-style bitmap nonces** — an MM flips one bit to void one quote; a cancelled bit can never fill.
- **Capacity over-consumption** from concurrent fills is blocked by **atomic check-and-increment** of `consumedNotional[quoteId]` before any external call.

Together these are **I7 + I9**. The router subtlety: in `createSwapRouted`, only the *executed* rail mutates nonce/capacity/lock — a quote that loses the route consumes nothing.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Dust grief, under-charge, and overcharge — the economic cluster">
Three economic-edge attacks, three deterministic clamps:

- **Dust griefing** — sub-economic spam — is floored at `MIN_POSITION_V0 = $100` and `MIN_PREMIUM = $1`.
- **Under-charge from integer division** rounds the *wrong* way only if you let it; Inflexion uses `ceilDiv` so the premium always rounds **up**.
- **Overcharge** is impossible by construction — the load is clamped at `maxLoad` (Path A) or `loadBps ≤ maxLoadBps` (Path B), and premium is additionally capped at MaxIL. This is **I10**, lives upstream of settle, and never touches the no-bad-debt proof. See [the load stack](/math/load-stack) and [I10 on the no-bad-debt concept page](/concepts/no-bad-debt).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pool run, senior loss, NFT hijack, rehypothecation, and reentrant settle — the systemic/custody cluster">
- **Pool run:** withdrawals are cooldown-gated and **junior cannot be drawn below `totalLocked`**. The same locked/free accounting raises `util_skew` before the pool over-commits.
- **Senior loss from underwriting:** `totalLocked ≤ juniorAssets` is enforced at every lock; the junior-first waterfall absorbs all underwriting loss before senior is touched. This is *senior protection*, not a capital guarantee — see [tranches](/concepts/tranches).
- **NFT hijack:** `ILVault.onERC721Received` accepts NFTs only from the canonical NonfungiblePositionManager; `claimFees` is forced to the registered LP.
- **Rehypothecation breach:** locked collateral never goes to a utilization-gated venue — a yield adapter must be `isInstantlyRedeemable()` (idle-only, sDAI/T-bills). See [collateral models](/protocol/collateral-models).
- **Reentrant settle:** status flips to `SETTLED` *before* any external call, so a malicious `oracle`/`ilMath` re-entering `settle` reverts on the `Status.ACTIVE` precondition. Full CEI on [the reentrancy page](/security/cei-reentrancy).
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## What the matcher cannot do — the structural backstop

Several rows above (forged quote, stale-quote pickoff, capacity) are *also* covered by a more general fact: the off-chain matching engine exists for **Path B only**, and its power is strictly bounded. It can censor, reorder, or drop Path-B quotes; it **cannot** steal funds, forge quotes, force a stale quote past the band, or touch Path A at all (which needs no engine). Settlement is non-custodial and on-chain, against the counterparty's own collateral.

This is the structural reason the table holds even against a *malicious operator*: the mitigations are enforced by the contracts, not by the relayer. Read the full [trust model](/security/trust-model) for the can/cannot grid and the direct-to-contract fallback.

## Two things the ledger does *not* claim

Two caveats, each with its *why* and its mitigation:

<Warning>
**Vol-only moves are not caught by a *spot* band.** A residual of [Fork-2](/security/forks): if implied vol jumps with little spot drift, the I9 spot band does not void the quote.
**Why it's a small surface:** on crypto majors, large IV jumps almost always co-move with spot, which *does* trip the band.
**Mitigation:** the short `validUntil` (default 8s, band [5s, 15s]) bounds the leakage window, and the MM bears within-band drift because it chose the band.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Depositor and MM capital is not guaranteed.** None of the mitigations above protect underwriter principal — they protect the *LP's payout* (Claim A) and the *protocol's integrity*. The pool is a volatility seller; it can lose.
**Why:** underwriting is a paid risk transfer, not a riskless service. Junior is first-loss; senior is protected from *underwriting* loss while junior buffers, but takes the systemic tail (USDC depeg, oracle failure, contract bug).
**Mitigation:** transparent tranching, the senior-protection invariant, and full disclosure — see [tranches](/concepts/tranches) and Claim B below.
</Warning>
